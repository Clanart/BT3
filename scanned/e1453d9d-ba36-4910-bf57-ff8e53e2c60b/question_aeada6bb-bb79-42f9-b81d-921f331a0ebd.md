Attacker action: call `InternalGetEmergencyStopTx(GetEmergencyStopTxRequest{move_to_vault_txid})` with no certificate for a `move_to_vault_txid` the attacker learned by watching the Bitcoin network for a completed move-to-vault broadcast. Preconditions: `client_verification=false`; `insert_signed_emergency_stop_tx_if_not_exists` has already stored the encrypted emergency-stop tx for that move txid (per `verify_and_save_emergency_stop_sigs`). Call sequence: Noop admits call -> handler retrieves and returns the stored emergency-stop artifact keyed only by `move_to_vault_txid`, with no check that the caller is the security council or any authorised emergency-stop invoker. Binding: the party able to retrieve/trigger the emergency-stop tx for a given move-to-vault UTXO == the party the protocol designates (security council/aggregator-internal) to invoke emergency stop, and an unauthenticated caller reaching this method violates that equality. Impact: High - leakage of a protocol commitment/secret (the emergency-stop transaction, encrypted under `emergency_stop_encryption_public_key`, but its *existence, timing and metadata* are still exposed) before its intended reveal, and if the response ever carries anything beyond ciphertext, this becomes Critical exposure of an unrevealed spend path over the move-to-vault UTXO.
