Target: `ClementineAggregator::new_deposit` DoS-adjacent-but-in-scope custody angle via `Aggregator::create_movetx`. Attacker action: unauthenticated caller sends `NewDeposit` for a real, currently-unspent `bridge_amount` deposit output that belongs to a legitimate, unsuspecting depositor (the attacker merely observed the deposit output on-chain and reuses its exact outpoint/scripts in a `DepositParams` message), racing the legitimate depositor's own `NewDeposit` call. Preconditions: `client_verification=false`; legitimate depositor has funded a valid `bridge_amount` deposit output on Bitcoin but has not yet called `new_deposit` on the aggregator. Call sequence: `Noop` admits attacker's request first -> `new_deposit` -> `create_move_to_vault_txhandler` builds a move tx keyed to that outpoint, drives verifiers to sign it, `create_movetx` finalizes it with attacker as the RPC caller who receives the finalized signed tx in the response. Binding: MINT_AUTHORITY equality (move txid presigned == the one unspent `bridge_amount` deposit output with exact required scripts) itself still holds structurally, but CALLER_AUTHORITY breaks: the party who receives the finalized signed move-to-vault transaction (and can be first to broadcast it, or learn its structure) is not the depositor who owns/authorised use of that deposit. Impact: Critical - attacker obtains the fully-signed move-to-vault transaction for someone else's deposit and can broadcast it themselves, potentially racing the legitimate mint flow or interfering with vault crediting semantics tied to who initiated the deposit. Proof idea: `cargo test` funding a deposit output via regtest, having an unauthenticated third party (not the depositor) call `new_deposit`/`send_move_to_vault_tx` for that exact outpoint before the depositor does, and asserting the RPC succeeds and yields a valid signed tx to the non-owning caller.
