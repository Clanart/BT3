Target: `Aggregator::optimistic_payout` versus `Verifier::sign_optimistic_payout`. Attacker action: send a `WithdrawParams` to `Aggregator::optimistic_payout` where `input_signature.sighash_type` is `SinglePlusAnyoneCanPay` and passes `SECP.verify_schnorr` for the *current* `withdrawal_prevout` fetched by the aggregator, but the aggregator's fetched `withdrawal_prevout` (via `self.rpc.get_txout_from_outpoint`) differs from what each verifier independently fetches later inside `sign_optimistic_payout` (`self.rpc.get_txout_from_outpoint(&input_outpoint)`), if the referenced UTXO is replaced between the two RPC calls (e.g. via a same-block RBF/conflicting spend visible to one node but not synced to another). Preconditions: aggregator and verifiers connected to distinct or differently-synced Bitcoin nodes. Call sequence: aggregator verifies against its own `withdrawal_prevout`; verifiers each independently re-derive `withdrawal_prevout` and re-build `create_optimistic_payout_txhandler`, but never re-run `SECP.verify_schnorr` themselves. Binding: the `TxOut` the aggregator verified `input_signature` against == the `TxOut` each verifier embeds in the txhandler it partially signs. Impact: Critical — N-of-N partial signatures could commit to a `SpendableTxIn::new_partial(input_utxo.outpoint, input_utxo.txout)` whose `txout` was never actually checked against the signature by any party, if aggregator and verifier disagree on the prevout.
