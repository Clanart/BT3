Target: `ClementineAggregator::withdraw` (calls all operators) reached via `Interceptors::Noop`. Attacker action: attacker sends `Withdraw(AggregatorWithdrawalInput{...})` with a `withdrawal_id` and an `input_outpoint`/`input_signature`/`output_script_pubkey` for a Bitcoin UTXO the attacker owns, without ever calling `withdraw` on the Citrea Bridge contract for that id. Preconditions: `client_verification=false`; no corresponding Citrea withdrawal record. Call sequence: request bypasses `only_aggregator_and_self`, reaches `AggregatorServer::withdraw`, which calls `Operator::withdraw` on all configured operators with attacker-supplied payout fields. Binding: `AggregatorWithdrawalInput.withdrawal_id` == a withdrawal actually registered by the Citrea Bridge contract, which is false. Impact: Critical - N-of-N/operator partial signatures produced for a spend no Citrea withdrawal authorises. Proof idea: `cargo test` with `MockCitreaClient` configured to have no withdrawal record for the chosen id, call `withdraw` from an unauthenticated client, and assert operators still attempt to build/sign a payout instead of failing at the caller-authority layer.
