`Aggregator::optimistic_payout` (core/src/rpc/aggregator.rs) and `Verifier::sign_optimistic_payout` (core/src/verifier.rs) both take a `deposit_id` and internally call `get_move_to_vault_txid_from_citrea_deposit(deposit_id)` to find the vault UTXO and `get_withdrawal_utxo_from_citrea_withdrawal(deposit_id)` to find the withdrawal UTXO/signature, implicitly assuming Citrea's deposit index and withdrawal index are the same integer for a given `deposit_id`. Can an attacker call Citrea's `withdraw` with a withdrawal index that is numerically valid but corresponds, under the Bridge contract's own indexing, to a different deposit than the one whose move-to-vault UTXO `get_move_to_vault_txid_from_citrea_deposit(deposit_id)` returns, causing `sign_optimistic_payout` to build and countersign a payout spending vault UTXO A while crediting withdrawal-authorization data meant for vault B? Binding: the move-to-vault txid returned by `get_move_to_vault_txid_from_citrea_deposit(deposit_id)` == the move-to-vault txid whose deposit output actually backs the withdrawal UTXO returned by `get_withdrawal_utxo_from_citrea_withdrawal(deposit_id)`. Impact: Critical, N-of-N partial signatures produced for a spend no Citrea withdrawal authorises - BTC leaves a move-to-vault UTXO without a matching fronted withdrawal. Proof idea: cargo test with a mocked `CitreaClientT` returning mismatched deposit/withdrawal records for the same `deposit_id`, asserting `sign_optimistic_payout` refuses to sign when the two lookups disagree.
