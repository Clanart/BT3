target: `verify_storage_proofs`, computed `storage_key_vout = storage_address + U256::from(storage_proof.index * 2 + 1)` (circuits-lib/src/bridge_circuit/storage_proof.rs). Attacker action: submit a `StorageProof` where `index` is chosen (via influencing which withdrawal is processed, since the attacker controls the withdrawal UTXO and can force ordering by paying to have their withdrawal processed at a specific queue position) such that `index * 2 + 1` overflows before the `+1`, producing a `storage_key_vout` that aliases the `storage_key_utxo` slot of a different withdrawal. Preconditions: `storage_proof.index` is `u32` and unchecked upstream. Call sequence: `verify_storage_proofs` derives `vout` from `vout_storage_proof.value`, later used in `bridge_circuit` (mod.rs) to assert `vout == input.payout_spv.transaction.input[payout_input_index].previous_output.vout`; if the vout slot aliases another withdrawal's utxo slot, the circuit could accept a spurious `vout` that happens to match the payout transaction's actual input by coincidence of aliasing rather than genuine correspondence. Binding: `vout` returned by `verify_storage_proofs` for withdrawal `index` == the vout the Bridge contract recorded for that specific withdrawal's UTXO, not for any other index. Impact: Critical - a bridge proof accepted for a claim that is false, causing BTC to leave a move-to-vault UTXO for an outpoint that was never the registered withdrawal target. Proof idea: cargo test in circuits-lib crafting two `StorageProof` fixtures whose `index` values differ by exactly `2^31` and checking that their derived `storage_key_vout`/`storage_key_utxo` never collide, verifying PROOF_SOUNDNESS.
