No vulnerability found for this question.

**Rationale:** `add_root` itself is a minimal function that only marks a slot as an unflushed root in the cache and bumps `max_root` — it contains no account-liveness or zero-lamport logic at all [1](#0-0) , so it cannot itself be the site of a resurrection bug regardless of write-churn timing.

The actual zero-lamport lifecycle (the place where a resurrection bug would have to live) is guarded by several deliberate, cross-checked mechanisms rather than a single unguarded path:

- `filter_zero_lamport_clean_for_incremental_snapshots` explicitly blocks purging a zero-lamport account when its dying slot is newer than the latest full snapshot, precisely to prevent an incremental-snapshot rebuild from resurrecting a stale non-zero version [2](#0-1) .
- Zero-lamport single-ref accounts encountered during shrink are either purged (if snapshot-safe) or retained as on-disk "tombstones" so an incremental snapshot can still propagate the deletion [3](#0-2) .
- `remove_zero_lamport_single_ref_accounts_after_shrink` decrements ref counts / removes index entries only after the storage side has been made consistent [4](#0-3) .

This exact "close/recreate churn near root movement" scenario is covered by dedicated regression tests that assert the invariant holds:
- `test_shrink_does_not_resurrect_dead_account` explicitly stores, roots, cleans, shrinks, and re-cleans a churned account shape and asserts it "should stay dead" [5](#0-4) .
- `test_accounts_clean_after_snapshot_restore_then_old_revives` is written specifically to catch premature purges that would let an older snapshot-restore state "revive" a zero-lamport pubkey [6](#0-5) .
- `test_incremental_snapshots_handle_tombstones` covers the fund/drain/shrink/incremental-snapshot round trip end-to-end and asserts the account "hasn't come back from the dead" [7](#0-6) .
- `test_clean_purges_zero_lamport_single_ref_at_reclaim` and `test_flush_cache_dont_clean_zero_lamport_account` cover heavy write-churn-then-root scenarios and confirm loads observe lamports==0 consistently rather than a stale non-zero balance [8](#0-7) .

Since the cited entrypoint (`add_root`) has no account-state logic, and the actual resurrection invariant is protected by explicit gating logic plus targeted regression tests that already exercise the exact "close and recreate churn near root movement" scenario described, there is no demonstrable break in the invariant reachable by an unprivileged transaction submitter through this path.
