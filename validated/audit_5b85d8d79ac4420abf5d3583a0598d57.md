### Title
Future-dated oracle timestamps bypass staleness gating in `oracle-timestamp-fresh` - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
The report warns that oracle updates may fail to track true market movement quickly enough or may report stale data, and stresses that the staleness/freshness gating logic must be robust before mainnet. Zest's own on-chain freshness check has a logic flaw that lets an oracle report bypass staleness validation entirely whenever the reported publish timestamp is at or after `stacks-block-time` — precisely the "confidence or staleness gating" analog class called out as in-scope.

### Finding Description
`price-resolve` gates every price used for collateral/debt valuation on `oracle-price-legal` and `oracle-timestamp-fresh`: [1](#0-0) 

```clarity
(define-private (oracle-timestamp-fresh (ts uint) (prev uint) (max-staleness uint))
  (let ((delta (if (> ts stacks-block-time)
                   u0
                   (- stacks-block-time ts))))
    (and
      (<= delta max-staleness)
      (>= ts prev))))
```

When the feed's reported `publish-time` (`ts`) is greater than the current `stacks-block-time`, `delta` is hard-coded to `u0`, which trivially satisfies `(<= delta max-staleness)` regardless of how stale the underlying data actually is. This means the per-asset `max-staleness` threshold configured in the asset registry — the exact mechanism the report recommends be "rigorously tested" against attack vectors — provides no protection at all in this branch. [2](#0-1) 

`price-resolve` feeds this result directly into `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` gating, and its output (`final-price`) is the price used for collateral/debt USD valuation throughout borrow, withdraw, and liquidation health checks. `stacks-block-time` tracks the local Stacks chain's block timestamp, which can legitimately lag behind real-world (wall-clock) time during periods of slow block production or chain congestion — the same class of "3h update period too slow during a flash event" concern the report raises about NFT floor oracles, except here it is the consumer's own gating logic, not the oracle, that fails to reject the mismatch. Any Pyth or DIA feed whose `publish-time`/converted timestamp exceeds the current (lagging) `stacks-block-time` is treated as unconditionally fresh, even if the true wall-clock staleness is minutes or hours.

### Impact Explanation
Once a stale price is accepted as "fresh," it can be used to:
- Allow borrowing/withdrawals against overvalued collateral before a price crash is reflected, leaving the protocol with under-collateralized debt (protocol insolvency risk).
- Block or delay correct liquidations because the stale price still shows the position as healthy, permanently or temporarily freezing at-risk collateral from being liquidated.

This lands in the Critical/High impact classes: protocol insolvency from mispriced collateral, or temporary freezing of funds due to liquidations being blocked by a falsely "fresh" stale price.

### Likelihood Explanation
This does not require a malicious oracle or a bad DAO registry entry — it is triggered purely by ordinary clock skew between the Stacks chain's block timestamp and a feed's UTC-based publish time, which becomes more likely during any period of block-production slowdown (a routine, not adversarial, chain condition). No special privileges or third-party oracle malfunction are required; the bug is fully contained in Zest's own `oracle-timestamp-fresh` comparison logic.

### Recommendation
Do not clamp `delta` to `0` when `ts > stacks-block-time`. Instead, either reject timestamps that are unreasonably far in the future (bound the allowed forward skew, e.g. a few blocks/seconds), or compute staleness using `abs(stacks-block-time - ts)` so that future-dated timestamps cannot silently bypass the `max-staleness` threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. A collateral asset's Pyth/DIA feed publishes a price at `publish-time = T_old` (genuinely stale, e.g., 2 hours old) but the local `stacks-block-time` has lagged and only reads `T_old - 1` due to slow block production.
2. `oracle-timestamp-fresh` computes `ts (T_old) > stacks-block-time (T_old - 1)` → `delta = u0`.
3. `(<= u0 max-staleness)` is always true, so the stale price passes `price-resolve`'s freshness assertion and is used as `final-price` for the asset's collateral/debt valuation in borrow/withdraw/liquidation flows in `v0-4-market.clar`, even though the real-world price is out of date and the max-staleness protection meant to prevent exactly this was bypassed.
