The `PhantomOrderPriceSnapshot.medianPrice` is read live from *destination-chain RPC balances at snapshot time* — a direct real-money analog to the `Checkpoints#getAtBlock()` flashloan pattern in the seed report: an unprivileged actor can transiently inflate a measured on-chain value (there: staked-token checkpoint; here: solver liquidity balance) around a single measurement point to bias a downstream financial computation that other unprivileged users then rely on to receive real value.

### Title
Flashloan-inflatable solver liquidity weight lets an attacker bias the Phantom price snapshot that directly prices real IntentGateway swaps - (File: `sdk/packages/sdk/src/protocols/intents/phantom-aggregation.ts`)

### Summary
`aggregatePhantomBids()` computes a `weightedMedian` price for a phantom (synthetic) order by weighting each solver's quote by a **live, spot** ERC-20 (+ ERC-4626 vault) balance read via `getTotalSolverBalance()` [1](#0-0) . This snapshot's `medianPrice`/`standardAmount` is then consumed directly as the exchange rate for real user swaps by `PhantomSnapshotIntentQuoteStrategy.quoteSnapshot()`, which computes `amountOut = netAmountIn * medianPrice / standardAmount` (or the exact-output inverse) with no independent price bound or sanity check against a second oracle [2](#0-1) . Because the weight is measured at whatever moment the snapshot handler executes (triggered by `PhantomBidWindowExhausted`) rather than over any time-window or checkpointed history, a solver can transiently balloon its own on-chain balance (e.g. via a flashloan, or simply routing funds through momentarily before repaying/moving them out) at that exact measurement instant to swing `weightedMedian` toward its own quoted price, exactly analogous to inflating a stake checkpoint around a single snapshot point in the seed report.

### Finding Description
The weighting model is explicitly designed so "a solver that can actually deliver size influence[s] the median more than those quoting on thin liquidity" [3](#0-2) , and the weight is defined as "the solver's total balance for the output token across native + vault venues" measured at the instant of the RPC call, with no requirement that the balance be held before or after that instant [4](#0-3) . The snapshot is a single point-in-time RPC read (`getTotalSolverBalance`) rather than a checkpoint/average over a challenge window, so — just like `Checkpoints#getAtBlock()` returning whatever checkpoint value exists at the moment queried, regardless of how transiently it was created — a solver can create a large but momentary balance solely to be observed by this one measurement.

The measurement point is also predictable and attacker-triggerable: `handlePhantomOrderPrices` fires on `PhantomBidWindowExhausted`, a phantom-order lifecycle event on a fixed interval (`PhantomBidWindow`), and a solver participating in the bidding already knows the commitment and can watch the chain for the closing block [5](#0-4) . A colluding/self-dealing solver can therefore: (1) submit a bid quoting an output amount favorable to itself, (2) immediately before the snapshot RPC executes, transiently inflate its own balance of the output token on the destination chain (flashloan or temporary transfer-in), (3) let the snapshot's `getTotalSolverBalance` observe the inflated balance and dominate `weightedMedian`, then (4) withdraw/repay the funds. The resulting biased `medianPrice` is persisted as `PhantomOrderPriceSnapshot` and is the *sole* pricing input consumed by real users' `quoteIntent()` calls for USDC↔cNGN swaps — `quoteSnapshot()` trusts `snapshot.medianPrice` unconditionally (only checking it's `> 0`) [6](#0-5) .

This differs from ordinary price-manipulation/oracle risk in that the existing code comments already assume the weight measurement is a defense (guarding against "unfillable quotes" and thin-liquidity manipulation) — but the defense itself has no time-window or repeated-observation requirement, making it exactly as bypassable as an un-throttled `getAtBlock()` checkpoint.

### Impact Explanation
`medianPrice`/`standardAmount` from `PhantomOrderPriceSnapshot` is the exchange rate the SDK returns from `quoteIntent()` and instructs users to use verbatim to size real `IntentGatewayV2` orders' `inputs`/`output.assets` — "Use the returned amounts directly as the order's `inputs` and `output.assets` — no further fee or slippage adjustment is required" [7](#0-6) . A biased snapshot causes real users to escrow more input tokens than the fair market rate justifies (or receive less output than fair) when they place and fill orders against that quote — a direct, unauthorized transfer of value from users to the manipulating solver via transaction/price manipulation, which the bounty's impact gate explicitly covers ("transaction manipulation, logic attacks").

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only an unprivileged solver already capable of bidding on phantom orders (no relayer/prover/admin compromise) plus a transient balance inflation technique (flashloan or self-funded temporary transfer) timed to the deterministic `PhantomBidWindowExhausted` trigger — a purely public-entrypoint, attacker-controlled sequence, matching the seed bug's "flashloan to stake and exit in the same block" primitive.

### Recommendation
Do not weight by a single spot balance read. Either (a) require the weight to be sampled multiple times across the bid window / challenge period and take the minimum or a time-weighted average, analogous to requiring `exit` at least one block after `stake` in the original fix; or (b) bound `medianPrice` against an independent reference price (e.g. the Uniswap V4 quoter path already used for other pairs) and reject/clamp snapshots that deviate beyond a configured tolerance; or (c) require solvers to lock/escrow the quoted liquidity (e.g. via the existing bid storage-deposit mechanism) rather than merely holding a balance at read time, so the measured weight cannot be created and destroyed within a single snapshot call.

### Proof of Concept
1. Solver `S` submits a `BidPlaced` bid on phantom order commitment `C` quoting a favorable `output amount` for USDC→cNGN.
2. `S` monitors Hyperbridge for the `PhantomBidWindowExhausted` event for `C` (deterministic per `PhantomBidWindow` interval).
3. In the same or immediately preceding block on the destination chain, `S` flashloans/temporarily routes a large amount of cNGN into its `SolverAccount` address.
4. The indexer's `handlePhantomOrderPrices` handler fires, calling `aggregatePhantomBids`, which calls `getTotalSolverBalance(destUrl, chain, outputTokenAddress, solver, yieldVaults)` for `S`'s address [8](#0-7)  — observing the inflated balance and assigning `S`'s quote a dominant weight in `weightedMedian`.
5. `S` moves the cNGN back out immediately after.
6. The written `PhantomOrderPriceSnapshot.medianPrice` now reflects `S`'s manipulated quote [9](#0-8) .
7. A real user calls `gateway.quoteIntent(...)`, which resolves to `PhantomSnapshotIntentQuoteStrategy.quote()` and computes `amountOut`/`amountIn` directly from the manipulated `medianPrice` [10](#0-9) , places the order at the skewed rate, and `S` (or a colluding solver) fills it at the now-favorable price, extracting value from the user.
