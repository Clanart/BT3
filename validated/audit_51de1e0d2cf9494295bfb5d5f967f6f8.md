### Title
Malicious signing participant can force the aggregated FROST nonce `R` to the point at infinity, panicking `x()`/`hram()` and aborting Bitcoin transaction signing - (File: networks/bitcoin/src/crypto.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2018-5268 is a crash/availability bug: a crafted input drives the JPEG2000 decoder into memory corruption. In safe Rust the reachable analog is a deterministic panic on attacker-controlled input. In `bitcoin-serai`'s BIP-340 `Hram`, the functions `x()` and `x_only()` call `.expect("point at infinity")` / `.expect("x_only was passed a point which was infinity or odd")` on the group nonce `R` passed into `hram` [1](#0-0) . `R` is the sum of nonce commitments supplied by *all* included participants via their preprocesses, which are read from untrusted bytes by `read_preprocess` → `Commitments::read` [2](#0-1) . `C::read_G` accepts the identity point (it only enforces canonical encoding) [3](#0-2) . A malicious signer who chooses their preprocess after seeing the other participants' preprocesses can pick nonce commitments that cancel the binding-weighted sum, forcing `R = identity` and making `sign_share`/`verify` panic [4](#0-3) .

### Finding Description
- `AlgorithmSignMachine::sign` collects each included participant's `Preprocess.commitments` and folds them, via the FROST binding factor `rho`, into per-generator nonce sums (`nonce_sums`) handed to `A::sign_share`/`verify_share`/`verify` [5](#0-4) .
- The only validation on the incoming bytes is `Commitments::read` (canonical points) plus set-level checks (`included` sorted, no duplicates, `<= n`). No check rejects identity commitments or an identity aggregate nonce [6](#0-5) .
- `Hram::hram` computes `x(R)` unconditionally; `x` panics on infinity [7](#0-6) . `verify` also negates based on `needs_negation(&sig.R)` after `x(R)` already ran [8](#0-7) .
- The crate's own comment claims the panic has "negligible probability ... even with malicious participants present" [9](#0-8) , which is wrong: the adversary is not relying on chance. Because `rho_i` for the malicious participant's preprocess is publicly computable once the other preprocesses are known, the attacker sets their commitment vector to `-(rho_malicious)^{-1} * (sum of everyone else's bound commitments)`, yielding `R = identity` deterministically.

### Impact Explanation
Any signing participant (an unprivileged party able to submit a preprocess during a FROST round for a Bitcoin spend) can deterministically panic every honest signer that processes their preprocess in `TransactionSignMachine::sign`/`TransactionSignatureMachine::complete` [10](#0-9) . A panic in the processor aborts the signing attempt (and, depending on executor behavior, the whole task), permanently stalling spends of vault outputs — a crafted-input denial of service, the same availability impact class as the CVE (CVSS A:H). No key material is leaked.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only that the adversary be a member of the signing set (or induce honest signers to accept their preprocess bytes) and order their preprocess last — trivially satisfiable by any participant, since preprocesses are exchanged per-attempt and the binding factor and challenge are computable by anyone holding the preprocess set. No special privileges, collisions, or brute force are needed; the panic is deterministic.

### Recommendation
- In `sign`/`complete`, reject preprocesses whose bound nonce sum (or any individual commitment) is the identity, returning `FrostError::InvalidPreprocess(l)` instead of proceeding.
- Make `Hram::hram` non-panicking for identity `R`/`A` (e.g., return a fixed challenge or signal `FrostError`), and have `verify` return `None` rather than panic when `sig.R` is identity.
- Mirror the `Signed::read` convention, which already rejects an identity signature nonce [11](#0-10) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume a 2-of-2 Bitcoin `TransactionSignMachine` signing session with participants `i = {1 (honest), 2 (adversary)}`.
2. Honest participant broadcasts its preprocess with nonce commitments `(D_1, E_1)`. The adversary computes the FROST binding factors `rho_1, rho_2` and the group key binding for the message, then constructs its preprocess with commitments `(D_2, E_2)` chosen so `D_1 + rho_1 * E_1 + D_2 + rho_2 * E_2 = identity` (solve `D_2 = -(D_1 + rho_1*E_1)`, `E_2 = 0`-adjusted accordingly per the actual sum formula).
3. The malicious bytes are parsed by `AlgorithmSignMachine::read_preprocess` → `Commitments::read`; identity and arbitrary points are accepted [12](#0-11) .
4. `TransactionSignMachine::sign` computes `nonce_sums = [identity]` and calls `Schnorr::sign_share` → `Hram::hram(&R=identity, ...)` → `x(R)` → `encoded.x().expect("point at infinity")` panics [13](#0-12) , crashing the signing attempt; the same panic fires in `verify` during `complete` if reached [8](#0-7) .

    fn hram(R: &ProjectivePoint, A: &ProjectivePoint, m: &[u8]) -> Scalar {
      const TAG_HASH: Sha256 = Sha256::const_hash(b"BIP0340/challenge");

      let mut data = Sha256::engine();
      data.input(TAG_HASH.as_ref());
      data.input(TAG_HASH.as_ref());
      data.input(&x(R));
      data.input(&x(A));
      data.input(m);

      let c = Scalar::reduce(U256::from_be_slice(Sha256::from_engine(data).as_ref()));
      // If the nonce was odd, sign `r - cx` instead of `r + cx`, allowing us to negate `s` at the
      // end to sign as `-r + cx`
      <_>::conditional_select(&c, &-c, needs_negation(R))
    }
