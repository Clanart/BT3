### Title
`ManualCompound.compound` sweeps the contract's entire reward-token balances to any caller regardless of what that caller actually claimed - ([File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol])

### Summary
`ManualCompound.compound()` lets any wallet trigger a `balanceOf(address(this))`-based sweep of every globally registered reward token (and any attacker-supplied token address) held by the `ManualCompound` contract to `msg.sender`, independent of whether that balance was actually earned by the caller in the same transaction. This mirrors the TOFT `exerciseOption` bug class: unvalidated, caller-supplied parameters are used to trigger a full-balance transfer of tokens the caller does not own.

### Finding Description
`compound()` first claims the caller's own pending rewards via `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` [1](#0-0) , then runs two loops that operate purely on the **current token balance of the `ManualCompound` contract**, not on any per-call accounting of what was actually just claimed for `msg.sender`:

- The "non-compoundable refund" loop iterates over attacker-supplied `_rewards[i][j]` token addresses and, for any token not marked `compoundableRewards`, transfers `IERC20(token).balanceOf(address(this))` — the entire balance, not the caller's share — to `msg.sender`. [2](#0-1) 
- The final loop unconditionally iterates over **all** globally registered `rewards` entries (not filtered by `_lps`/`_rewards` at all) and, for each, reads `receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` and sends/locks/converts that whole balance for `msg.sender`. [3](#0-2) 

Because these loops key off contract-wide `balanceOf`, any residual balance held by `ManualCompound` (e.g., un-swept dust from a prior `compound()` call left after `IConverter.convertFor`, `ILocker.lockFor`, or `ISimpleHelper.depositFor` didn't consume 100% due to rounding, or tokens accidentally sent to the contract, or overlap between multiple in-flight `compound()` calls) is attributed entirely to whichever address next calls `compound()`. An attacker can call `compound()` with empty `_lps`/`_rewards` arrays (or with `_lps`/`_rewards` unrelated to the residual token) to claim nothing of their own yet still walk away with the leftover balance, because the final loop runs regardless of the `_lps`/`_rewards` arguments supplied.

This is the same root cause as the reported TOFT issue: a function that performs `IERC20(token).safeTransfer(caller, IERC20(token).balanceOf(address(this)))` using unvalidated/attacker-influenced parameters, with `msg.sender` at the reward-pool level actually being the intermediate contract (`ManualCompound`) that legitimately custodies pooled funds, not the caller who receives them.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged wallet can drain reward-token balances held by `ManualCompound` that do not belong to it, resulting in direct theft of other users' unclaimed/queued yield sitting in the compounder contract. This satisfies the "theft or permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends on `ManualCompound` actually holding a non-zero balance of a registered/compoundable token between transactions (e.g., due to rounding dust from `convertFor`/`lockFor`/`depositFor`, or transient balances from concurrent user calls in the same block/mempool). Given the contract loops over `balanceOf` rather than tracking exact per-call amounts, such residual balances are a normal byproduct of the conversion/locking math and are not prevented by any guard, so this is realistically triggerable, though the amount stolen per opportunity is bounded by whatever residual balance currently sits in the contract rather than the full pool of staked value.

### Recommendation
Do not rely on `balanceOf(address(this))` deltas across unrelated calls. Track the exact amount received from `multiclaimOnBehalf` for the current `msg.sender` within the same transaction (e.g., via `balanceOf` snapshot immediately before and after the specific claim call, or by having `multiclaimOnBehalf`/`getRewards` return per-token amounts) and only ever transfer/convert/lock that delta, never the full contract balance. Additionally, gate the final loop so it only processes tokens that correspond to the `_lps`/`_rewards` actually claimed in the current call, rather than iterating unconditionally over all globally registered reward tokens.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume `ManualCompound` has some non-zero balance of a registered `rewards[i].tokenAddress` (e.g., `X`) left over from a prior `compound()` call's rounding remainder in `IConverter.convertFor`/`ILocker.lockFor`.
2. Attacker calls `compound(_lps = [], _rewards = [], _convertRatio = 0, _minRec = 0, _lockMgp = false)`.
3. `multiclaimOnBehalf` is a no-op (empty arrays), so the attacker claims nothing of their own. [1](#0-0) 
4. The first refund loop is skipped (`_lps.length == 0`). [4](#0-3) 
5. The final loop still executes over every entry in `rewards`, reads `receivedBalance = IERC20(X).balanceOf(address(this))` (the leftover balance, not owned by the attacker), and since no convertor/locker/helper is set (or `_lockMgp=false`), transfers that entire balance to `msg.sender` via `IERC20(X).safeTransfer(msg.sender, receivedBalance)`. [3](#0-2) 
6. The attacker receives tokens they never earned, at zero cost.
