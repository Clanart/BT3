### Title
PedPoP encryption key registration lacks proof-of-possession and duplicate detection, letting a participant redirect shares to an unintended decryptor - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
In PedPoP, each participant registers a long-term encryption public key (`enc_key`) via `EncryptionKeyMessage`, and senders ECDH a fresh per-message key against it to encrypt secret shares. Registration performs no proof-of-possession check and no duplicate-key check: `Encryption::register`/`Decryption::register` blindly store whatever point the participant supplies (`enc_keys.insert(participant, msg.enc_key)`). A malicious participant can therefore register another victim participant's `enc_pub_key` as their own. Every share addressed to the attacker is then encrypted under the victim's private key — the victim can decrypt all of the attacker's incoming shares and recover the attacker's final secret share, while the attacker (who cannot decrypt) is forced into abort/blame where they cannot even produce a valid `EncryptionKeyProof`, misblaming themselves. This is the Serai analog of the credential-forwarding class: secret material addressed to one party is silently delivered to a different party than intended.

### Finding Description
`EncryptionKeyMessage` carries `{ msg, enc_key }` with no PoP on `enc_key` (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:50-53`). `Decryption::register` only asserts the participant hasn't registered before (`encryption.rs:356-360`) and `Encryption::register` forwards directly (`encryption.rs:452-458`). `Encryption::encrypt` then derives the shared cipher key as `ecdh(per_message_key, enc_keys[&participant])` (`encryption.rs:460-467`, `ecdh` at line 95). Because the per-message PoP (`pop`, lines 83-91) binds only the ephemeral key — not the registered `enc_key` — a participant Eve can set `enc_key = Bob.enc_pub_key`. All senders encrypt Eve's shares under Bob's ECDH key. Bob, holding `enc_key` private, computes `ecdh(enc_key_priv, msg.key)` and decrypts every share addressed to Eve, obtaining Eve's summed secret share. The per-message PoP mitigation documented in the code (lines 84-90) and spec (`spec/cryptography/Distributed Key Generation.md`) only prevents *message*-key co-option for blame amplification; it does not cover *registration*-key co-option, and nothing rejects two participants registering the same point.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged participant with public inputs (a crafted commitment/`EncryptionKeyMessage`) causes every honest sender to encrypt shares intended for the attacker's index to a key the attacker chose — a victim's key. The victim recovers the attacker's full `ThresholdKeys` secret share (sum of all incoming shares, `lib.rs:484`). That is concrete key-share recovery of a participant's secret share, reducing the effective threshold and leaking credential material to a party it was never addressed to — the same shape as forwarding authorization headers to an unintended server. Additionally, blame is corrupted: the attacker can't produce `EncryptionKeyProof` (no private key), so honest-looking accusations resolve incorrectly.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any single participant in a PedPoP session: they need only observe the victim's registered `enc_pub_key` (broadcast in the commitment round) and reuse it as their own. No collusion, no broken BFT, no timing. The only prerequisite is that the attacker registers their poisoned key, which the protocol accepts unconditionally.

### Recommendation
Require a proof-of-possession (Schnorr signature over `context || participant || enc_key`) inside `EncryptionKeyMessage` and verify it in `Decryption::register`, and/or reject duplicate `enc_key` values across participants during registration.

### Proof of Concept
1. n-of-t PedPoP; Bob registers `enc_pub_key_B` honestly in round 1.
2. Eve reads Bob's `EncryptionKeyMessage` and constructs her own with `enc_key = enc_pub_key_B` plus her valid `Commitments`. `register` accepts it.
3. Each honest participant `j` calls `encrypt(rng, Eve, share_j(Eve))`, computing `ecdh(k_j, enc_pub_key_B)` and transmitting `key = k_j·G`, PoP, ciphertext.
4. Bob computes `ecdh(enc_key_B_priv, k_j·G)` for each message addressed to Eve, applies `cipher(context, ecdh)` (static IV `b"DKG IV v0.2\0"`), decrypts every `SecretShare`, and sums them to recover Eve's secret share — credentials forwarded to an unintended recipient, with Eve additionally misblamed when she fails to produce `EncryptionKeyProof`.
