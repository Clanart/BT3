### Title
Wrapped SEI (WSEI) `withdraw()` uses `.transfer()` with a fixed 2300 gas stipend, permanently bricking unwraps for smart-contract holders - (File: contracts/src/WSEI.sol)

### Summary
The canonical Wrapped SEI contract's `withdraw()` function sends native SEI back to `msg.sender` using Solidity's `.transfer()`, which forwards only 2300 gas. Any smart-contract holder of WSEI (multisig, smart-contract wallet, DeFi vault, proxy, etc.) whose `receive`/`fallback` needs more than 2300 gas — or which is reached through a proxy that itself consumes gas before forwarding the call — will have every `withdraw()` call permanently revert, freezing their SEI inside the WSEI contract with no alternate recovery path.

### Finding Description
`WSEI.sol` is Sei's system Wrapped-native-token contract, analogous to WETH9, compiled and embedded as chain artifacts and deployable/deployed via the dedicated `CmdDeployWSEI` CLI command: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Its `withdraw` function is: [3](#0-2) 

`payable(msg.sender).transfer(wad)` hard-caps the gas forwarded to the recipient at 2300 gas (the historical Solidity gas stipend for `transfer`/`send`). Since anyone (EOA or contract) can hold WSEI and call `withdraw`, this is directly reachable by any unprivileged public-RPC/transaction sender who deposits SEI into WSEI from a smart-contract account and later tries to unwrap it. If the caller contract's `receive()`/`fallback()` performs any non-trivial logic (e.g., updating state, emitting events, forwarding funds, or is itself a proxy adding call overhead), the 2300 gas stipend is insufficient and the SEI transfer — and therefore the entire `withdraw()` transaction — reverts unconditionally, every time.

Because `msg.sender` cannot be changed for that contract's own balance, and there is no alternative withdrawal path (no `call`-based fallback, no pull-payment mechanism) in `WSEI.sol`, the locked WSEI balance becomes permanently non-redeemable to native SEI for that account.

### Impact Explanation
This meets the "permanent freezing of funds" bar: any smart-contract account (common for DeFi protocols, multisig wallets such as Gnosis Safe, or other contracts composing with WSEI) that wraps SEI into WSEI and cannot receive plain-value transfers within 2300 gas will have those funds permanently stuck in the WSEI contract, unable to unwrap back to native SEI through any function exposed by the contract.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is not trivial to trigger accidentally by simple EOAs, but is realistically triggered by any smart-contract integrator of WSEI (a very common usage pattern for wrapped-native tokens in DeFi), and requires no special privilege — a normal transaction from a contract account with a gas-hungry `receive()`/`fallback()`, or one accessed through a proxy, will hit this deterministically on every attempt.

### Recommendation
Replace `payable(msg.sender).transfer(wad)` with a low-level `call` and explicit success check, forwarding all available gas and following checks-effects-interactions:
```solidity
(bool success, ) = payable(msg.sender).call{value: wad}("");
require(success, "WSEI: SEI transfer failed");
```
This avoids the fixed 2300 gas stipend limitation while still reverting on failure, and matches the widely-adopted fix pattern used by modern WETH-style contracts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy a contract `MaliciousReceiver` whose `receive()` function performs an operation costing more than 2300 gas (e.g., an `SSTORE` to a fresh slot, which alone costs 20000 gas).
2. From `MaliciousReceiver`, call `WSEI.deposit{value: X}()` to wrap SEI.
3. From `MaliciousReceiver`, call `WSEI.withdraw(X)`.
4. The internal `payable(msg.sender).transfer(X)` call runs out of gas inside `MaliciousReceiver.receive()`, causing `transfer` to revert and the whole `withdraw()` transaction to fail.
5. `MaliciousReceiver`'s WSEI balance remains locked; every subsequent call to `withdraw()` reverts identically, permanently freezing the funds.
