### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Why the claimed binding does not break anything in scope:**

`get_deposit_scripts` builds two leaves for a `BaseDeposit`: the `BaseDepositScript` keyed on `nofn_xonly_pk` (the N-of-N move-to-vault spend path) and a `TimelockScript` keyed on whatever x-only key is extracted from the depositor-supplied `recovery_taproot_address` via `try_get_taproot_pk` [1](#0-0) . Both leaves are committed into the taproot output that becomes `expected_scriptpubkey`, which `Verifier::is_deposit_valid` compares byte-for-byte against the on-chain `deposit_txout_in_chain.script_pubkey` [2](#0-1) .

If a depositor sets `recovery_taproot_address` so that its extracted x-only key equals `nofn_xonly_pk`, the resulting `TimelockScript` requires (after `user_takes_after` blocks) a signature from the same key used for the N-of-N move-to-vault path [3](#0-2) . However:

- The signature needed to spend via that timelock leaf is a Schnorr signature over the sighash of *that specific transaction spending that specific outpoint/leaf*. Verifiers only ever produce nofn signatures for the specific presigned transactions in the graph (i.e., the move-to-vault transaction for that exact deposit), never a general-purpose signature reusable across leaves or scripts. Colliding the recovery key with `nofn_xonly_pk` does not cause verifiers to sign this alternate leaf/message, so no usable "aggregated nofn signature" is ever produced for it.
- Because the deposit's `deposit_outpoint` and `BaseDepositScript` (which embeds the deposit's `evm_address`) are unique per deposit, and Schnorr sighashes bind to the specific outpoint being spent, a collision cannot be reused across deposits — even if two different depositors set the same colliding recovery key, a signature valid for one deposit's outpoint is not valid for another's.
- The effect of such a collision is entirely self-contained to the depositor's own recovery leaf: at worst it makes the depositor's *own* recovery path require an nofn signature that is never produced in practice for this purpose, which is self-sabotage, not fund theft. No third party's deposit, operator reimbursement, or collateral is affected, and no N-of-N partial signature for an unauthorized spend is ever created.

This does not correspond to any of the in-scope Critical/High impact categories (no BTC leaves a move-to-vault UTXO without a fronted withdrawal, no wrong reimbursement, no honest operator/collateral harm, no N-of-N signature obtained for an unauthorized spend), and the equality `recovery_extracted_xonly_pk == nofn_xonly_pk` — even if permitted by the current checks — has no reachable exploit path through this repository's signing or verification logic.
