### Title
Attacker can front-run/back-run `queueNewRewards` to steal previously queued rewards via `totalStaked()==0` griefing - (File: rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol)

### Summary
`BaseRewardPool._provisionReward` checks the *spot* value of `totalStaked()` (live balance of the staking token held by `operator`) to decide whether new rewards should be immediately distributed via `rewardPerTokenStored` or parked in `queuedRewards`. Because this check has no time-weighting or snapshot protection, a staker who observes a pending `queueNewRewards` transaction in the mempool can withdraw their full stake immediately before it lands (forcing `totalStaked()==0` and the reward to be queued instead of distributed), then re-deposit right after to become the dominant (or sole) staker before the next `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` call folds the queued amount into `rewardPerTokenStored`, disproportionately capturing rewards meant for the broader staker set.

### Finding Description
`_provisionReward` in `rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol` (lines 297-320):

```solidity
if (this.totalStaked() == 0) {
    rewardInfo.queuedRewards += _amountReward;
} else {
    if (rewardInfo.queuedRewards > 0) {
        _amountReward += rewardInfo.queuedRewards;
        rewardInfo.queuedRewards = 0;
    }
    rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored =
        rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored +
        (_amountReward * 10**stakingDecimals()) / this.totalStaked();
}
``` [1](#0-0) 

`totalStaked()` is a live read of `IERC20(stakingToken).balanceOf(operator)` [2](#0-1) , not a snapshot/checkpoint tied to the reward accrual period. This means the accounting of "how much reward-per-token to credit" is entirely dependent on whoever holds stake at the exact instant `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` (an external, unprivileged-callable function per `donateRewards`) executes.

Exploit flow:
1. Attacker observes a manager's pending `queueNewRewards()` transaction in the mempool.
2. Attacker (and colluding/other stakers, or simply timing around low-liquidity periods) withdraws their full stake via MasterMagpie's withdraw path, driving `totalStaked()` to 0 right before the manager's tx lands.
3. `_provisionReward` sees `totalStaked()==0` and adds the entire `_amountReward` to `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` instead of distributing it pro-rata to the reward index.
4. Attacker deposits a small amount, becoming the dominant/sole staker.
5. On the next `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` call (which anyone can trigger via `donateRewards` since it's an external, permissionless function once a reward token is registered) [3](#0-2) , the full `queuedRewards` balance is folded into `rewardPerTokenStored` and divided by the now much smaller `totalStaked()`, crediting a disproportionate share to the attacker rather than the depositors who were staked when the reward was originally earned/queued.

No mechanism (checkpointing, time-weighting, minimum stake duration, or reward-per-share snapshotting keyed to deposit/withdraw times) exists to prevent this manipulation of the reward-per-token calculation.

### Impact Explanation
This constitutes theft of unclaimed yield: rewards intended to be distributed pro-rata to the staker set present during the reward period are instead captured disproportionately by whichever address controls the staking-token balance at the precise moment `queuedRewards` is folded into `rewardPerTokenStored`. This matches the "theft of unclaimed yield" / "direct theft of user funds" Immunefi impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Feasibility depends on: (1) the ability to observe/front-run the manager's `queueNewRewards` mempool transaction (public mempool visibility, standard MEV capability), (2) the pool reaching `totalStaked()==0` transiently, which is realistic in low-TVL pools or pools where a single/few large stakers dominate and can time their withdraw/redeposit, and (3) a subsequent `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` call to fold the queued rewards - which is guaranteed to happen eventually since it's a normal, recurring operational call, and can even be triggered permissionlessly by the attacker via `donateRewards` with a near-zero donation once they are dominant staker. No special privileges are required; only ordinary stake/withdraw/deposit calls and mempool observation. This is a real, repeatable, capital-light griefing/theft path, though its scale is bounded by how much of the pool's stake can be simultaneously withdrawn/re-entered by the attacker (largest practical impact in pools where the attacker is a whale or where the pool has few stakers, e.g., early-stage or low-TVL reward pools).

### Recommendation
Decouple reward distribution from spot `totalStaked()` at the instant of `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards`. Options: (a) track a time-weighted or checkpointed total-staked value that cannot be manipulated within a single block/transaction sequence, (b) apply a minimum-holding-period or cooldown on withdraw-then-redeposit before a user's balance counts toward reward distribution, or (c) accrue rewards continuously per-second against historical stake snapshots (similar to a synthetix-style rewards-duration streaming model) rather than lump-summing into `rewardPerTokenStored` based on the staked balance at the moment of `queueNewRewards`.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, `BaseRewardPool`, staking token, reward token; register reward manager.
2. Have User A and User B each stake 100 tokens (`totalStaked()=200`).
3. Simulate manager's `queueNewRewards(1000, rewardToken)` transaction pending in mempool.
4. Before it lands, have the attacker (and/or all stakers) call `withdraw(all)` so `totalStaked()==0`.
5. Manager's `queueNewRewards(1000, rewardToken)` executes; assert `rewards[rewardToken].queuedRewards == 1000` and `rewardPerTokenStored` unchanged.
6. Attacker calls `deposit(1)`, becoming sole staker.
7. Call `donateRewards(0, rewardToken)` or a second `queueNewRewards(0, rewardToken)` to trigger folding of `queuedRewards` into `rewardPerTokenStored` divided by `totalStaked()==1`.
8. Attacker calls `getReward()`; assert attacker's payout ≈ 1000 (minus dust), far exceeding their fair share (0 tokens staked during the reward-earning period), while User A/B who were staked when the reward was queued receive nothing from that 1000.
9. Assert conservation invariant violated: attacker's claimed reward > proportional share based on stake-time-weighted contribution.
