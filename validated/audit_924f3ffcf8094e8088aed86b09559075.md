### Title
Unauthenticated PedPoP encryption keys permit redirection of encrypted secret shares - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
PedPoP authenticates the coefficient commitments with a Schnorr proof of knowledge, but the accompanying long-term encryption public key is neither proof-bound nor validated. `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` accepts an arbitrary group element as `enc_key`, and `SecretShareMachine::verify_r1` verifies only the coefficient-commitment PoK before registering that key. A malicious participant can therefore supply another party’s encryption key and cause every dealer’s share intended for the malicious participant index to be encrypted to that unrelated key.

### Finding Description
`EncryptionKeyMessage::read` deserializes the payload and `enc_key` independently, with no proof that the submitter controls `enc_key` and no rejection of identity or otherwise unsuitable keys. [1](#0-0) 

During round-one verification, `verify_r1` registers each participant’s supplied encryption key before checking only `msg.sig` against `msg.commitments[0]`; the PoK challenge covers the participant and commitments, but not `enc_key`. [2](#0-1) 

The challenge itself binds `context`, participant, nonce, and serialized commitments, confirming that the encryption key is absent from the authentication transcript. [3](#0-2) 

Subsequent share encryption trusts the registered key directly: `Encryption::encrypt` passes `self.decryption.enc_keys[&participant]` to `encrypt`, which derives ECDH from that attacker-selected point. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
A PedPoP participant can make all secret shares addressed to its participant index decryptable by the private-key holder of an arbitrary registered public key, rather than by a key proved to belong to that participant. If those ciphertexts are delivered or relayed to that holder, the holder learns share material for an additional participant index.

For low thresholds, this can become key-share or group-secret recovery. For example, with `t = 2`, an honest participant whose encryption key is substituted for the malicious participant’s key can obtain evaluations of every dealer polynomial at both its own index and the malicious participant’s index. Two evaluations uniquely reconstruct each degree-one polynomial, exposing every dealer’s constant coefficient and therefore the aggregate secret.

Even where reconstruction is not possible, substituting the identity or another unusable key causes shares to be encrypted under a public or undecryptable ECDH result, breaking confidentiality assumptions or causing DKG failure.

### Likelihood Explanation
The malicious participant only needs to send a syntactically valid `EncryptionKeyMessage` containing valid commitments and PoK, while setting `enc_key` to another participant’s previously published key or to the identity. Because `enc_key` is outside the PoK and is not validated, the message passes `verify_r1`. The attack requires participation in the DKG and visibility or control over delivery of the resulting encrypted-share messages, but no private-key compromise or malformed curve encoding.

### Recommendation
Bind the encryption key to the round-one authenticated commitment message and require proof of possession for it:

- Include `enc_key` in the Schnorr PoK transcript, preferably by committing it in `cached_msg` or signing a compound statement.
- Reject the identity element and any other invalid encryption public key during `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` or `Decryption::register`.
- Derive a fresh authenticated encryption-key transcript per participant and context, rather than relying solely on external channel attribution.
- Add tests for foreign-key substitution, identity keys, and replayed encryption keys.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptually, participant `l` constructs a normal PedPoP commitment message but replaces its `enc_key` field with victim participant `v`’s published encryption public key:

```rust
let mut msg = victim_registration_message;
msg.msg = valid_commitments_for_l;   // valid PoK for participant l
msg.enc_key = victim_enc_pub_key;    // unauthenticated foreign key
```

`SecretShareMachine::verify_r1` accepts it because only `valid_commitments_for_l.sig` is verified. Every honest dealer then calls `Encryption::encrypt`, computes `victim_enc_pub_key * ephemeral_scalar`, and encrypts `f_j(l)` to the victim. If the victim obtains those ciphertexts and `t = 2`, the victim decrypts `f_j(l)` in addition to its legitimate `f_j(v)`, reconstructs each dealer polynomial, and recovers the aggregate secret.
