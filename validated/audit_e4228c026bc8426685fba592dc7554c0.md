### Title
Identity encryption key in PedPoP DKG causes secret shares to be encrypted under a publicly derivable ChaCha20 key - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
`EncryptionKeyMessage::read` accepts the identity point as a participant's registered encryption key (`enc_key`), because it is deserialized with `Ciphersuite::read_G` which enforces only canonical encoding, not non-identity. `Encryption::encrypt` then computes the ECDH shared secret as `msg.key_to = enc_key * ephemeral_scalar`; with `enc_key = identity`, the shared secret is the identity point for every sender, so the ChaCha20 key stream in `cipher` is derived solely from the public `context` and the public identity encoding. Every `SecretShare` ciphertext addressed to that participant is therefore decryptable by any passive observer, leaking the full secret share `s_l = Σ f_i(l)` of the participant who registered the identity key.

### Finding Description
- `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` calls `C::read_G(reader)` for `enc_key`. For the `Ciphersuite` bound used in `pedpop`, this resolves to `Ciphersuite::read_G` (`crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-101`), which performs canonical-encoding validation but does not reject the identity point. The identity-rejecting variant (`Curve::read_G`, `crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131`) exists only under the `frost::Curve` trait, which `pedpop` does not use.
- `Decryption::register` (`encryption.rs:351-362`) stores `msg.enc_key` with no validation at all — no identity check, no torsion check beyond decoding, and critically no proof of possession or proof of knowledge for the registered key.
- `Encryption::encrypt` → `encrypt` (`encryption.rs:135-168`) computes `ecdh(&key, to)` = `enc_key * ephemeral`, i.e. `identity * k = identity`, and feeds `to_bytes()` of that point into `cipher` (`encryption.rs:101-133`) along with only the public `context: [u8; 32]`. The resulting ChaCha20 key/IV are computable by anyone.
- `SecretShareMachine::generate_secret_shares` (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:357-370`) encrypts `f_i(l)` to every other participant using exactly this path, over a channel the protocol explicitly assumes is only authenticated, not confidential — the entire encryption layer exists because the channel is not private.
- There is no PoP/PoK on `enc_key` anywhere; the Schnorr PoP inside `EncryptedMessage` covers only the per-message ephemeral key (`encryption.rs:83-91`, `pop_challenge`), not the registered `enc_key`.

### Impact Explanation
A participant who registers `enc_key = identity` causes all `n - 1` honest parties to encrypt their polynomial evaluations `f_i(l)` under a keystream that any observer of the wire can reproduce: `key = Transcript("DKG Encryption v0.2" || context || "encryption_key" || identity_bytes).challenge("key")[..32]`, IV `"DKG IV v0.2\0"`. Summing the decrypted shares yields the registrant's complete secret share `s_l` in the clear. This degrades the threshold key's security by broadcasting an honest-computed share publicly, and by enabling a faulty participant to exfiltrate their share (or, combined with blame/reveal flows, shares of the set) to arbitrary third parties while appearing well-formed — every PoK, PoP, and share-verification statement still passes. Analogous to the avahi flaw: the system responds with sensitive material to/for a party whose supplied "address" (key) was never validated as legitimate, converting an authenticated-but-public channel into a plaintext disclosure.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single unprivileged DKG participant controls their own `EncryptionKeyMessage` bytes end-to-end; encoding the identity point is a trivial, canonical, well-formed serialization that passes all existing checks. No grinding, collusion, or privileged position is required — only sending one crafted round-1 message.

### Recommendation
Reject the identity point when reading `enc_key` in `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` (use an identity-rejecting reader or an explicit `is_identity` check in `Decryption::register`), and preferably require a proof of possession for the registered encryption key so that a registrant must know its discrete logarithm and cannot register degenerate or third-party keys.

### Proof of Concept
1. Malicious participant `l` runs `KeyGenMachine::generate_coefficients`, then mutates its outgoing `EncryptionKeyMessage<Commitments>` so `enc_key = C::G::identity()` (write `identity.to_bytes()` into the serialized message; `write` emits `msg` then `enc_key`).
2. All honest participants call `SecretShareMachine::generate_secret_shares`; `verify_r1` succeeds (the PoK covers `commitments`, not `enc_key`), and each calls `self.encryption.encrypt(rng, l, share)` → `ecdh = identity * k = identity`.
3. Each honest participant emits `EncryptedMessage` ciphertext `c_i = share_i ⊕ ChaCha20(k_pub)`, where `k_pub` is derived from public `context` and `identity.to_bytes()`.
4. A passive observer computes `k_pub`, decrypts every `c_i` to `f_i(l)`, and reconstructs `s_l = Σ f_i(l)` — participant `l`'s full secret share — without any private input.
