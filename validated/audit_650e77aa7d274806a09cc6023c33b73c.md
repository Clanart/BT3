### Title
Public `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` can be re-invoked to re-snapshot a shrinking denominator, letting an attacker steal disproportionate bonus funds - (File: rewards/Airdrop.sol)

### Summary
`claim()` only auto-triggers `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` when `totalEndRemainingAllocation == 0` [1](#0-0) , making the automatic path effectively one-shot, but the public `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` function itself has no such guard and can be called by anyone, any number of times, as long as `block.timestamp >= periodsEndTime[4]` [2](#0-1) . Because `getBonusAmount` divides by the current `totalEndRemainingAllocation` [3](#0-2) , an attacker holding the largest remaining allocation can wait for other holders to claim (shrinking `totalRemainingAllocation`), then re-call `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` directly to re-snapshot a much smaller denominator right before their own `claim()`, inflating their bonus share far beyond their fair proportion of `totalBonus`.

### Finding Description
`totalBonus` accumulates forfeited allocations from users who claim before full vesting (period 4) [4](#0-3) . After `periodsEndTime[4]`, remaining holders are meant to split `totalBonus` proportionally to their share of the total remaining allocation pool at the moment vesting ends, via `bonusAmount = userAllocation * totalBonus / totalEndRemainingAllocation` [5](#0-4) . This design only works correctly if `totalEndRemainingAllocation` is captured exactly once, immediately when period 4 ends, and never changes afterward — otherwise the sum of individually-computed bonus shares no longer sums to `totalBonus`.

`claim()` attempts to enforce "once" semantics by guarding the auto-call with `if (totalEndRemainingAllocation == 0)` [6](#0-5) , but `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` is a `public` function with no access control and no check preventing repeated calls — it unconditionally overwrites `totalEndRemainingAllocation = totalRemainingAllocation` every time it is invoked post-vesting [2](#0-1) . Since `totalRemainingAllocation` decreases with every claim (`totalRemainingAllocation -= userAllocation` at line 164), an attacker can:
1. Let the first claimant (anyone) trigger the initial snapshot `totalEndRemainingAllocation = R0` (full remaining pool at vesting end).
2. Allow/wait for other holders to claim, shrinking `totalRemainingAllocation` down toward just the attacker's own allocation `A`.
3. Directly call the public `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` to overwrite `totalEndRemainingAllocation` to the now-small `totalRemainingAllocation` (≈`A`).
4. Call `claim()`: `getBonusAmount` now computes `A * totalBonus / A ≈ totalBonus`, letting the attacker claim nearly the entire bonus pool instead of their fair `A/R0` share.

The already-paid bonuses to earlier claimants (computed against the larger `R0`) plus the attacker's inflated claim now sum to more than `totalBonus`, draining `aidropToken` balance that also backs other users' principal allocations — this either causes later legitimate claims to revert with `InsufficientBalance` (permanent freezing of funds) or directly transfers funds meant for other users to the attacker (direct theft).

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the invariant that `getClaimableAmount`/`allocations` reconciliation and total distributed bonus must not exceed `totalBonus`. The attacker (unprivileged, holding the largest remaining allocation) can extract a disproportionate share of the airdrop bonus pool at the expense of other legitimate claimants, and/or cause the contract's token balance to become insufficient for remaining claimants — matching Critical: direct theft of user funds / freezing of funds.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully feasible for any unprivileged EOA: `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` has no `onlyOwner`/role modifier and can be called directly by anyone once `block.timestamp >= periodsEndTime[4]` [2](#0-1) . The attacker only needs to already be a registered allocation holder (a normal airdrop recipient) with the largest remaining allocation, and to time their transactions relative to other claimants (observable on-chain), requiring no special capital, flash loans, or privileged roles.

### Recommendation
Make the snapshot strictly one-shot: guard `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` itself (not just the caller in `claim()`) so it can only set `totalEndRemainingAllocation` once, e.g. add `if (totalEndRemainingAllocation != 0) return;` (or revert) inside `updateEndRemainingAllocation()`, ensuring both the automatic and manual paths enforce the identical one-shot constraint.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `Airdrop` with `startTime`, register allocations for users `A` (large, attacker), `B`, `C` (smaller), fund contract with `aidropToken`.
2. Warp to `periodsEndTime[4]`.
3. Have `B` call `claim()` — this sets `totalEndRemainingAllocation = R0` (sum of A+B+C's remaining allocations) and pays `B` `bonus = allocB * totalBonus / R0`.
4. Have `C` call `claim()` similarly, paid against `R0`.
5. Attacker `A` calls `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` directly (public, no role needed) — now `totalEndRemainingAllocation` is overwritten to `totalRemainingAllocation ≈ allocA` (since B and C already zeroed out).
6. Attacker `A` calls `claim()` — assert `getBonusAmount(A)` computed as `allocA * totalBonus / allocA ≈ totalBonus`, i.e., far larger than `allocA/R0 * totalBonus`.
7. Assert: `bonus(B) + bonus(C) + bonus(A) > totalBonus` (over-distribution), and/or assert contract's `aidropToken` balance is insufficient to pay a hypothetical remaining claimant, and assert attacker's net token balance increased beyond their fair proportional share — confirming the invariant break and fund theft.
