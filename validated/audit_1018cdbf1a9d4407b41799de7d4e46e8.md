### Title
Fee-rate truncation in `median_fee` underestimates the block's fee rate, producing underpriced multisig transactions - (File: networks/bitcoin/../processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs)

### Summary
The Unstoppable report is a "rounding in the wrong direction against the protocol's own holdings" bug: a division result that should favor the vault is instead truncated in favor of the user. Serai's in-scope analog lives in Bitcoin fee estimation: `Bitcoin::median_fee` truncates each transaction's fee rate with `(in_value - out) / tx.vsize()` and then takes the median. Every sampled rate is biased downward by up to 1 sat/vbyte, and the median is drawn from attacker-influenced mempool/block contents. The resulting `fee_per_vbyte` is passed to `SignableTransaction::new` as `fee.0`, so transactions built for payments/change pay a lower effective rate than the true block median — the protocol underpays miners and over-delivers value relative to its own accounting intent.

### Finding Description
In `processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs` `median_fee`, each non-coinbase transaction's fee rate is computed as `fees.push((in_value - out) / u64::try_from(tx.vsize()).unwrap())` — plain integer division that floors the true rate. The median of these floored values is returned with only a `.max(1)` floor. In `make_signable_transaction` this fee is used directly as `fee_per_vbyte` for `BSignableTransaction::new`, which sets `needed_fee = fee_per_vbyte * vbytes`. Per the Unstoppable rounding rule, the fee a vault/protocol must pay to succeed should be rounded up (ceil) so the protocol doesn't spend more (in the form of stuck capital) than intended; here it is rounded down.

Reachability: `median_fee` samples `block.txdata` — arbitrary transactions any unprivileged party can broadcast and have mined. An attacker who fills a block with transactions just above the 1 sat/vbyte relay minimum pushes the median to its floor; combined with truncation, `median_fee` returns 1 sat/vbyte regardless of real market conditions. Serai's multisig then signs payment/change transactions at that rate (the `TooLowFee` guard only enforces the absolute relay minimum, which truncation also floors via `/1000`), so transactions are stuck unconfirmed while payments are already deducted from scheduler plans.

### Impact Explanation
Payment and change transactions emitted by the Serai multisig are systematically underpriced. At best they sit unconfirmed until replaced/expired; at worst (with sustained block stuffing) all scheduler payments at a block's height are priced at the relay floor and never confirm, while `amortize`/scheduler accounting has already committed the fee and payment amounts — funds are locked in limbo and operating-cost accounting drifts (leftover change computed off the wrong rate). This is a Medium: no direct theft, but persistent denial of service on withdrawals and incorrect fee accounting reachable entirely through public Bitcoin transactions.

### Likelihood Explanation
High-ish for the truncation effect (every block), moderate for exploitation. Block stuffing with minimum-fee transactions is cheap and any party can do it; miners include them at 1 sat/vbyte. Truncation alone guarantees the estimate is never above the true per-tx rates, so the multisig always lands at the bottom of whatever fee range the attacker shapes.

### Recommendation
Round up in `median_fee`: `fees.push((in_value - out).div_ceil(u64::try_from(tx.vsize()).unwrap()))`, and likewise make the relay-minimum comparison in `SignableTransaction::new` (`send.rs` line ~211) use ceiling division: `((DEFAULT_MIN_RELAY_TX_FEE * vbytes) + 999) / 1000`. Consider also a multi-block median and an absolute floor above the relay minimum so an attacker cannot pin the estimate.

### Proof of Concept
- Root cause: `processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs` `median_fee` lines ~406-414 — `(in_value - out) / tx.vsize()` floors each sample; `fees.get(fees.len()/2)` picks the floored median.
- Consumption: `make_signable_transaction` (~431-451) passes `fee.0` into `BSignableTransaction::new`; in `networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs` lines ~204-213 `needed_fee = fee_per_vbyte * vbytes` and the minimum check `(DEFAULT_MIN_RELAY_TX_FEE * vbytes) / 1000` also floors.
- Sketch: attacker broadcasts N minimum-fee transactions that get mined in block B. `median_fee(B)` returns `1`. `signable_transaction` builds a payment TX at 1 sat/vbyte while market rate is e.g. 20 sat/vbyte; the multisig-signed TX never confirms, leaving scheduled payments unexecuted until rebroadcast, with the scheduler having already recorded the (wrong) fee amortization.

Caveat: the dominant exploitation vector is the median selection itself; truncation adds a consistent downward bias but under pure truncation alone the error is <1 sat/vbyte. If only the strict rounding-direction defect is in scope and median manipulation is considered out of scope ( attacker-influenced oracle data), then this analog does not meet the bar and should be downgraded.
