The claim's `payee` is bound into the signed message itself — the relayer signs `outbound_consensus_delivery_message(set_id, destination, payee)` with the EVM key that must match the `EvmHost._epochs[set_id]` slot, and the pallet verifies that binding before paying out [1](#0-0) . The reward transfer target is the `payee` decoded from the claim struct, not `msg.sender`/the extrinsic submitter [2](#0-1) . Anyone can submit the extrinsic and it pays the same `payee_A` regardless of who broadcasts it first, because `payee` is cryptographically fixed at signing time and cannot be swapped post-hoc without invalidating the signature [3](#0-2) .

So resubmitting the identical `(state_proof, set_id, payee_A, signature)` verbatim ahead of the original submitter does not redirect funds — it still pays `payee_A`, just possibly with someone else's gas paying for the call. The idempotency guard `OutboundConsensusRotationsClaimed::contains_key(destination, set_id)` only prevents double payment, and the outcome (correct beneficiary) is unaffected by who submits the extrinsic [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) . This is exactly the front-run-only category explicitly excluded by the review scope, and the proof-of-concept described in the question would itself confirm that frontrunning cannot redirect the reward.
