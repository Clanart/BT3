### Title
`ReceivedOutput::read` accepts an unconstrained `TxOut` value/outpoint, letting a supplied "received" amount mismatch reality - (File: networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/mod.rs)

### Summary
The Dinari finding is the absence of a consistency check between an operator-supplied `receivedAmount` and the order's actual terms: the accounting layer trusts a value asserted by an outside party. The Serai analog lives in the bitcoin wallet crate: `ReceivedOutput::read` deserializes `(offset, TxOut, OutPoint)` from untrusted bytes and stores them as-is, and `SignableTransaction::new` then treats `output.value` as ground truth for input sums, fee/change computation, and the BIP-341 signature preimage — without ever checking that the claimed value/outpoint correspond to a real on-chain output or even to the `offset`/`script_pubkey` pairing. The only consistency check performed later (`SignableTransaction::multisig`) binds `offset` to `script_pubkey`, but never binds `value` or `outpoint` to anything verifiable.

### Finding Description
`ReceivedOutput` is normally produced by `Scanner::scan_transaction`, which derives `output` and `outpoint` from a real chain transaction [1](#0-0) . However, the struct is also constructible purely from attacker bytes via `ReceivedOutput::read`, which decodes `offset`, a `TxOut` (containing the value), and an `OutPoint` with no cross-validation [2](#0-1) .

`SignableTransaction::new` consumes `Vec<ReceivedOutput>` and computes `input_sat` directly from the claimed `input.output.value` [3](#0-2) , uses it for the `NotEnoughFunds` check [4](#0-3) , sizes the change output from it [5](#0-4) , and embeds the claimed `TxOut`s into `prevouts` [6](#0-5) . Signing then commits to those claimed prevouts via `Prevouts::All` [7](#0-6) .

The sole integrity check is that the key derived from `keys.offset(offset)` matches `prevouts[i].script_pubkey` [8](#0-7)  — analogous to Dinari checking that the order exists and `fillAmount <= remainingOrder`, while leaving the value-bearing field (`receivedAmount` there, `TxOut.value`/`OutPoint` here) completely unconstrained.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged party who can feed bytes into `ReceivedOutput::read` can fabricate a `ReceivedOutput` claiming an arbitrary `value` at an arbitrary `outpoint`, as long as the `script_pubkey` corresponds to a registered offset. Two concrete consequences, paralleling Dinari's "funds accounted at a wrong received amount":

1. **Funds reported received that are not spendable**: a fabricated `outpoint`/inflated `value` is recorded as a spendable output. Any signing attempt over it produces signatures that are invalid on-chain, because BIP-341 commits to every prevout's amount — the claimed output cannot ever be spent as reported.
2. **Consistent-input DoS / value accounting corruption**: because `Prevouts::All` commits to all claimed prevouts, one forged `ReceivedOutput` mixed with real ones invalidates every input's signature, blocking spends of legitimate outputs included in the same transaction. Additionally, an understated `value` shrinks the computed change (or eliminates it via the `value >= DUST` gate at `send.rs:229`), so accounting derived from `fee()`/`needed_fee()` reflects amounts that were never real.

This is the same shape as Dinari: a value field supplied alongside otherwise-valid order data is recorded into state (`_orders[orderId].received`) and used for token accounting, with no check against what actually occurred.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability is bounded by two factors: (a) the attacker must control bytes reaching `ReceivedOutput::read` (a documented untrusted-bytes entry point), and (b) the `offset`-to-`script_pubkey` binding in `multisig()` prevents attaching forged values to arbitrary foreign outputs — the fabricated `script_pubkey` must correspond to a watched key. Within those constraints, no secret, privilege, or collusion is needed: the attacker only supplies serialization bytes. Since value mismatches invalidate signatures rather than redirect funds, direct theft is not achievable through this path alone; the realistic impact is reporting unspendable funds and stalling spends of real co-spent outputs — consistent with a Medium-severity accounting-integrity issue.

### Recommendation
- Do not expose a trust boundary where `ReceivedOutput::read` output flows into `SignableTransaction::new` without re-validating the `outpoint`/`TxOut` against the chain (e.g., fetch the prevout and compare `value` and `script_pubkey`), or mark the deserialized value as unverified.
- In `SignableTransaction::multisig`/`new`, additionally verify the claimed `outpoint` resolves to a prevout equal to `self.prevouts[i]` before signing — analogous to Dinari's recommended `receivedAmount` range check.
- At minimum, sanity-bound `output.value` (e.g., ≤ 21M BTC supply) and reject `read`-constructed outputs from untrusted sources in favor of `Scanner`-produced ones.
