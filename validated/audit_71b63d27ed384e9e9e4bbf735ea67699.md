### Title
Unbacked `rewardAmount` accrual in `ReferralStorage.trigger()` allows insolvency and permanent freezing of other referrers' rewards - ([File: rewards/ReferralStorage.sol])

### Finding Description
`trigger()` is callable only by `masterMagpie` [1](#0-0) , and on every call it purely increments `refererInfo.rewardAmount` and `refereeInfo.rewardAmount` by amounts derived from `_amount` with no check against `MGP.balanceOf(address(this))` [2](#0-1) . `claimReward()` similarly performs no solvency check — it only verifies the caller's own `rewardAmount != 0` and then calls `MGP.safeTransfer` [3](#0-2) . There is no global invariant anywhere in the contract enforcing that `sum(userInfos[*].rewardAmount) <= MGP.balanceOf(address(this))`.

Because `trigger()` is invoked repeatedly for every referee claim routed through `masterMagpie` (e.g., via `MasterMagpie.multiclaim`), and each call only requires that the referee has previously called `useCode()` to link to a referrer and later trigger any reward-bearing claim, an attacker who creates many Sybil referee wallets, links them all to their own referral code, and drives minimal claim activity through each wallet can cause many `trigger()` calls that keep incrementing their own `refererInfo.rewardAmount`. Since `ReferralStorage`'s MGP balance is a fixed pool funded manually by the owner/admin (there is no minting or top-up logic visible in this contract), the sum of credited `rewardAmount` across all users can grow without bound relative to the actual token balance held by the contract.

Once the attacker calls `claimReward()` first and drains the available MGP balance, any other legitimate referrer whose `rewardAmount` was credited earlier will have their subsequent `claimReward()` call revert inside `MGP.safeTransfer` due to insufficient contract balance, since ERC20 transfers revert on insufficient balance and `claimReward()` has no fallback, partial-payment, or balance-check-before-accrual logic.

No existing modifier (`_onlyMasterMagpie`, `nonReentrant`, `whenNotPaused`) prevents this, because the root cause is a missing accounting invariant tying credited `rewardAmount` to actual backing, not an access-control or reentrancy issue. I was not able to fully verify, within the scope of this audit, how `_amount` is determined by `MasterMagpie`/`multiclaim` before it reaches `trigger()` — specifically whether `_amount` is strictly bounded by real reward tokens the protocol distributes elsewhere (which could limit but not eliminate the Sybil amplification, since many small dust-stake claims can still each generate nonzero `trigger()` credits) or whether it is more directly attacker-influenceable. This should be confirmed by examining `rewards/MasterMagpie.sol`'s claim/multiclaim flow, which was not fully retrievable in this session.

### Impact Explanation
This matches "theft or permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" — legitimate referrers' already-accrued `rewardAmount` becomes unbacked and their `claimReward()` reverts indefinitely until an admin manually tops up the MGP balance, which is an out-of-protocol, non-guaranteed remediation. The attacker effectively gets first-claim priority over a shared, under-collateralized pool. [3](#0-2) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Feasibility depends on (a) the cost of creating N Sybil wallets and driving minimal dust-stake claim activity through `MasterMagpie.multiclaim` for each, and (b) how large `_amount` can be made per call, which is determined in `MasterMagpie`'s claim logic — a part of the flow I could not fully verify with the available tool budget. If `_amount` scales with real distributed rewards proportional to genuine stake, the attack requires proportionally large capital/activity to meaningfully outpace the funded MGP balance; if it does not scale down for dust stakes, the attack becomes cheap and highly repeatable.

### Recommendation
Enforce a solvency invariant before crediting or paying rewards: either (1) require `trigger()` to check that pending liabilities plus new credit do not exceed `MGP.balanceOf(address(this))` (or a tracked `totalCommitted` accounting variable) and revert/queue otherwise, or (2) fund `ReferralStorage` atomically/pro-rata from the same source that funds the `_amount` passed into `trigger()`, so credited rewards are always backed 1:1 at accrual time rather than relying on periodic manual top-ups.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test outline:
1. Deploy `ReferralStorage`, set `masterMagpie` to a test-controlled address, register `codeA` for `referrerA` and `codeB` for `referrerB`.
2. Fund `ReferralStorage` with a small fixed MGP balance (e.g., 100 MGP).
3. From the `masterMagpie` address, loop N times: create referee wallet, `useCode(codeA)`, then call `trigger(refereeN, amount)` with `amount` chosen so cumulative `refererInfo.rewardAmount` for `referrerA` exceeds 100 MGP.
4. Also call `trigger()` once for `referrerB` crediting some smaller `rewardAmount`.
5. Assert `userInfos[referrerA].rewardAmount + userInfos[referrerB].rewardAmount > MGP.balanceOf(address(referralStorage))`.
6. Have `referrerA.claimReward()` succeed and drain the balance.
7. Assert `referrerB.claimReward()` reverts (insufficient balance), proving legitimate referrer's credited-but-unbacked reward is frozen.
