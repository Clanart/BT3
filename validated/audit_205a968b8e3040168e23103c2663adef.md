### Title
Attacker can DoS their own liquidation by adding trivial collateral in an asset whose price resolution reverts, panicking the atomic notional-evaluation across the whole position - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`liquidate` computes `total-collateral-usd`/`total-debt-usd` for a borrower's *entire* position mask in one atomic step via `get-assets` → `price-multi-resolve`. Because `get-assets` uses `unwrap-panic` on the multi-price result, a resolution failure (stale timestamp, low Pyth confidence, or zero price) for **any single asset in the mask** aborts the whole transaction — including liquidation of an otherwise unhealthy, unrelated collateral/debt pair. A borrower can deposit a trivial amount of a low-liquidity/rarely-updated asset as collateral, adding its bit to their position mask, and thereby hold their liquidation hostage to that one feed's staleness/confidence gate whenever it fails to refresh in time.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` builds the notional valuation for the borrower's full enabled mask: [1](#0-0) 

`get-assets` resolves prices for every asset bit set in that mask in a single batched call and **panics** if resolution of any one of them fails: [2](#0-1) 

`price-multi-resolve` only returns an `Err` (via `asserts!`) when any per-asset `price-resolve` fails — it does not skip/zero out the failing asset: [3](#0-2) 

`price-resolve` itself enforces the staleness/confidence gate that can fail per-asset (`oracle-timestamp-fresh`, `oracle-price-legal`, and `check-confidence` inside `resolve-pyth`): [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Since `get-assets`'s `unwrap-panic` propagates any single-asset gate failure into a full transaction abort, the *entire* `liquidate` call reverts even though the failing asset may be an unrelated, near-zero-value position the borrower deliberately opened. This is the same shape as the Moonwell finding: a user manipulates a low-activity/rarely-touched market they've entered so that a per-market computation breaks, and that break propagates into the shared, atomic health/liquidity calculation used by the liquidation path for the whole account, blocking liquidators from acting on genuinely unhealthy collateral.

### Impact Explanation
A borrower with a large, liquidatable position in one market (e.g., sBTC/USDC) can add one unit of a thinly-traded or DIA-fed asset as collateral. Whenever that asset's feed goes stale or its confidence ratio spikes beyond `max-confidence-ratio`, every call to `get-assets` for that borrower's mask — including the liquidator's `liquidate` call — reverts. This causes temporary freezing of the liquidation mechanism for that account: liquidators cannot seize collateral or repay debt while the gate is tripped, letting bad debt accrue and increasing insolvency risk for the vaults exposed to that borrower.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires the borrower to (a) already be near/at the liquidation threshold, and (b) hold a small deposit in an asset whose oracle staleness/confidence window is easy to trip (thinly-traded token, DIA feed with sparse updates). No special privileges or third-party misbehavior are required — this is purely the borrower choosing which assets to enter into their mask and timing liquidation attempts to coincide with a stale/low-confidence tick on that feed.

### Recommendation
Do not let a single asset's price-resolution failure abort valuation for the entire position, especially during liquidation. Options:
- In `get-assets`/`price-multi-resolve`, degrade gracefully per-asset (e.g., exclude assets with 0 balance from the resolved set, or treat a stale/low-confidence asset as contributing 0 collateral/max debt rather than reverting the whole call) when computing values used for liquidation eligibility.
- Alternatively, allow `liquidate` to proceed using the last-known-good/cached price for the failing asset instead of `unwrap-panic`, so liquidation of the healthy/target collateral is not blocked by an unrelated feed.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower supplies sBTC and borrows USDC up to just under `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`.
2. Borrower deposits a minimal amount of a thinly-traded asset (e.g., USDH via DIA, or any asset with sparse feed updates) as additional collateral, setting its bit in their position mask.
3. Price of sBTC/USDC moves such that the position becomes liquidatable (`current-ltv >= ltv-liq-partial`).
4. A liquidator calls `liquidate`; internally `get-assets(mask)` resolves prices for the full mask including the thinly-traded asset.
5. If that asset's Pyth/DIA feed is stale (`oracle-timestamp-fresh` fails) or its confidence ratio is too wide (`check-confidence` fails in `resolve-pyth`), `price-multi-resolve` returns `Err`, and `get-assets`'s `unwrap-panic` aborts the entire `liquidate` transaction.
6. The borrower's clearly unhealthy sBTC/USDC position cannot be liquidated until the thinly-traded feed refreshes within tolerance — which the borrower can effectively delay by choosing an asset with a long/rare update cadence.

(define-private (price-resolve
  (data { type: (buff 1), ident: (buff 32), callcode: (optional (buff 1)), max-staleness: uint }))
  (let ((type (get type data))
        (ident (get ident data))
        (key { type: type, ident: ident })
        (resolution (try! (resolve-price-feed type ident)))
        (price (get value resolution))
        (callcode (get callcode data))
        (final-price (try! (resolve-callcode price callcode)))
        (last-update-time (oracle-last-update key))
        (timestamp (get timestamp resolution))
        (max-staleness (get max-staleness data)))

    ;; validate price and timestamp using max-staleness from oracle data
    (asserts! (and (oracle-price-legal final-price) (oracle-timestamp-fresh timestamp last-update-time max-staleness))
              ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT)

    ;; update timestamp if newer
    (if (> timestamp last-update-time)
        (map-set last-update key timestamp)
        false)

    (ok final-price)))

(define-private (price-multi-resolve
  (data (list 64 { type: (buff 1), ident: (buff 32), callcode: (optional (buff 1)), max-staleness: uint }))
  (aids (list 64 uint)))
  (let ((init { output: (list), valid: true, aids: aids, idx: u0 })
        (response (fold iter-price-multi data init)))
    (asserts! (get valid response) ERR-ORACLE-MULTI)
    (ok (get output response))))
