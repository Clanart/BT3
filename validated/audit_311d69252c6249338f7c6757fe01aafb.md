**Analysis:**

The compute charges in `SyscallLogData::rust` are:
1. `syscall_base_cost` once [1](#0-0) 
2. `syscall_base_cost.saturating_mul(untranslated_fields.len())` — a per-field flat charge [2](#0-1) 
3. Sum of all field byte lengths, charged 1 CU per byte [3](#0-2) 

The downstream work in `stable_log::program_data` is `data.iter().map(|v| BASE64_STANDARD.encode(v)).join(" ")`, which is O(1) per-field overhead (allocation + base64 call + join separator) plus O(byte length) encoding work per field [4](#0-3) . That is exactly `O(N) + O(total_bytes)`, which is linearly dominated by the charged `syscall_base_cost * N + total_bytes` term — there is no multiplicative (e.g. `O(N * total_bytes)` or `O(N^2)`) blow-up from splitting a fixed total byte count into more, smaller fields. Each additional field costs one more `syscall_base_cost` unit and gets one more O(1) encode/join operation; the per-field encode/alloc/format overhead is far cheaper in wall-clock terms than the CU already charged per field (`syscall_base_cost` is a generous flat per-syscall-like charge, not a byte-proportional one), so the pricing over-charges rather than under-charges the "many tiny fields" pattern.

Additionally, the initial `translate_slice::<VmSlice<u8>>` call that reads the field-descriptor array validates the whole `addr..addr+len*size_of::<VmSlice<u8>>()` span with a single bounds/region-map lookup (not a per-element loop) [5](#0-4) , so it contributes no hidden O(N) or worse cost prior to the `cost = syscall_base_cost * N` charge being applied.

Finally, `LogCollector` truncates recorded log messages at `LOG_MESSAGES_BYTES_LIMIT` (10,000 bytes) [6](#0-5) , but even ignoring that (since the full `format!`/encode happens before truncation via `ic_logger_msg!` [7](#0-6) ), the field count `N` an attacker can afford is bounded by the transaction's compute budget divided by `syscall_base_cost`, keeping both charged CU and actual wall-clock work in the same linear regime.

No superlinear/multiplicative amplification exists; the per-field flat charge already correctly prices the "high field-count, low per-field-length" pattern described in the question.
