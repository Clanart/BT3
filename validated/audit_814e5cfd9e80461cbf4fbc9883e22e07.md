### Title
PedPoP DKG accepts identity/small-order encryption keys, letting a malicious participant turn their inbound secret-share ciphertexts into publicly decryptable messages and expose the aggregate group secret share at their index - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
The PedPoP DKG encrypts each participant's secret share via an ECDH between a fresh per-message key and the recipient's registered `enc_key`. `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` / `Decryption::register` accept any canonically-encoded point — no identity rejection and no proof-of-possession on the long-term encryption key — so a participant can register `enc_key = identity` (or, for Ed25519 via `dalek-ff-group`, a low-order/torsion point). Every honest sender then computes `ecdh(key, identity) = identity`, producing a ChaCha20 keystream derivable by anyone who knows the public `context`. All shares addressed to that participant decrypt publicly, exposing the aggregate secret share at the malicious participant's index to any observer.

### Finding Description
Analogue of CVE-2026-54422 ("a malicious deployed artifact extracts the credentials used to fetch it"): here, a malicious DKG participant supplies a crafted public input — their `EncryptionKeyMessage.enc_key` — which causes honest parties to hand over secret material (their shares of the group key) encrypted under a publicly computable key.

- `Ciphersuite::read_G` (crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-101) validates only that a point decodes and re-encodes canonically. Unlike `Curve::read_G` (crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131), it does not reject the identity.
- `Decryption::register` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:351-362) inserts `msg.enc_key` into `enc_keys` with no validity check and — critically — no proof-of-possession of the corresponding secret key.
- `encrypt` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:154) computes `ecdh(key, to)` where `to` is this registered key; `ecdh` (line 95-97) is a bare scalar multiplication with no identity check on the result.
- `cipher` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:101-133) derives the ChaCha20 key solely from `transcript(context) || shared_key.to_bytes()` with a static IV — so `shared_key = identity` (a known, serialized point) yields a keystream computable by any third party who knows `context`, which is public (derived from session/network/attempt strings, processor/src/key_gen.rs:184-197).
- `SecretShareMachine::verify_r1` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:315) calls `self.encryption.register(l, msg)` on every received commitment message before share distribution; nothing between `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` and `encrypt` rejects an identity `enc_key`.

On Ristretto, identity is a canonical `GroupEncoding`, so it passes `read_G`. On Ed25519 (`dalek-ff-group`), decompression additionally accepts small-order points, so any of the 8 torsion points also works, producing a keystream brute-forceable over 8 candidates even where cofactor multiplication wouldn't fully collapse it.

### Impact Explanation
Each honest sender `i` encrypts `f_i(V)` — their polynomial evaluated at the malicious participant `V`'s index — under `KDF(context || identity)`. Since the DKG's aggregate secret share at index `V` is `Σ_i f_i(V)`, every observer of the public/authenticated channel who collects all `n-1` ciphertexts addressed to `V` recovers `V`'s aggregate group secret share. Combined with the attacker's own protocol share (attacker is participant `V` and also decrypts normally), in a `t=2` configuration this immediately yields threshold control of the generated group key; in general it gratuitously hands out one full valid share of the group secret to non-participants, degrading the threshold by one against any eavesdropper and fully breaking key confidentiality for small `t`. This is key-share recovery reachable purely via a participant-supplied public input (`EncryptionKeyMessage`), matching the credential-extraction class of the CVE.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only that the malicious participant broadcast an `EncryptionKeyMessage` whose `enc_key` is the identity encoding — a fully "valid" message per every code path (`read_G` canonicality is the only gate). It is deterministic, requires no races or cooperation, leaks the share to *all* observers (not just the attacker), and is undetectable at protocol time: decryption succeeds normally for the attacker, shares verify, and no blame is raised. The only mitigation is external: an implementation noticing the anomaly after the fact.

### Recommendation
- Validate `enc_key` on registration in `Decryption::register` / `EncryptionKeyMessage::read`: reject identity and require a Schnorr proof-of-possession of the encryption secret key (same pattern already used for per-message `key` via `pop`).
- For non-prime-order groups (Ed25519), additionally multiply `ecdh` output by the cofactor or reject non-torsion-free keys.
- Defense in depth: in `ecdh`, reject/error when the shared result is identity.

### Proof of Concept
1. Malicious participant `V` in a `t-of-n` PedPoP session runs `KeyGenMachine::generate_coefficients` normally, but constructs its `EncryptionKeyMessage` with `enc_key = C::G::identity()` (canonical encoding passes `read_G`).
2. Every honest participant `i` calls `SecretShareMachine::verify_r1`, which registers `V`'s identity key without PoP, then `encryption.encrypt(rng, V, share_i_to_V)`, computing `ecdh(k_i, identity) = identity` and `cipher(context, identity)` — a keystream `Ks = ChaCha20(KDF("DKG Encryption v0.2" || context || identity.to_bytes()), "DKG IV v0.2\0")` computable by anyone.
3. `V`'s inbound ciphertexts `share_i_to_V ⊕ Ks` are broadcast on the authenticated channel. Any observer computes `Ks`, XORs, and obtains every `f_i(V)` in the clear.
4. The observer sums the decrypted shares: `secret_share_V = Σ_i f_i(V)`, recovering the aggregate group secret share at index `V` — a credential honest participants only intended for `V`'s private decryption. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6)
