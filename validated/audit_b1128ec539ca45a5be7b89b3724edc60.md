## Analysis

The Blend bug's core invariant: **a fixed claim amount is computed against a shared pool at time T₀, but the pool can be drained by an independent, permissionless action before the claim executes, and the accounting for that claim has already been "locked in" — leaving it permanently unfillable / lost, with no cap re-check at settlement time.**

The closest local analog in Hyperbridge is the relayer fee withdrawal flow, where a relayer's `available_amount` is captured and the pallet's ledger is zeroed *before* the cross-chain payout message is ever delivered against the destination chain's shared fee-token balance — a balance that any number of other relayers (or governance) can independently drain in the interim.

### Title
Relayer fee withdrawal zeroes the ledger before the shared destination-chain balance is reserved, permanently stranding fee claims that race a concurrent drain - (File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs)

### Summary
`Pallet::withdraw` in the relayer pallet reads a relayer's `available_amount` from `Fees`, immediately zeroes that entry, and dispatches a fixed-amount ISMP POST instructing the destination `EvmHost`/`HostManager` to pay it out later. Neither this pallet, nor `EvmHost::withdraw`, nor `pallet-host-executive::withdraw` (the governance withdrawal path that drains the *same* pool) reserve or cap the amount against the actual balance available at settlement time. Multiple relayers (and governance) draw from one shared, unreserved fee-token balance on the destination host contract, so a legitimate, unprivileged relayer can permanently strand another relayer's already-zeroed claim simply by withdrawing first.

### Finding Description
`Pallet::withdraw` performs, in order:
1. `available_amount = Fees::<T>::get(dest_chain, address)` [1](#0-0) 
2. Increments the relayer's nonce [2](#0-1) 
3. Builds a fixed-amount `WithdrawalParams`/`WithdrawalRequest` body and dispatches a non-timing-out (`timeout: 0`) ISMP POST to the destination `host_manager` [3](#0-2) 
4. **Zeroes `Fees` immediately**, before the message is ever delivered: `Fees::<T>::insert(withdrawal_data.dest_chain, address.clone(), U256::zero());` [4](#0-3) 

On the EVM side, delivery of that fixed-amount message ultimately calls `EvmHost::withdraw`, which unconditionally attempts `IERC20(params.token).safeTransfer(params.beneficiary, params.amount)` or a native `.call{value: params.amount}("")` with **no cap against the host's current balance** — it just reverts if insufficient: [5](#0-4) . This is confirmed by the test asserting a bare, uncapped revert on insufficient balance: [6](#0-5) .

Crucially, this same `EvmHost` fee-token balance is the **shared pool** backing:
- Every other relayer's fee withdrawal on that chain (same `withdraw` flow, same destination `host_manager`).
- Governance's own protocol-fee withdrawal via `pallet-host-executive::withdraw`, which dispatches to the identical `host_manager` address with an independently-chosen amount and no coordination with relayer claims: [7](#0-6) .

Because `Fees` is zeroed at step 4 *before* the payout is confirmed, and the destination-side transfer has no cap/reservation logic, a relayer's claim is fixed and "spent" from the Hyperbridge-side ledger at dispatch time, but its execution depends entirely on whichever withdrawal message (this relayer's, another relayer's, or governance's) lands first against the shared balance. The relayer client's own auto-withdraw documentation confirms failed withdrawal messages are simply retried unmodified rather than recomputed: "Any failed withdrawal attempts will be retried each time the withdrawal task is triggered" [8](#0-7) , and pending withdrawals are persisted and redelivered as-is: [9](#0-8) .

This exactly mirrors the Blend bug's broken invariant: a claim's size is fixed and its supporting on-chain accounting entry is already consumed/removed, while the shared pool it depends on can be independently drained by any other unprivileged (or privileged, in the governance case) actor's legitimate withdrawal, with no capping mechanism at settlement — leaving the original claim permanently stuck until the pool coincidentally refills to the exact required level.

### Impact Explanation
A relayer whose `withdraw()` call succeeds (Fees zeroed, message dispatched) has no guarantee of ever receiving their fee tokens: if the shared `EvmHost` balance for that destination chain is drained below `available_amount` by any other relayer's concurrent withdrawal or by governance's `pallet-host-executive::withdraw` before this specific message is delivered, the payout reverts on delivery and the relayer's on-chain entitlement (`Fees`) has already been zeroed with no recomputation path. This is a direct loss/lock of relayer reward funds — the exact "reward claims ... must move exactly once and only to the rightful beneficiary and amount" invariant is broken, since the amount instead moves to nobody while the ledger records it as already paid.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no malicious peer, relayer, prover, or admin — only ordinary concurrent, legitimate relayer withdrawal activity on a chain whose accumulated fee-token balance is thin relative to outstanding claims, which is a normal operating condition (the same class of "low backstop supply" likelihood argument Blend's auditor made). Any chain with multiple active relayers competing for the same destination's collected fees, or any governance sweep timed near relayer withdrawals, can trigger it.

### Recommendation
Cap the payout amount at settlement time against the destination host's live balance (mirroring Blend's `pool_backstop_data.tokens.min(lot_amount)` mitigation direction, extended further to *reserve* rather than just cap), or defer zeroing `Fees` until delivery is confirmed successful, and/or introduce a reservation/escrow mechanism on the destination host so concurrent withdrawal messages cannot race against the same unreserved balance.

### Proof of Concept
1. Relayer A and Relayer B each accumulate fees on Hyperbridge for the same `dest_chain`, together exceeding the `EvmHost`'s current fee-token balance on that chain.
2. A calls `Pallet::withdraw` — `Fees[A]` is read as `amount_A`, zeroed, and a fixed-amount POST to `host_manager` is dispatched (`modules/pallets/relayer/src/withdrawal.rs:116-184`).
3. B calls `Pallet::withdraw` similarly for `amount_B`, zeroed and dispatched.
4. B's message lands first and drains the `EvmHost` balance via `EvmHost::withdraw` (`evm/src/core/EvmHost.sol:651-660`).
5. A's message is delivered next; `IERC20(token).safeTransfer` reverts (insufficient balance) as shown by `test_host_manager_insufficient_balance` (`evm/tests/rust/src/tests/host_manager.rs:143-172`).
6. `Fees[A]` is already `0` on Hyperbridge; A's tesseract relayer will keep retrying the identical failed pending withdrawal (per `tesseract/messaging/messaging/src/fees.rs:100-121` and the relayer docs) but has no on-chain claim to fall back on if the host balance never again reaches `amount_A` for A specifically — the funds are effectively lost/stuck.

```text
### Automatic accumulation and withdrawals

The relayer also runs background tasks for automatic fee accumulation and withdrawals. Whenever a batch of messages is successfully delivered, the fee accumulation task receives the delivery receipts and starts the process of accumulating the fees on hyperbridge. This process happens concurrently for all successfully delivered message batches. For redundancy, the delivery receipts are stored in the database prior to accumulation so they can be retried manually if any error is encountered.

<br />
Withdrawing fees from hyperbridge is triggered at fixed intervals based on the
configured `withdrawal_frequency` and `minimum_withdrawal_amount`. Feel free to
the adjust these values as desired. The task will only make a withdrawal attempt
if your balance on hyperbridge is greater than or equal to the configured
`minimum_withdrawal_amount`. Any failed withdrawal attempts will be retried each
time the withdrawal task is triggered. The manual `withdraw` subcommand
described above can be used as a fallback when errors are encountered by the
automated task.
```
