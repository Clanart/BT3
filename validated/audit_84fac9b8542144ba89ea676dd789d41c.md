### Title
Missing proof of possession on DKG encryption keys lets a participant co-opt a victim's key and leak an honest sender's secret share via blame - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to the NodeBB report (a missing origin/session check causes private messages to leak to third parties), `EncryptionKeyMessage.enc_key` is registered without any proof of possession. A participant can claim another participant's encryption public key as their own, and the resulting blame protocol reveals the victim's ECDH key, decrypting an honest sender's secret share to all observers.

### Finding Description
Each participant broadcasts `EncryptionKeyMessage { msg: Commitments, enc_key }`. `enc_key` is a bare point with no PoP; `Decryption::register` just stores it (`encryption.rs:351-362`), and `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` accepts any `C::read_G` point (`encryption.rs:56-59`). The per-message `EncryptedMessage.pop` only proves knowledge of the per-message key `msg.key` (`encryption.rs:302-324`), not of `enc_key`.

The spec (`spec/cryptography/Distributed Key Generation.md`, lines 28-35) claims the co-option attack is resolved by the per-message PoP, but that PoP covers `msg.key` — the sender's ephemeral key — not the registered `enc_key`. So claiming someone else's `enc_key` still works.

Attack:
1. Eve registers `enc_key = Alice.enc_pub_key` (Eve doesn't know the scalar).
2. Bob encrypts his secret share for Eve: `cipher(ecdh(bob_msg_key, Alice.enc_pub))` (`encryption.rs:460-467`, `encrypt` at 135-168).
3. Eve cannot decrypt, so she triggers blame. Bob runs `decrypt`/`blame_internal` and publishes `EncryptionKeyProof.key = ecdh(msg.key_scalar, Alice.enc_pub)` (`encryption.rs:487-499`, `blame_internal` at 575-609).
4. That published key is exactly the shared key needed to decrypt Bob's ciphertext — `decrypt_with_proof` uses it via `cipher(self.context, &proof.key)` (`encryption.rs:392`). Everyone can now recover Bob's secret share for participant index `i_Eve`, a valid point on Bob's private polynomial.

### Impact Explanation
Bob's secret share for Eve's index leaks publicly — unintended leakage of private DKG material to third parties caused purely by a malformed registration. With `t` such leaked shares for distinct indexes (the attacker can repeat the co-option across participant slots or sessions), Bob's entire polynomial — and thus his contribution to every key share — is recoverable. Even a single leaked share undermines the privacy guarantees of the DKG and the blame protocol, which is designed to reveal only the accused party's data.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any DKG participant can submit an `EncryptionKeyMessage` containing an arbitrary `enc_key` via `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` — no secret knowledge is needed since victim keys are public. Eve then simply claims her share was invalid, which honest Bob services automatically. The only requirement is participation in the protocol, matching the "unprivileged party with public inputs" profile.

### Recommendation
Bind each registered encryption key to its owner with a proof of possession (e.g., a `SchnorrSignature` over `enc_key` challenge-bound to `context` and the participant ID, verified during `verify_r1`), or transcript `enc_key` into the per-participant PoK challenge in `challenge::<C>` so a foreign key cannot be claimed.

### Proof of Concept
In `crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/tests.rs` style: run a 3-participant `KeyGenMachine` flow where participant 3's `EncryptionKeyMessage` is constructed as `EncryptionKeyMessage { msg: /* participant 3's own Commitments */, enc_key: participant_1.enc_pub_key }`. Participant 2's `generate_secret_shares` succeeds; participant 3 calls `blame`; `blame_internal` returns the published `EncryptionKeyProof.key`, and applying `cipher(context, key)` to participant 2's `EncryptedMessage` for participant 3 yields participant 2's secret share `polynomial_2(i_3)` — verify it against `share_verification_statements(Participant(3), commitments[2], share)` summing to identity.
