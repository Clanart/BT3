### Title
Reward-per-token retroactive attribution allows a 1-wei mWomSV locker to capture rewards queued while totalStaked()==0 - ([File: rewards/mWOMSVBaseRewarder.sol])

### Summary
`_provisionReward` (called from `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards`) accumulates any rewards sent while `totalStaked()==0` into `rewardInfo.queuedRewards`, and only folds that lump sum into `rewardPerTokenStored` on a later call to `_provisionReward` (or via forfeiture through `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer`), dividing by whatever `totalStaked()` is *at that later moment*. Because `rewardPerTokenStored` is a single global accumulator, whoever holds mWomSV balance at the moment of the fold-in captures the entire increase in proportion to their share of `totalStaked()`, regardless of whether they were staked while the reward accrued. An attacker who locks a minimal (e.g. 1 wei) amount of mWomSV between the reward-queuing transaction and the next `_provisionReward` call becomes the sole/majority staker and receives the whole queued reward.

### Finding Description
`totalStaked()` returns `IERC20(mWOMSV).totalSupply()` [1](#0-0) . In `_provisionReward`, when `totalStaked()==0` the incoming reward is only added to `rewardInfo.queuedRewards`; it is not distributed until a subsequent call sees `totalStaked() > 0`, at which point the *entire* accumulated `queuedRewards` (plus the new amount) is divided by the then-current `totalStaked()` and added to `rewardPerTokenStored`: [2](#0-1) 

Because `rewardPerTokenStored` is a flat, non-time-weighted accumulator, `_earned` credits any account based purely on `(rewardPerTokenStored - userRewardPerTokenPaid) * balance`, with no accounting for how long the balance was held before the fold-in: [3](#0-2) 

An attacker who calls `mWomSV.lock(1)` right after a manager's `queueNewRewards` call (while `totalStaked()==0`) becomes the first/only staker, with `userRewardPerTokenPaid == rewardPerTokenStored` at the time of locking (no update happens on lock itself, since `updateReward` is only invoked via `getReward`/`getRewards`, not on staking). `mWomSV.lock`/`lockFor` enforce no minimum amount [4](#0-3) . When the next `_provisionReward`-triggering call occurs (another `queueNewRewards`, `donateRewards`, or even a forfeiture-driven `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` from any user's `_sendReward`) with `totalStaked()==1`, the whole `queuedRewards` lump sum is folded into `rewardPerTokenStored` divided by `1`, making the attacker's tiny stake worth the entire reward. The attacker can then claim it via `getReward`/`getRewards` through MasterMagpie.

Neither `nonReentrant`, `whenNotPaused`, nor the `updateRewards`/`updateReward` modifiers prevent this: they only ensure per-user accounting is internally consistent, not that queued rewards are distributed fairly to the population that "earned" them while they were staking previously (there was none) or that will stake later at scale.

### Impact Explanation
This is a theft of yield: rewards intended to eventually be shared pro rata among the actual population of mWomSV lockers can be fully diverted to a single wei-sized locker who front-runs/opportunistically times a lock right after a large reward queue while `totalStaked()==0`. This matches the "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class. The magnitude scales with however much reward accumulated in `queuedRewards` while the pool had zero stake — potentially the entirety of a distribution round.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Precondition: `totalStaked()==0` (mWomSV total supply is zero) must occur, which is possible whenever all lockers fully unlock/exit or after contract bootstrap before any locker joins, and a manager calls `queueNewRewards` at that moment (a normal, non-malicious operational action).
- The attacker needs to observe this on-chain state (e.g., via mempool monitoring of the manager's `queueNewRewards` tx, or simply polling `totalStaked()==0`) and front-run/immediately follow with `mWomSV.lock(1)`.
- No minimum lock amount is enforced, and no special privilege is required — any EOA can call `mWomSV.lock`.
- Feasibility is highest during protocol bootstrap (before the pool has meaningful stake) or after a mass-unlock event; it is repeatable each time `totalStaked()` returns to zero and a subsequent reward is queued/donated.
- Capital required is negligible (1 wei of mWOM to lock, plus gas).

### Recommendation
Do not defer distribution of rewards queued while `totalStaked()==0` to be captured entirely by whoever is staked at the next unrelated `_provisionReward` call. Options:
1. Track a snapshot/checkpoint mechanism so `queuedRewards` accrued during zero-stake periods are distributed only to accounts staked as of the block when the fold-in occurs is inherently unfair — instead, require a minimum time delay or minimum total-stake threshold before folding in queued rewards, or stream `queuedRewards` linearly over time (`rewardRate`) rather than instantly crediting the full amount to the current supply.
2. Alternatively, refuse to fold `queuedRewards` into `rewardPerTokenStored` when `totalStaked()` is below some minimum bootstrap threshold, extending the "queued" state until a broader population has staked.
3. Consider transferring rewards queued during zero-supply periods to a treasury/redistribution mechanism instead of the reward-per-token accumulator to avoid single-staker capture.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `mWOMSVBaseRewarder`, `mWomSV`, and dependencies with a manager account.
2. Ensure `mWomSV.totalSupply() == 0` (either fresh deployment or after full unlock/withdraw by any prior lockers).
3. Manager calls `queueNewRewards(largeAmount, rewardToken)` — assert `rewards[rewardToken].queuedRewards == largeAmount` and `rewardPerTokenStored` unchanged.
4. Attacker (a fresh EOA with no other interaction) calls `mWomSV.lock(1)` (acquiring 1 wei of the underlying mWOM first, e.g., via `mWOM.deposit(1)`).
5. Trigger a second `_provisionReward` invocation with `totalStaked()==1` — e.g., manager calls `queueNewRewards(0, rewardToken)` or `donateRewards(0, rewardToken)` (or have any user's `getReward` trigger a forfeiture path calling `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer`).
6. Assert `rewardPerTokenStored` for `rewardToken` jumped by `largeAmount * 1e18 / 1`.
7. Attacker calls `getReward`/`getRewards` via MasterMagpie and asserts they receive (approximately) the full `largeAmount`, despite holding only 1 wei and having staked for a negligible time after the reward was queued.
8. Assert no other/legitimate future locker who stakes normal amounts afterward receives any share of `largeAmount`.

```text
    function _provisionReward(uint256 _amountReward, address _rewardToken) internal {
        IERC20Metadata(_rewardToken).safeTransferFrom(
            msg.sender,
            address(this),
            _amountReward
        );
        Reward storage rewardInfo = rewards[_rewardToken];
        rewardInfo.historicalRewards =
            rewardInfo.historicalRewards +
            _amountReward;

        if (totalStaked() == 0) {
            rewardInfo.queuedRewards += _amountReward;
        } else {
            if (rewardInfo.queuedRewards > 0) {
                _amountReward += rewardInfo.queuedRewards;
                rewardInfo.queuedRewards = 0;
            }
            rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored =
                rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored +
                (_amountReward * 10**mWOMSVDecimal) / totalStaked();
        }
        emit RewardAdded(_amountReward, _rewardToken);
    }
```
