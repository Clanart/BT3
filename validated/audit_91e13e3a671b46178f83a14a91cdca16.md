### Title
Reward misallocation via `totalStaked()==0` branch in `_provisionReward` allows theft of unclaimed bribe yield from prior stakers - ([File: rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol])

### Summary
`BaseRewardPoolV2._provisionReward` distributes rewards purely based on the stakers present at the exact moment `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` is called, with no time-weighted accrual. If `totalStaked()==0` at that instant, the whole reward is stashed in `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` and is fully credited via `rewardPerTokenStored` to whichever staker happens to be present the next time a provision event occurs — regardless of who was staked and earned the bribe during the intervening period. Because `BribeRewardPool.withdrawFor`/`stakeFor` are invoked synchronously and permissionlessly through `WombatBribeManager.unvote()`/`vote()`, and harvests can be triggered by anyone via `castVotes()`/`harvestSinglePool()`, an attacker can engineer the `totalStaked()==0` window and then re-enter as sole/majority staker to capture rewards earned by a prior staker.

### Finding Description
`_provisionReward` in `rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol` computes distribution strictly at call time: [1](#0-0) 
`rewardPerToken()` simply returns the stored value with no time-based interpolation, so all accounting for who "earns" a reward hinges entirely on who is staked at the exact block a `queueNewRewards` call lands: [2](#0-1) 

In `BribeRewardPool`, `totalStaked()` is simply `totalSupply`, and `stakeFor`/`withdrawFor` mutate it synchronously and are only gated by `onlyOperator` (the `WombatBribeManager`), not by any protected/privileged end-user role: [3](#0-2) 

`WombatBribeManager.unvote()` lets any user with vote weight immediately zero out their entire stake in a pool's `BribeRewardPool` via `withdrawFor`, and `vote()` lets them re-stake via `stakeFor` — both are public, unprivileged, callable at will: [4](#0-3) 

Harvest/reward provisioning is triggered through `WombatStaking.vote()` (called from `castVotes`/`harvestSinglePool`, both public with no access restriction beyond `msg.sender == bribeManager` internally, but reachable by anyone through the bribe manager's public entry points), which calls `IBaseRewardPool(_rewarders[i]).queueNewRewards(...)`: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Exploit flow:
1. Staker A holds the entire (or majority) vote/stake in a given LP's `BribeRewardPool` and has accrued bribe rights over a period.
2. A calls `unvote(lp)` right before a harvest lands, calling `withdrawFor` and driving `totalSupply` (i.e. `totalStaked()`) to `0`.
3. Attacker (or A themselves, or anyone) triggers `harvestSinglePool(lp)`/`castVotes()`, which forces `wombatStaking.vote()` → `queueNewRewards` → `_provisionReward` with `totalStaked()==0`, so the whole bribe amount for the period A was staked is pushed into `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` instead of being distributed via `rewardPerTokenStored` (A gets nothing for that period since their share was reset to zero right before accrual was recorded).
4. Attacker immediately calls `vote(lp, largeDelta)`, becoming the sole/majority staker with `stakeFor` (which runs `updateRewards`, freezing `userRewardPerTokenPaid` at the pre-queue value).
5. On the next `queueNewRewards` call (even a small, unrelated donation/bribe), `_provisionReward`'s `totalStaked() != 0` branch folds the entire previously queued amount into `rewardPerTokenStored`: `_amountReward += rewardInfo.queuedRewards`. Since the attacker is now the dominant staker, this entire folded amount (which was earned during A's staking period) is credited overwhelmingly to the attacker.

No existing modifier (`onlyOperator`, `updateRewards`, `onlyManager`) prevents this because they only gate *who* can call stake/withdraw/queue, not *when* relative to `totalStaked()` transitioning to/from zero. There is no time-weighted or checkpointed accrual to protect stakers who exit before a harvest lands.

### Impact Explanation
This is a theft of unclaimed yield: a legitimate staker who contributed voting power to earn a bribe reward for a full accrual period receives materially less than their pro-rata entitlement, while an attacker who was not staked during the earning period captures a disproportionate (up to ~100%) share once they re-enter as dominant staker before the next provisioning event. This matches Immunefi's "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires: (a) the target `BribeRewardPool` momentarily reaching `totalStaked()==0` — realistic for low-participation/niche LP pools with one or few voters, or via coordinated/self-timed `unvote()` immediately before a self-triggered `harvestSinglePool()`/`castVotes()` call; (b) the attacker holding sufficient vlMGP voting power to re-vote a large delta into the pool. Both `unvote`, `vote`, and `harvestSinglePool`/`castVotes` are public and unprivileged, and an attacker (or a colluding/self-acting single staker) can fully control the sequencing since harvest can be self-triggered rather than merely front-run. This is repeatable every time a pool empties out even momentarily.

### Recommendation
Replace the instantaneous, snapshot-based distribution model with a time-weighted accrual mechanism (e.g., track `lastUpdateTime` and accrue `rewardPerTokenStored` continuously based on elapsed time and `totalStaked()` at each checkpoint, Synthetix-style), or, at minimum, checkpoint/record accrued-but-undistributed rewards against the balances/participants that were staked when the reward was earned rather than deferring 100% of it to whoever is staked at the next provisioning call. Additionally, consider disallowing `withdrawFor`/`unvote` from fully draining `totalStaked()` to zero immediately before a pending harvest, or snapshotting entitlement at unvote time so an exiting staker still receives credit for rewards queued while they held zero balance but had contributed during the relevant period.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan against `BribeRewardPool`/`WombatBribeManager`:
1. Deploy `WombatBribeManager`, `WombatStaking`, and a `BribeRewardPool` for a mock LP pool with mocked `MasterWombat`/`Voter`/`Bribe` contracts.
2. Staker A votes `X` vlMGP into the pool (`vote([lp],[X])`), becoming sole staker (`totalStaked() == X`).
3. Simulate bribe accrual period, then have A call `unvote(lp)` right before harvest, driving `totalStaked()` to `0`.
4. Trigger harvest (`harvestSinglePool([lp])` or `castVotes()`), mocking the external bribe contract to pay out `R` reward tokens; assert `rewards[token].queuedRewards == R` and `rewardPerTokenStored` unchanged.
5. Attacker calls `vote([lp],[Y])` with `Y >> 0`, becoming sole staker.
6. Trigger a subsequent harvest with a small additional reward `r`; assert `_provisionReward` folds `queuedRewards` (`R`) into `rewardPerTokenStored` based on `totalStaked() == Y` (attacker's balance).
7. Assert `earned(attacker, token) ≈ (R + r)` while `earned(A, token) == 0`, proving A received none of the `R` reward despite having been the sole staker who "earned" it, and the attacker who staked only after the reward was already generated received the full amount.

    function _provisionReward(uint256 _amountReward, address _rewardToken) internal {
        IERC20(_rewardToken).safeTransferFrom(
            msg.sender,
            address(this),
            _amountReward
        );
        Reward storage rewardInfo = rewards[_rewardToken];
        rewardInfo.historicalRewards =
            rewardInfo.historicalRewards +
            _amountReward;

        if (totalStaked() == 0) {
            rewardInfo.queuedRewards += _amountReward;
        } else {
            if (rewardInfo.queuedRewards > 0) {
                _amountReward += rewardInfo.queuedRewards;
                rewardInfo.queuedRewards = 0;

                rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored +
                (_amountReward * 10**stakingTokenDecimals) /
                totalStaked();
        }
