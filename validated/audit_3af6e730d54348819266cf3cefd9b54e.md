Flash-stake reward sniping via instantaneous (non-time-weighted) `rewardPerTokenStored` update in `BaseRewardPool`/`BaseRewardPoolV2` - ([File: rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol], [File: rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol])

### Summary
`BaseRewardPool._provisionReward` / `BaseRewardPoolV2._provisionReward` credit newly queued rewards to `rewardPerTokenStored` instantly, in a single block, proportional to whatever `totalStaked()` is at that exact moment — there is no streaming/vesting period (e.g. Synthetix-style `rewardRate` over a `rewardsDuration`) and no minimum holding time. An unprivileged attacker who deposits into `MasterMagpie` immediately before a reward manager's `queueNewRewards()` call, then claims and withdraws immediately after, captures a full pro-rata share of that reward despite holding the position for zero economically meaningful time. The exact literal call order given in the question (`queueNewRewards → deposit → getReward → withdraw`) does **not** work, but the reverse order (`deposit → queueNewRewards → getReward → withdraw`) does.

### Finding Description
`_provisionReward` in both `rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol` (lines 297-320) and `rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol` (lines 290-314) increases `rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored` by `(_amountReward * scale) / totalStaked()` at the moment `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` is called [1](#0-0) . `totalStaked()` reads `IERC20(stakingToken).balanceOf(operator)` (i.e., MasterMagpie's current balance) at call time [2](#0-1) , and `balanceOf(account)` for reward accounting reads the user's live `stakingInfo` from `MasterMagpie` [3](#0-2) .

In `MasterMagpie._deposit`, `_harvestBaseRewarder` (which calls `rewarder.updateFor(account)`, snapshotting `userRewardPerTokenPaid` to the *current* `rewardPerTokenStored`) runs **before** `user.amount` is incremented [4](#0-3) . This means:

- If the attacker deposits **before** the manager calls `queueNewRewards`, their `userRewardPerTokenPaid` is snapshotted to the *pre-reward* value while their balance is already counted in `totalStaked()` when the reward is provisioned. When the manager's `queueNewRewards` call fires, `rewardPerTokenStored` jumps up based on a `totalStaked()` that already includes the attacker's flash deposit (diluting the per-token reward relative to what long-term stakers "should" get on a time-weighted basis, and pumping the flash lot into the distribution). The attacker can then call `getReward`/multiclaim and receive `balanceOf(attacker) * (rewardPerTokenStored_after - rewardPerTokenStored_before)` — a full share commensurate with their balance, despite holding it for less than one block.
- If instead the attacker deposits **after** `queueNewRewards` (the literal order in the question), `updateFor` snapshots `userRewardPerTokenPaid` to the value that *already includes* the just-queued reward, so `earned()` for that reward increment is zero. This specific literal sequence therefore does **not** produce a profit.

No mechanism (cooldown, minimum stake duration, linear reward streaming, or snapshot-based fairness) exists anywhere in `MasterMagpie.sol`, `BaseRewardPool.sol`, or `BaseRewardPoolV2.sol` to prevent the deposit-before-queue variant of this attack — `nonReentrant`/`whenNotPaused` guard against reentrancy/pause states but do nothing to prevent atomic same-block deposit→(reward queued)→claim→withdraw sequences.

### Impact Explanation
This is a theft of unclaimed yield from long-term stakers: an attacker with no time-weighted exposure to the pool captures a share of freshly queued rewards that should have accrued only to stakers who held their position for the relevant period. Repeated across every `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` call (which happens routinely as part of normal reward-manager operations), this systematically transfers yield away from genuine long-term LPs to opportunistic flash-stakers, directly matching the "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
- The attacker needs only capital to acquire/borrow the staking token (potentially via flash loan, since no time-based exposure is required) and gas to front-run the manager's `queueNewRewards` transaction (mempool-observable, permissionless to react to).
- No privileged role is required by the attacker — `queueNewRewards` being called by a legitimate reward manager is normal, expected protocol operation, not malicious admin behavior.
- The exploit is fully repeatable every time new rewards are queued, and capital is only needed transiently within one transaction/block (self-liquidating via `withdraw`), making it highly capital-efficient and scalable.

### Recommendation
Replace instantaneous reward crediting with a time-weighted streaming model (Synthetix-style `rewardRate` distributed linearly over a `rewardsDuration`, updated via a `lastUpdateTime`/`periodFinish` mechanism), so that `rewardPerToken()` only accrues gradually as time passes rather than jumping fully at the moment of `queueNewRewards`. Alternatively/additionally, introduce a minimum staking duration or a deposit-then-reward-eligibility delay (e.g., rewards only accrue to balances present before the reward-queuing block, or apply a per-block/epoch snapshot for reward eligibility) to eliminate the same-block deposit-before-queue exploit.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, register a pool with a staking token and a `BaseRewardPool`/`BaseRewardPoolV2` rewarder; register a reward manager.
2. Have `userA` (long-term staker) deposit `1000` staking tokens and let some time pass (not required for the exploit, but establishes baseline).
3. Have `attacker` (holding staking tokens, e.g. via flash loan) call `MasterMagpie.deposit(stakingToken, 1000)` — matching `userA`'s balance — in the same block.
4. Reward manager calls `rewarder.queueNewRewards(rewardAmount, rewardToken)` in the same block.
5. Attacker immediately calls `MasterMagpie` multiclaim/`getReward` for the staking token, then `MasterMagpie.withdraw(stakingToken, 1000)` — all in the same transaction/block.
6. Assert: `attacker` received `rewardAmount / 2` (pro-rata to their instantaneous 50% share of `totalStaked()`), i.e., a nonzero reward despite zero blocks/time elapsed holding the position; assert `userA`'s claimable reward is correspondingly diluted to `rewardAmount / 2` instead of the full `rewardAmount` they would have received absent the attacker's flash deposit.
7. Contrast with the literal order in the question (`queueNewRewards → deposit → getReward → withdraw`): assert attacker's claimed reward is `0`, confirming that ordering is not exploitable but the reverse (deposit-before-queue) is.
