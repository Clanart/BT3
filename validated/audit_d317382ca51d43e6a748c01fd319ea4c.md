### Title
GC owners can front-run staker deposits by instantly raising `PublicDelegation.updateCommissionRate` with no timelock or staker-set cap - (File: contracts/bindings/publicdelegation/PublicDelegation.go)

### Summary
`PublicDelegation` (the KIP staking-pool contract used by delegators/stakers to stake toward a GC and share in staking rewards net of a commission) exposes `updateCommissionRate(uint256 _commissionRate)` as an immediate, single-step state change bounded only by `MAX_COMMISSION_RATE`, with no delay, epoch boundary, or staker-supplied maximum-acceptable-rate parameter. [1](#0-0)  The pool owner (GC operator) can watch the mempool for a staker's `stake()`/`stakeFor()`/`deposit`-style transaction and front-run it by raising `commissionRate` up to `MAX_COMMISSION_RATE` immediately beforehand, so the staker's principal is locked into the pool at a materially worse fee-share than they expected when they signed their transaction. This mirrors the referenced `BondBaseTeller.setReferrerFee()` front-running pattern: an unauthenticated (from the staker's perspective, unbounded at signing time) fee-rate setter that any counterparty can adjust at will right before a value-bearing user transaction executes.

### Finding Description
`PublicDelegation` tracks `commissionRate` (capped by `MAX_COMMISSION_RATE`, denominated against `COMMISSION_DENOMINATOR`) and applies it to rewards paid out to `commissionTo`. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2)  The rate is updated via a single transaction, `updateCommissionRate`, which immediately overwrites the prior rate and emits `UpdateCommissionRate(prevCommissionRate, commissionRate)` — there is no pending-rate/effective-block delay mechanism analogous to a timelock. [4](#0-3) 

Stakers interact with the pool through `stake()` / `stakeFor(address)` (and the ERC-4626-style `previewDeposit`/`previewWithdraw`), committing KAIA to the pool under the assumption that rewards will be shared at the currently observed `commissionRate`. [5](#0-4)  Because the owner-controlled `updateCommissionRate` call has no cooldown, no per-staker maximum-acceptable-rate parameter, and takes effect instantly and permanently for all future reward distributions to already-staked funds, the pool owner can observe a pending stake transaction in the mempool, submit `updateCommissionRate(MAX_COMMISSION_RATE)` with a higher gas price to be included first, and lock in the higher commission before the staker's deposit is processed — exactly the "referrer front-runs fee to increase their own share" pattern from the source report, translated to the staking/commission counterparty role.

### Impact Explanation
A staker's deposited principal earns rewards distributed net of `commissionRate` to `commissionTo`. [6](#0-5)  If the GC owner front-runs a staker's deposit by raising the rate to its maximum right before the stake is confirmed, all subsequent reward distributions attributable to that staker's shares are computed at the inflated rate, redirecting a larger portion of staking rewards to the commission recipient than the staker agreed to when broadcasting the transaction. This is a concrete reward-redirection/fee-abuse impact reachable by an unprivileged staker's normal usage flow (submitting a stake transaction), triggered by a counterparty (the pool/GC owner) who has legitimate but unbounded authority over the fee parameter.

### Likelihood Explanation
The precondition is simply that the pool owner is willing to front-run — a low-cost, always-available action requiring no special privileges beyond normal owner rights and the ability to submit a higher-gas-price transaction, which is realistic for any GC operator that wants to extract more fee revenue from newly attracted stakers. No governance delay, no staker slippage/rate-cap parameter, and no timelocked commission change process exist in the interface to prevent this. [7](#0-6) 

### Recommendation
Add a staker-supplied maximum acceptable `commissionRate` parameter to `stake()`/`stakeFor()` that reverts if the current `commissionRate` exceeds it at execution time, and/or enforce a mandatory delay (e.g., only take effect at the next epoch boundary, or require a `requestUpdateCommissionRate` + timelock two-step flow) before an `updateCommissionRate` change becomes effective, giving stakers a guaranteed window to react before their deposits are subject to a new rate.

### Proof of Concept
1. Staker A observes the current `commissionRate` via the public getter and submits `stakeFor(A)`/`stake()` expecting rewards to be split at that rate. [8](#0-7) 
2. The pool owner monitors the mempool, sees Staker A's pending transaction, and submits `updateCommissionRate(MAX_COMMISSION_RATE)` with higher gas price so it is included first. [1](#0-0) 
3. `UpdateCommissionRate(prevRate, MAX_COMMISSION_RATE)` is emitted and `commissionRate` state is updated instantly and with no delay. [9](#0-8) 
4. Staker A's stake transaction is then mined, and all future reward distributions to Staker A's shares are computed against the inflated commission rate that Staker A never agreed to.
