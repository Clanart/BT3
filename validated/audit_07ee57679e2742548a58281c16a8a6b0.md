### Title
Malformed FROST preprocess can force an identity aggregate nonce and panic BIP-340 signing - (File: `networks/bitcoin/src/crypto.rs`)

### Summary
`bitcoin-serai`’s BIP-340 FROST algorithm accepts peer-controlled preprocess commitments and later assumes the aggregate nonce `R` is non-infinity. A malicious signer can contribute a specially formed preprocess whose bound nonce contribution cancels the honest aggregate nonce, causing `Hram::hram` to panic while converting the point-at-infinity to its x-coordinate.

### Finding Description
FROST preprocesses are parsed only as elliptic-curve commitments; no validation ensures the final aggregate nonce is nonzero:

- `AlgorithmSignMachine::read_preprocess` deserializes attacker-controlled bytes via `Commitments::read`.
- `BindingFactor::nonces` computes each participant’s bound nonce as `D + rho * E` and sums them into the session nonce.
- `Schnorr::sign_share` passes `nonce_sums[0][0]` to the Bitcoin HRAM.
- `Hram::hram` calls `x(R)`, which panics if `R` is infinity.

An attacker can submit `E` as the identity point, making their bound contribution equal to their chosen `D` regardless of the binding factor. After observing the other preprocesses, they can choose `D` to negate the rest of the aggregate nonce. The resulting aggregate `R` is the identity point.

The panic occurs at `x(R)`, because `encoded.x()` is `None` for infinity:

### Impact Explanation
An unauthenticated or peer-level participant who can supply a FROST preprocess for a Bitcoin threshold-signing session can deterministically abort the signer with a panic. This is analogous to malformed wire-protocol input crashing `mongos`: parsing succeeds, but semantic validation leaves an invalid algebraic state that reaches an infallible API.

### Likelihood Explanation
The malicious participant needs to submit a preprocess in a targeted signing session and know the other included preprocesses before selecting the cancelling `D`. This is realistic for the second-stage collection path, where preprocesses are gathered into a `HashMap` and then signed together. The malformed contribution is byte-level input to `read_preprocess`, not dependent on compromised keys or validators.

### Recommendation
Reject invalid aggregate nonces before calling `sign_share`, and reject identity `D`/`E` commitments during preprocess validation where the curve encoding permits them. In addition, make the BIP-340 HRAM return `Option<C::F>` or explicitly reject infinity rather than relying on `expect("point at infinity")`.

A safe check belongs after `B.nonces(&nonces)` and before `algorithm.sign_share(...)`: return `FrostError::InvalidPreprocess(l)` or `InvalidSigningSet` when any session nonce or aggregate `R` is identity.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual byte-level PoC for a two-party Secp256k1 FROST session:

1. Honest participant `1` and attacker `2` each provide one preprocess containing two compressed secp256k1 points `(D, E)`.
2. Attacker encodes `E` as the canonical point-at-infinity encoding if accepted by `Secp256k1::read_G`.
3. Attacker computes the honest party’s bound nonce contribution and sets their own `D` to its negation, making the aggregate nonce `R = identity`.
4. `read_preprocess` accepts the byte sequence because it only performs `read_G` deserialization.
5. `sign` reaches `Schnorr::sign_share`, `Hram::hram`, then `x(R)`, and panics on `point at infinity`.

The decisive issue is the panic path in `networks/bitcoin/src/crypto.rs`, not merely a rejected signature or invalid preprocess.
