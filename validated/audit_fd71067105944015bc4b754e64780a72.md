### Title
ManualCompound sweeps entire contract token balance instead of only the amount claimed in the current transaction, allowing theft of other users' residual reward dust - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`ManualCompound.compound()` distributes reward tokens to `msg.sender` by reading `IERC20(token).balanceOf(address(this))` and transferring that entire balance, rather than tracking and transferring only the amount actually claimed for the caller in that transaction. This is the same root-cause bug class described in the report: using the contract's live balance as the withdrawal/payout amount instead of the specific amount owed to the operation, allowing any unprivileged caller to sweep tokens that belong to other users.

### Finding Description
`compound()` first claims rewards on behalf of the caller via `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` [1](#0-0) , then loops over caller-supplied, non-compoundable reward tokens and pays out `IERC20(_rewards[i][j]).balanceOf(address(this))` in full to `msg.sender`: [2](#0-1) . It then loops over the *global* list of registered compoundable `rewards` and again pays out `IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` — the entire live balance of the token — to whichever address happens to call `compound()`, via convertor/locker/helper or a direct `safeTransfer`: [3](#0-2) .

Because the payout amount is derived from `balanceOf(address(this))` rather than from the amount actually attributable to the caller's own claim in that transaction, any residual balance sitting in the contract — e.g., dust left over from a prior user's compound because a `locker`/`helper`/`convertor` call didn't consume 100% of the received amount due to internal rounding, or a reward token transferred to the contract outside of a full compound flow — becomes claimable by the next arbitrary caller. The non-compoundable loop is worse: the token addresses iterated (`_rewards[i][j]`) are fully attacker-supplied, so any wallet can pass an arbitrary reward-token address that happens to have a leftover balance in `ManualCompound` and have it swept to themselves, regardless of whether they claimed anything for that token in the current call.

This mirrors the `CurveSwapper.withdraw()` bug: the contract should track exactly how much was received/attributable during the current operation (e.g. via before/after balance diff scoped to the caller's own claim) instead of sweeping the whole live balance.

### Impact Explanation
Because `ManualCompound` is a shared, permissionless entry point (any staker can call `compound()`), and rewards routed through `multiclaimOnBehalf` land in this single contract's balance before being redistributed, any dust or residual reward tokens belonging to other users (from imprecise conversion/locking, or non-compoundable rewards not fully swept in the same transaction) can be permanently misappropriated by the next unrelated caller. This is a direct theft of other users' unclaimed/queued yield, satisfying the "theft or permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" impact bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any regular wallet holding a staking position can call `compound()` at will, with attacker-chosen `_rewards` token lists for the non-compoundable branch, requiring no special privileges — only that some residual balance of a reward token exists in the `ManualCompound` contract from prior activity (rounding dust from `IConverter`/`ILocker`/`ISimpleHelper` integrations is plausible given typical fixed-point math in these downstream contracts).

### Recommendation
Track the amount attributable to the current caller's claim explicitly (e.g., via a before/after balance diff computed solely around the `multiclaimOnBehalf` call, per token, per user) rather than reading `balanceOf(address(this))` at payout time. Do not allow the non-compoundable-token loop to sweep arbitrary caller-supplied token addresses' full balances; only distribute what was actually credited to `msg.sender` in the same transaction.

### Proof of Concept
1. User A calls `compound()`, claiming a compoundable reward token `X` with a configured `locker`/`helper` that (due to internal rounding) leaves a small dust balance of `X` in `ManualCompound` after `lockFor`/`depositFor` returns.
2. User B (unrelated, arbitrary wallet) calls `compound()` with `_lps = []` (or pools yielding zero additional reward), so `multiclaimOnBehalf` adds nothing new for token `X`.
3. In the compoundable-rewards loop, `receivedBalance = IERC20(X).balanceOf(address(this))` still reflects User A's leftover dust [4](#0-3) , which gets locked/converted/transferred to User B instead of User A, resulting in theft of User A's reward remainder.
