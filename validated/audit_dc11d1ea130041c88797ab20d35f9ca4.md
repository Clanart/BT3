### Title
Wildcard `with-ft` allowance amount is checked independently per distinct fungible asset instead of as an aggregate budget - (File: clarity/src/vm/functions/post_conditions.rs)

### Summary
`restrict-assets?` / `as-contract?` let a contract author bound the assets an untrusted, nested call (e.g. a caller-supplied trait implementation) can move, by declaring `with-ft` allowances. A wildcard asset name of `"*"` is meant to let a single allowance cover "any fungible token from this contract." The enforcement in `check_allowances` re-applies the *same* wildcard amount independently to every distinct fungible-token asset actually moved, instead of treating it as one shared budget. An attacker who controls the nested call (this is the entire threat model `restrict-assets?`/`as-contract?` exists for) can therefore move up to the allowed amount of *every* distinct fungible asset under the wildcarded contract, multiplying the intended cap by the number of token types they choose to move — an asset moving past what the post-condition/allowance was supposed to permit.

### Finding Description
In `clarity/src/vm/functions/post_conditions.rs`, `check_allowances` first buckets all declared FT allowances by `AssetIdentifier` (contract + asset name), including the wildcard identifier `(contract, "*")`: [1](#0-0) 

It then iterates over every fungible asset that was *actually* moved (`assets.get_all_fungible_tokens(owner)`), and for each one builds a `merged` list consisting of exact-match allowances **plus** any wildcard allowance for that contract: [2](#0-1) 

Finally, for each entry of `merged` it checks `amount_moved > allowance`: [3](#0-2) 

Because this whole block runs once per *distinct* moved asset, and the wildcard entry is looked up fresh (by cloning `contract_identifier` and the literal `"*"` name) inside every iteration, the same single wildcard `amount` from the allowance list is used as the ceiling for *each* asset independently. There is no running total that subtracts previously-moved wildcard-covered assets from a shared budget — the same numeric cap is reused, unmodified, once per token type.

The `Allowance::Ft` doc comment states the allowance "permits the asset owner to move or burn up to the specified amount of a fungible token" (singular) — i.e. the natural reading of `(with-ft contract "*" amount)` is "up to `amount` total from this contract," not "up to `amount` of every token this contract happens to define." [4](#0-3) 

This is structurally the same root cause as the Predy `PriceFeed` bug: a single scalar parameter (`decimalsDiff` there, the wildcard `amount` here) is meant to represent one conversion/budget concept but the enforcement code applies it independently across multiple different "directions"/dimensions (base vs. quote pair there; per distinct FT asset type here) without reconciling them into one consistent total, breaking the equality between "amount authorized by the post-condition/allowance" and "amount actually permitted to move."

### Impact Explanation
An asset can move past what its enclosing post-condition/allowance was supposed to authorize (Critical impact category: "an asset moving past its post-conditions"). Concretely: a contract author writes
```
(as-contract? ((with-ft .some-contract "*" u100))
  (contract-call? untrusted-trait ...))
```
intending to cap the contract's exposure to at most 100 units of any single fungible token defined by `.some-contract`. If `.some-contract` defines multiple distinct FT assets (e.g. `token-a`, `token-b`, `token-c`), an attacker controlling `untrusted-trait` (the whole reason the allowance exists) can transfer up to 100 of `token-a`, 100 of `token-b`, and 100 of `token-c` in the same call — 300 units moved against a cap the author believed was 100. The attacker here is an unprivileged party (any contract passed in as a callee/trait implementation); no signing key, miner, or admin privilege is required, satisfying the "unprivileged-sender" and "no miner/signer/admin" constraints.

### Likelihood Explanation
High for any contract that (a) protects a call with a wildcard `with-ft` allowance against an untrusted/attacker-supplied contract, and (b) the wildcarded target contract defines more than one fungible-token asset. This is a realistic pattern: `restrict-assets?`/`as-contract?` exist specifically to sandbox calls into untrusted trait implementations, and wildcards are the ergonomic way to say "any token from this contract" without enumerating every asset name up front (which may not even be known to the caller). No special network conditions, timing, or races are needed — a single Clarity call reproduces the discrepancy deterministically.

### Recommendation
Track a running, shared remaining-budget per wildcard `Allowance::Ft` entry (keyed by allowance index) and decrement it by every distinct moved asset it covers, rejecting once the aggregate exceeds the declared amount, rather than re-checking each moved asset independently against the untouched original amount. Alternatively, require exact asset-name matches for FT allowances and drop/clearly re-scope the `"*"` semantics so its documented behavior (aggregate vs. per-asset) matches the enforcement.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual Clarity reproduction (illustrates the discrepancy inherent in `check_allowances`'s per-asset re-use of the wildcard amount):
```clarity
;; .some-contract defines two independent SIP-010 fungible tokens
(define-fungible-token token-a)
(define-fungible-token token-b)

;; sandboxing contract
(define-public (safe-call (callee <trait>))
  (as-contract?
    ((with-ft .some-contract "*" u100))     ;; author intends: at most 100 units total
    (contract-call? callee drain)))
```
If `callee` (attacker-controlled) implements `drain` as:
```clarity
(define-public (drain)
  (begin
    (as-contract (ft-transfer? token-a u100 tx-sender 'ATTACKER))
    (as-contract (ft-transfer? token-b u100 tx-sender 'ATTACKER))
    (ok true)))
```
`check_allowances` evaluates the `token-a` move against the merged list containing the wildcard `(idx, u100)` — `100 > 100` is false, no violation — and independently evaluates the `token-b` move against the *same* wildcard entry — again `100 > 100` is false. Both pass, so 200 units move out despite the author's single `u100` wildcard cap, exactly following the code path cited in `clarity/src/vm/functions/post_conditions.rs:598-626`.

**Uncertainty**: I was not able to inspect `clarity/src/vm/tests/post_conditions.rs` in full to confirm whether the wildcard's intended semantics (aggregate vs. per-asset) are explicitly pinned down by an existing test asserting the *aggregate* behavior is by design (in which case this would be a documentation gap rather than a vulnerability). The core-logic evidence above (the amount is looked up fresh per distinct moved asset with no shared/decrementing budget) is taken directly from the enforcement function itself and is unambiguous on its own terms; only the *original design intent* for `"*"` is unverified due to running out of investigation budget.
