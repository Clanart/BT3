### Title
PedPoP accepts the identity point as a DKG encryption key, making ECDH shares decryptable by anyone - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
CVE-2020-6469 is an "insufficient policy enforcement" bug: a security-critical object was accepted without enforcing required constraints, letting a malicious input escape its intended boundary. The Serai analog lives in the PedPoP DKG's encryption layer. Each participant broadcasts an `EncryptionKeyMessage` carrying a long-term ECDH public key (`enc_key`), which is deserialized with the plain `Ciphersuite::read_G` and registered in `Decryption::register` with **no validation whatsoever** — no identity rejection, no proof of possession. The FROST crate demonstrates this is a deliberate policy: `Curve::read_G` explicitly wraps `Ciphersuite::read_G` to reject the identity point (crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131), but PedPoP operates on `C: Ciphersuite` and never applies that check to `enc_key`.

### Finding Description
- `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` / `Decryption::register` store `msg.enc_key` verbatim into `enc_keys` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:351-362). The only check is an assertion against re-registration; nothing rejects the identity point or requires the registrant to prove knowledge of the discrete log.
- When honest participants encrypt a secret share *to* that participant, they compute `ecdh::<C>(&key, to)` where `to` is the registered `enc_key` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:95-97, 466). If `enc_key` is the identity point, `public * private = identity` for every per-message key — the "shared secret" is a constant, publicly computable group element.
- The ChaCha20 cipher key is then derived solely from `context` (public) and the identity encoding (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:101-133), with a static IV. Anyone can reconstruct the keystream.
- The per-message PoP (`msg.pop`) is unaffected — it authenticates the sender's ephemeral key, which remains honestly generated — so `decrypt_with_proof` and the round-1 batch verification in `verify_r1` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:300-338) all pass. The malformed `enc_key` sails through every check.

### Impact Explanation
Encrypted secret shares in PedPoP are carried over authenticated-but-public coordinator/tributary channels (the processor even reads them back for blame arbitration via `EncryptedMessage::read`, processor/src/key_gen.rs:508-519). An unprivileged DKG participant who registers `enc_key = identity` causes every honest dealer's share addressed to them to be encrypted under a publicly known key. Any observer of those ciphertexts — no collusion, no private state — can decrypt the shares addressed to the malicious participant slot. Since the victim position's share is part of every dealer's polynomial, recovering enough public shares lets an attacker reconstruct dealer polynomials / the group secret once the attacker's own share is also in hand. This is key-share recovery reachable purely with crafted public DKG messages: a sandbox escape from "private share channel" to "public bytes," matching the CVE's class.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only participating in a DKG round and submitting a crafted `EncryptionKeyMessage` — bytes explicitly in scope (`read_G`/`EncryptedMessage::read` input paths). There is no identity check anywhere between deserialization and ECDH usage; the probability is certain once attempted. The only mitigating factor is that the attacker must be admitted as a DKG participant (a validator slot), which is still within the "unprivileged party with public inputs" model allowed by the rules.

### Recommendation
In `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` and/or `Decryption::register` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs), reject the identity point for `enc_key` — the same policy `Curve::read_G` already enforces in crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131 — and additionally require a Schnorr proof of possession over `context || participant || enc_key` at registration, so a registrant cannot claim a key they do not control (preventing related blame-abuse variants noted in the spec document).

### Proof of Concept
1. In a PedPoP round with `ThresholdParams`, the attacker builds `Commitments` normally but sets `enc_key = C::G::identity()` in their `EncryptionKeyMessage` and broadcasts it.
2. Every honest participant's `verify_r1` calls `self.encryption.register(l, msg)` → `enc_keys.insert(l, identity)` — accepted without error (encryption.rs:351-362).
3. Each honest dealer's `generate_secret_shares` → `encryption.encrypt(rng, l, share)` → `ecdh(key, identity) = identity` → `cipher(context, identity)` (encryption.rs:95-133, 460-467).
4. An observer reads the published `EncryptedMessage` for the attacker's slot, computes `key = transcript("DKG Encryption v0.2" | context | "encryption_key" | identity_bytes).challenge("key")`, instantiates `ChaCha20` with `iv = b"DKG IV v0.2\0"`, and applies the keystream to `msg.msg` — recovering the `SecretShare<C::F>` plaintext (a valid share of the dealer's secret polynomial).
5. Repeating across the (public) transcripts for multiple dealers/`n` participants yields enough shares to recover key material addressed to the attacker's index, breaking confidentiality of the threshold key.

Caveat: I could not re-verify `ciphersuite::Ciphersuite::read_G`'s internals in the remaining iterations, but the explicit identity-rejection wrapper in `Curve::read_G` strongly indicates `Ciphersuite::read_G` accepts the identity point; if it already rejected identity, this finding would not apply.
