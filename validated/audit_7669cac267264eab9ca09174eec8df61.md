### Title
`compound()` distributes the full contract token balance instead of the caller's own claim delta - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`ManualCompound.compound()` computes `receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` for every configured reward token and forwards/locks/converts that entire balance to `msg.sender`, rather than tracking the delta produced specifically by that caller's own `multiclaimOnBehalf` call. Any reward tokens that are already resident on the contract at the time `compound()` executes — whether from un-swept dust, partial consumption by a convertor/locker, or a direct token transfer — are paid out entirely to whichever address happens to call `compound()` next.

### Finding Description
In `compound()`, the flow is:
1. `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` claims the caller's accrued rewards, which are transferred to `address(this)` (the `ManualCompound` contract) [1](#0-0) .
2. For each configured reward, `receivedBalance` is read as the raw ERC20 balance of the contract, with no snapshot taken before the claim to compute a delta: [2](#0-1) .
3. This full balance is then approved and sent to a convertor, a locker, a helper, or transferred directly to `msg.sender` [3](#0-2) .

Because step 2/3 sweeps the entire current balance rather than only the amount attributable to `msg.sender`'s own claim, any tokens sitting on the contract prior to the call — e.g., dust left behind because a `convertor`/`locker`/`helper` call doesn't consume 100% of the approved `receivedBalance` (rounding, partial fills), or tokens accidentally/deliberately sent directly to the contract address — get swept up and paid to whoever calls `compound()` next. Since `compound()` has no access control (`external`, no modifier) and any address can pass arbitrary `_lps`/`_rewards` arrays, an attacker can call `compound()` with any (even minimal or empty-reward) claim and collect residual balances left by a previous caller's imperfect sweep, or race to call immediately after tokens land on the contract from any external source.

The atomicity of `multiclaimOnBehalf` followed immediately by the balance read within the same transaction means a *fully synchronous* front-run of "another pending multiclaimOnBehalf in the mempool" landing in the same block does not by itself create a window (the victim's own claim-and-sweep is atomic in their own transaction). The exploitable window instead arises whenever the contract holds residual reward-token balance across separate calls — i.e., whenever a prior `compound()` invocation (or any external transfer) leaves tokens behind that were not fully consumed/forwarded, or a `convertor`/`locker` does not consume the exact `receivedBalance` approved to it.

### Impact Explanation
Whoever calls `compound()` while the contract holds residual reward token balance receives value that was not produced by their own claim, i.e. Direct theft of user/protocol funds (reward tokens meant for a different accrual event get paid to an unrelated caller). This matches "Critical - Direct theft of user funds" for cases where the diverted balance is deterministically triggerable (e.g., convertor rounding dust accumulating over time, or a benign integration that transfers tokens directly to `ManualCompound`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on there being a source of leftover balance on the contract between two separate `compound()` calls (e.g., `IConverter.convertFor`/`ILocker.lockFor` not consuming the full approved amount, reward tokens shared across multiple `_lps` claims settling asynchronously, or direct/accidental transfers). The pure "front-run a pending `multiclaimOnBehalf` in the same block" framing as stated does not hold because claim-and-sweep occur atomically inside a single caller's own transaction, so it does not by itself create theft opportunity absent an external residual-balance source. This makes the concrete exploit conditional and not fully self-contained by an unprivileged attacker alone.

### Recommendation
Track `receivedBalance` as a delta: snapshot `IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` immediately before `multiclaimOnBehalf`, and after the claim compute `balanceOf(address(this)) - balanceBefore` per token, forwarding only that delta to `msg.sender`. Alternatively, have `multiclaimOnBehalf` return exact claimed amounts per token and use those instead of any balance read.

### Proof of Concept
Not reproducible as a fully self-contained unprivileged single-transaction PoC under the stated "pending mempool `multiclaimOnBehalf`" precondition, because `compound()`'s claim-and-sweep is atomic per caller. A valid PoC would instead need to first demonstrate a residual-balance source (e.g., a convertor/locker mock that doesn't consume 100% of `receivedBalance`, or a direct token transfer to `ManualCompound`), then show a second unrelated caller's `compound()` sweeping that residual balance alongside their own claim — this is a different precondition than the one specified in the question.
