### Title
MuSig DKG accepts identity public keys, allowing anyone to forge that member's signature share (unauthorized group membership/impersonation) - ([File: crypto/dkg/musig/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
`check_keys` in `crypto/dkg/musig/src/lib.rs` validates the MuSig key list for emptiness, length, and duplicate byte encodings, but never rejects the identity point `C::G::identity()`. An attacker who registers the identity point as "their" public key occupies a participant slot whose discrete logarithm is the publicly known scalar `0`. Because the resulting `verification_shares` entry for that `Participant` is the identity point, FROST's per-share verification (`verify_share` against `view.verification_share(l)`) is satisfied by a share anyone can compute from public data. This is a membership/permission bypass: a slot that is supposed to be gated by knowledge of a secret key is satisfiable by any unprivileged observer.

### Finding Description
- `check_keys` (lines 46-63) builds a `HashSet` of `key.to_bytes()` to reject duplicates, but performs no identity/torsion check on `key`.
- `musig` (lines 116-161) inserts each supplied key verbatim into `verification_shares` (`verification_shares.insert(i, key)` at line 149) and builds `ThresholdKeys` with `Interpolation::Constant(binding_factors)`.
- In `crypto/frost/src/sign.rs`, `AlgorithmSignatureMachine::complete` verifies each share via `self.params.algorithm.verify_share(self.view.verification_share(*l), &self.B.bound(*l), responses[l])` (lines 475-485). For a participant whose verification share is the identity, the share equation reduces to the nonce contribution `d_j + rho_j * e_j` — reconstructible entirely from the public `Commitments`/binding factors, since the secret multiplier is `0`.
- `ThresholdKeys::new`/`ThresholdKeys::read` (`crypto/dkg/src/lib.rs` lines 349-391, 574-632) likewise never check that a verification share is non-identity, so this state is also reachable by feeding crafted bytes to `ThresholdKeys::read`.

### Impact Explanation
Any party — including one with no secret key at all — can produce a cryptographically valid signature share for the identity-keyed participant slot. Concretely, the "member" is fully impersonable: their share passes `verify_share`, contributes to `sum` in `complete`, and the aggregated signature verifies. This mirrors the Discourse flaw: possessing a peripheral capability (submitting a public key / creating a policy) lets an actor obtain membership privileges inside a restricted group (satisfying a threshold slot) that was intended to require exclusive secret material. It also silently degrades an n-of-n to (n-1)-of-(n-1) effective security, since the identity slot contributes `binding_factor * identity = 0` to the group key while still counting as a required signer.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachability requires an attacker to place the identity point in the `keys` list accepted by other participants (e.g., a validator-set registration flow that doesn't PoK the key, or crafted `ThresholdKeys` bytes). Medium likelihood: it needs the victim set to accept an attacker-chosen key list entry, but no collusion, no leaked keys, and no malicious-node assumptions — only public inputs to `musig`/`ThresholdKeys::read`/`verify_share`.

### Recommendation
In `check_keys`, reject `key == C::G::identity()` (and ideally reject non-canonical/torsion encodings upstream of `read_G`). In `musig`, additionally require a proof of possession or at minimum assert `our_pub_key != identity`. In `ThresholdKeys::new`, reject identity verification shares so `ThresholdKeys::read` cannot construct the same state.
