### Title
`ReferralStorage.claimReward()` allows insolvency because `trigger()` credits `rewardAmount` without any real MGP transfer into the contract - ([File: rewards/ReferralStorage.sol])

### Summary
`MasterMagpie._multiClaim()` calls `IReferralStorage(referral).trigger()` purely to update an internal `rewardAmount` ledger for the referrer and referee; no MGP tokens are ever transferred into `ReferralStorage` as part of this call. Because `claimReward()` pays out based solely on the ledger value via `MGP.safeTransfer`, the contract's actual MGP balance is decoupled from the sum of `rewardAmount` entries, letting whichever user claims first drain the pool and leaving other legitimate claimants unable to withdraw.

### Finding Description
In `rewards/MasterMagpie.sol`, `_multiClaim()` sends MGP to the claiming user/receiver via `_sendMGP`, `_sendMGPForVlMGPPool`, or `_sendVlMGPFor`, and separately calls `IReferralStorage(referral).trigger(_user, totalReward)` [1](#0-0) . `trigger()` in `ReferralStorage.sol` only performs bookkeeping — it increments `refererInfo.rewardAmount` and `refereeInfo.rewardAmount` — with no `safeTransferFrom`/`safeTransfer` moving MGP tokens into `ReferralStorage` at all [2](#0-1) . `claimReward()` then pays out strictly from the ledger, assuming the contract's real MGP balance already covers it: it reads `userInfo.rewardAmount`, calls `MGP.safeTransfer(msg.sender, userInfo.rewardAmount)`, and zeroes the ledger entry [3](#0-2) . There is no code path anywhere in the reviewed contracts that deposits MGP into `ReferralStorage` in proportion to accrued `rewardAmount`; funding must occur out-of-band (e.g., an admin/treasury transfer), which is not tied to or validated against the ledger growth from repeated `trigger()` calls. Consequently, the sum of all `rewardAmount` entries can exceed `MGP.balanceOf(address(ReferralStorage))` at any time. `claimReward()` has no check comparing the requested payout against actual on-hand balance beyond `ERC20`'s own revert-on-insufficient-balance behavior, so whichever referrer/referee calls first can withdraw MGP intended to back other users' ledgered amounts, and later claimants' `claimReward()` calls revert (their yield becomes permanently unclaimable if the pool isn't refunded).

### Impact Explanation
This is a real insolvency/backing-mismatch bug in the referral reward pool: `rewardAmount` (an unbacked internal liability) can grow unboundedly via ordinary user activity (staking/claiming through `MasterMagpie`, which triggers `trigger()`), while actual MGP backing lags behind or is never sufficiently topped up. The first claimant(s) can withdraw a disproportionate share of whatever MGP happens to be in the contract, permanently freezing or destroying the yield owed to other referrers/referees (their `claimReward()` will revert once the balance is exhausted). This matches the "theft or permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" / protocol insolvency impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged access is required — any user who has registered a referral code and accumulated referees, or any referee, will have their `rewardAmount` incremented automatically as part of normal reward-claiming flow through `MasterMagpie._multiClaim()`. The exploit condition is simply a race to `claimReward()` before the pool is sufficiently funded, which is highly feasible any time the operational funding of `ReferralStorage` lags behind accrued referral obligations (which the contract itself does nothing to prevent, since `trigger()` never transfers tokens in). This is repeatable every funding cycle.

### Recommendation
Have `trigger()` (or the reward-generation flow in `MasterMagpie`) atomically transfer the corresponding MGP amount into `ReferralStorage` at the time the ledger is credited, or have `claimReward()` pull MGP directly from `MasterMagpie`/a funded escrow at claim time rather than relying on a pre-funded balance. Alternatively, enforce an invariant check (e.g., track total outstanding `rewardAmount` and revert `trigger()` or funding operations that would push total ledgered liabilities above actual backing) so that claims can never exceed the real MGP balance.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry/Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, `ReferralStorage`, `MGP` token, and `VLMGP` per existing test harness.
2. Fund `ReferralStorage` with a small MGP balance (e.g., 10 MGP) to simulate partial/delayed funding.
3. Register referral codes: `refererA` registers a code; `attacker` (as referee) calls `useCode` to link to `refererA`.
4. Repeatedly trigger MGP-claim flows through `MasterMagpie` for the attacker (staking/claim cycles) so that `ReferralStorage.trigger()` is invoked multiple times, inflating `userInfos[attacker].rewardAmount` and `userInfos[refererA].rewardAmount` far beyond the 10 MGP actually held by `ReferralStorage`.
5. Have a second referrer/referee (`victim`) also accrue legitimate `rewardAmount` via the same mechanism.
6. Call `attacker`'s (or `refererA`'s) `claimReward()` first — assert it succeeds and drains most/all of the 10 MGP balance despite `rewardAmount` ledger being much larger.
7. Call `victim`'s `claimReward()` — assert it reverts (insufficient balance) even though `victim.rewardAmount > 0`, proving the backing invariant `sum(rewardAmount) > MGP.balanceOf(ReferralStorage)` is violated and causes fund freezing/insolvency for other claimants.
