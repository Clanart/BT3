### Title
Front-running reward injections (`queueNewRewards`) via flash-stake deposit/withdraw allows theft of un-earned yield from long-term stakers - (File: `rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol` / `rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol`, reached via `rewards/MasterMagpie.sol`)

### Summary
`BaseRewardPool`/`BaseRewardPoolV2` distribute rewards as an instantaneous lump-sum bump to a global `rewardPerTokenStored` accumulator proportional to `totalStaked()` at the exact moment `queueNewRewards` executes, with no time-weighting or vesting period. Because `earned()`/`_updateFor()` always use the account's *live* `balanceOf` (sourced from `MasterMagpie.stakingInfo`), an attacker can deposit into `MasterMagpie` immediately before a reward manager's `queueNewRewards` transaction and withdraw immediately after, capturing a pro-rata share of the injected reward for a zero-duration stake.

### Finding Description
Reward accrual is a share-based accumulator model:
- `_provisionReward` in `rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol` bumps `rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored += (_amountReward * 10**decimals) / totalStaked()` [1](#0-0)  the moment `queueNewRewards` is called [2](#0-1) .
- `earned()`/`_earned()` compute reward strictly as `balanceOf(account) * (rewardPerToken - userRewardPerTokenPaid) + userRewards`, with no time component at all [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) .
- `balanceOf` reads the *live* stake from `MasterMagpie.stakingInfo` [5](#0-4) .
- In `MasterMagpie._deposit`, the checkpoint (`_harvestBaseRewarder` → `rewarder.updateFor`) is called before `user.amount` is incremented [6](#0-5) , so the deposit itself does not immediately steal past rewards. However, once the deposit completes, the attacker's `user.amount` (and thus `balanceOf`) is nonzero and stays elevated in `stakingInfo` with `userRewardPerTokenPaid` set to the pre-injection `rewardPerToken` value.

Exploit flow:
1. Attacker watches mempool for a reward manager's `queueNewRewards` transaction on a target `BaseRewardPool`.
2. Attacker front-runs it with `MasterMagpie.deposit` for staking token `X`, checkpointing `userRewardPerTokenPaid = rewardPerToken_before`.
3. `queueNewRewards` executes, bumping `rewardPerTokenStored` for the whole pool (denominator includes attacker's newly added stake, diluting everyone's future per-share reward, but crediting attacker a pro-rata cut of the injected reward the moment their balance is later checked).
4. Attacker immediately calls `MasterMagpie.withdraw` (or `claim`), which calls `_harvestBaseRewarder`/`getReward`, computing `earned = balanceOf * (rewardPerToken_after - rewardPerToken_before)`, crediting the attacker a proportional share of `R` for zero holding time.
5. There is no minimum staking duration, cooldown, epoch snapshot, or `nonReentrant`-style same-block guard preventing deposit+withdraw in one block for standard staking-token pools (only vlMGP-style pools have any lock semantics unrelated to this).

This differs from time-decayed reward-rate designs (e.g., Synthetix `StakingRewards`, which vests rewards over a `rewardsDuration`); here the entire reward is instantly reflected in the accumulator, so any address holding a balance at call time captures a full pro-rata share regardless of how long they held it.

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of un-earned/unclaimed yield from legitimate long-term stakers: capital that arrives 1 block before an injection and leaves 1 block after siphons a share of `R` that should have accrued only to stakers who held their position while rewards were being earned/queued. This matches the "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class. The magnitude scales with the attacker's capital relative to `totalStaked()` and the size of `R`, and is repeatable for every future reward injection.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: attacker needs (a) visibility of a pending `queueNewRewards` transaction (mempool monitoring; `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` are plain transactions, not protected from front-running) and (b) capital to deposit into the target pool's staking token, which can be sized arbitrarily (even flash-loaned if the staking token is loanable) since no lockup applies to normal pools. No privileged role is required — `deposit`/`withdraw` are public functions and the reward manager's tx is a normal transaction visible pre-confirmation. This is realistically exploitable on any chain without private relays, and is repeatable every time `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` is called.

### Recommendation
Replace the instant lump-sum accumulator with a time-weighted reward-rate distribution (rewards released linearly over a duration, as in Synthetix `StakingRewards`), and/or introduce a minimum staking duration or reward-eligibility snapshot (e.g., checkpoint balances before allowing them to earn newly queued rewards, or require deposits to be held for at least one epoch before being eligible for pending reward pools) so that instantaneous deposit-before/withdraw-after cannot capture rewards proportional to duration-zero stakes.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry/Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, a staking token, and a `BaseRewardPoolV2` with a designated reward manager.
2. Stakers A and B each deposit `100` staking tokens and hold for several blocks (simulate normal long-term staking).
3. In block N: attacker deposits `100` staking tokens into the same pool (front-running), then in the same block the reward manager calls `queueNewRewards(R, rewardToken)`.
4. In block N (or N+1), attacker calls `MasterMagpie.withdraw` for their full `100` tokens and claims rewards via `getReward`.
5. Assert: attacker's claimed reward `≈ R * 100/(300)` (their pro-rata share) despite holding stake for 0 blocks/no meaningful duration, while A and B's remaining claimable reward is correspondingly reduced by the denominator dilution from the attacker's transient stake — demonstrating that unclaimed yield intended for long-term stakers is redirected to a zero-duration flash staker.
