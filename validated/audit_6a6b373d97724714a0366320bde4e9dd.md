### Title
Missing recipient binding in PedPoP encrypted shares lets a participant weaponize another recipient's ciphertext into a false-blame conviction - ([File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs])

### Summary
`EncryptedMessage` binds its proof-of-possession to the `sender` and the ciphertext, but never to the intended `recipient`. `Decryption::decrypt_with_proof` trusts caller-supplied `(sender, decryptor)` indexes, so any party who observes a valid encrypted share `Alice -> Bob` can re-present it as `Alice -> Mallory`, supply a self-generated `EncryptionKeyProof`, and cause the blame machine to wrongfully convict the honest `sender`.

### Finding Description
In `crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs`, `pop_challenge` (lines 302-324) commits `context`, `nonce`, `key`, `sender`, and `message` into the Schnorr PoP transcript - but no recipient identity. The ciphertext itself is keyed by `ecdh(key, to)` where `to` is the recipient's registered encryption key, yet that binding is implicit in decryption, not asserted anywhere.

`Decryption::decrypt_with_proof` (lines 366-397) takes `from` and `decryptor` as unconstrained `Participant` parameters. It verifies:
1. the PoP against `pop_challenge(context, R, key, from, msg)` - `from` is attacker-chosen, so replaying Alice's message with `from = Alice` passes;
2. the optional DLEq proof binding `[G, msg.key]` to `[enc_keys[decryptor], proof.key]` - the accuser can legitimately satisfy this for `decryptor = themselves`, since they know their own private `enc_key` and can compute `proof.key = enc_key * msg.key`;
3. it then decrypts with that wrong key, producing garbage that fails `F::from_repr` or share verification.

In `BlameMachine::blame_internal` (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs`, lines 575-609), every one of those failure modes returns `sender` as the faulty party. So an accusation constructed entirely from another recipient's honestly-generated ciphertext deterministically convicts the honest sender.

### Impact Explanation
Blame is the slashing input: the coordinator's `InvalidDkgShare` handling (`coordinator/src/tributary/handle.rs`) resolves to `VerifyBlame`, and a guilty verdict fatally slashes the accused validator. A malicious participant can therefore get an honest validator blamed and slashed using only bytes that legitimately exist on the wire (the victim's encrypted share to someone else), aborting the DKG and punishing an innocent party. This is a forged/incorrect blame verdict reachable purely from public inputs - analogous to the missing-permission-check class where a party causes an authorization-bearing artifact to be evaluated outside its intended recipient scope.

### Likelihood Explanation
Encrypted shares traverse coordinator-mediated authenticated channels and are stored/retrievable (`DkgShare::get`); any participant in a DKG session can observe or relay ciphertexts addressed to other participants. The attack requires only a DLEq proof the attacker can honestly generate over their own encryption key. Its success depends on the integrator attributing `(sender, recipient)` to the message rather than the ciphertext cryptographically enforcing it - the library's own doc for `blame` only requires the message be "authenticated as actually having come from the sender in question," which is exactly the gap.

### Recommendation
Bind the intended recipient into the per-message proof-of-possession: append `transcript.append_message(b"recipient", to_participant.to_bytes())` in `pop_challenge` (requires threading the recipient `Participant` into `encrypt`/`decrypt`, which already know it). Additionally, `decrypt_with_proof` should treat a valid PoP whose message was not addressed to `decryptor` as `InvalidProof` against the accuser rather than evidence against the sender.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice runs `generate_secret_shares`, producing `Enc_AB = EncryptedMessage` addressed to Bob (PoP signed by Alice with `sender = Alice`).
2. Mallory obtains `Enc_AB` (relay/coordinator storage).
3. Mallory calls `blame(sender = Alice, recipient = Mallory, msg = Enc_AB, proof = Some(EncryptionKeyProof { key: e_M * Enc_AB.key, dleq: DLEq(G, Enc_AB.key; enc_keys[Mallory], e_M * Enc_AB.key) }))`.
4. PoP verifies (`from = Alice` matches Alice's signature). The DLEq verifies because Mallory knows `e_M`. Decryption under `e_M * Enc_AB.key` yields garbage; `from_repr` fails; `blame_internal` returns `Alice` as faulty - a false conviction of an honest participant.
