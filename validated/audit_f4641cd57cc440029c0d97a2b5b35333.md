### Title
`updateEndRemainingAllocation()` lacks a one-shot guard, allowing the bonus denominator to be re-snapshotted after claims occur - (File: rewards/Airdrop.sol)

### Summary
`updateEndRemainingAllocation()` is `public`, unauthenticated, and has no guard preventing repeated execution once `block.timestamp >= periodsEndTime[4]` [1](#0-0) . Because `totalRemainingAllocation` shrinks every time any user calls `claim()` [2](#0-1) , anyone can re-invoke `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` after some users have already claimed to shrink the bonus denominator used in `getBonusAmount()`, inflating the bonus paid to whichever address claims next relative to the fixed `totalBonus` pool.

### Finding Description
`getBonusAmount()` computes each user's bonus share as `userAllocation * totalBonus / totalEndRemainingAllocation` [3](#0-2) . This formula is only mathematically self-consistent (i.e., the sum of all bonus payouts equals `totalBonus`) if `totalEndRemainingAllocation` is fixed exactly once, at the moment the last period ends, to the sum of all allocations not yet claimed at that time.

`claim()` only invokes the snapshot function conditionally, when `totalEndRemainingAllocation == 0` [4](#0-3) , which is meant to perform this one-time initialization lazily. However, `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` itself has no such guard and no access control — it unconditionally overwrites `totalEndRemainingAllocation = totalRemainingAllocation` whenever `block.timestamp >= periodsEndTime[4]` [1](#0-0) .

Since `claim()` decrements `totalRemainingAllocation` by the claimant's allocation on every call, regardless of timing [2](#0-1) , an attacker can:
1. Wait until `periodsEndTime[4]` passes and observe several users call `claim()` (which reduces `totalRemainingAllocation` but does not touch `totalEndRemainingAllocation` after the first snapshot).
2. Call `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` directly to re-snapshot `totalEndRemainingAllocation` to the now-smaller `totalRemainingAllocation`.
3. Immediately call `claim()`. Because the denominator has shrunk while `totalBonus` (the numerator) stayed the same, the caller receives a bonus amount larger than their fair pro-rata share of the originally forfeited bonus pool.

The sum of all bonus payouts across all claimants will then exceed `totalBonus`, meaning excess `aidropToken` is paid out to whoever exploits the re-snapshot timing, at the expense of remaining unclaimed users (who may later hit `InsufficientBalance` reverts or receive a smaller effective share once the pool is drained).

### Impact Explanation
This allows an attacker (who holds any allocation, however small) to extract more `aidropToken` than their legitimate pro-rata bonus share by timing a public, permissionless call to `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` after other users have already claimed. This is a direct theft of funds that rightfully belong to other allocation holders' bonus shares, funded from the shared `aidropToken` balance, and can leave the contract under-collateralized for later claimants.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged role is required — any EOA can call `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` at any point after `periodsEndTime[4]`. The only precondition is that at least one other user has already claimed (reducing `totalRemainingAllocation` below its value at the first snapshot), which is a very likely to occur once the fourth period ends since claiming is permissionless and expected behavior. The attack costs only gas and is repeatable.

Note: under the narrow precondition literally stated in the question's "Fast validation" (block.timestamp just passed `periodsEndTime[4]` and *no one has claimed yet*), a single call to `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` is a no-op — it sets `totalEndRemainingAllocation` to the same value `totalRemainingAllocation` already holds, since nothing has changed it. The exploit requires at least one intervening `claim()` to have occurred before the malicious re-snapshot call.

### Recommendation
Add a one-shot guard so the snapshot can only ever be set once, e.g. `require(totalEndRemainingAllocation == 0, "already set")` inside `updateEndRemainingAllocation()`, or remove the function's public visibility and only allow the internal lazy-initialization from `claim()`'s existing `if (totalEndRemainingAllocation == 0)` check.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry/Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `Airdrop` with `startTime`, register three users A (100), B (100), C (100) via `register()`; fund the contract with sufficient `aidropToken`.
2. Warp `block.timestamp` to before `periodsEndTime[4]`; have A `claim()` early (partial periods), forfeiting some allocation into `totalBonus` (`claimableAmount < userAllocation` case) [5](#0-4) .
3. Warp `block.timestamp` to `>= periodsEndTime[4]`.
4. Have B call `claim()` — this triggers the internal lazy snapshot: `totalEndRemainingAllocation = totalRemainingAllocation` (covering B and C's remaining allocation), and B receives a bonus computed against that denominator.
5. Attacker (any EOA, e.g. C) directly calls `updateEndRemainingAllocation()` — this re-snapshots `totalEndRemainingAllocation` to the new, smaller `totalRemainingAllocation` (now excluding B, who already claimed).
6. C calls `claim()` and receives `getBonusAmount(C)` computed with the smaller denominator, i.e. a larger bonus share than C's fair pro-rata portion of `totalBonus`.
7. Assert: `getBonusAmount(B)_at_step4 + getBonusAmount(C)_at_step6 > totalBonus` recorded before either claim, demonstrating over-payment of the bonus pool caused solely by the unguarded, permissionless re-snapshot call.
