### Title
Encrypted DKG shares are not bound to their intended recipient, letting any participant replay another recipient's ciphertext to frame an honest sender for a fatal blame - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
In PedPoP, every `EncryptedMessage` carries a Schnorr proof-of-possession (`pop`) that binds the message to its claimed *sender* (`from`) and context, but nothing binds it to its intended *recipient*. `Decryption::decrypt_with_proof` then accepts a `EncryptionKeyProof` whose DLEq only proves consistency with *the accusing decryptor's* registered encryption key — it never checks the message was actually ECDH-encrypted to that key. Any participant can therefore take a share message a sender produced for someone else (all `DkgShares` payloads are public on the tributary), present it as the share addressed to them, produce a validly-verifying decryption proof, and cause `blame_internal` to fault the honest sender — analogously to the advisory's unauthorized-party access to internal channels affecting other users.

### Finding Description
`encrypt` computes the PoP as `SchnorrSignature::sign(&key, nonce, pop_challenge::<C>(context, pub_nonce, pub_key, from, msg.as_ref()))` — binding `context`, the sender index `from`, and the ciphertext, but not the destination key or destination `Participant` [1](#0-0) . On the blame path, `decrypt_with_proof` verifies the PoP with `from = sender`, then verifies the DLEq against `[C::generator(), msg.key]` and `[self.enc_keys[&decryptor], *proof.key]` — the *decryptor's* registered key, wherever it was supplied — and decrypts with the prover-supplied `proof.key` [2](#0-1) . `blame_internal` then blames the `sender` whenever the decrypted bytes are a non-canonical scalar or fail share verification [3](#0-2) . The processor's `VerifyBlame` handler accepts accuser-supplied `share` bytes and `blame` proof with no check that the share was the one actually stored under `(accused → accuser)` in `DkgShare` [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
An honest participant can be adjudged faulty by `blame()` / `AdditionalBlameMachine::blame`, which in Serai's coordinator translates into a fatal slash (`ProcessorMessage::Blame { participant: accused }` → fatal slashing). The attacker (the accuser) needs only public data: the target's `EncryptedMessage` bytes addressed to a third party (broadcast in `Transaction::DkgShares` and persisted via `DkgShare::set`), plus their own private `enc_key` to compute the ECDH key and DLEq. The DLEq always verifies because it is defined relative to the accuser's registered `enc_key` regardless of whom the sender encrypted to; decryption then yields garbage, and every failure branch in `blame_internal` (`InvalidSignature` excluded — the PoP still verifies since `from`/ciphertext are unmodified) returns `sender` [5](#0-4) .

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a single malicious DKG participant with public inputs — the broadcast share ciphertext and their own encryption key. No collusion, no threshold compromise, no network-level interception is needed. It does require the accuser to initiate a blame claim against a specific sender and for that sender to have a share message to a third party on the public channel, which is always true in a real DKG round.

### Recommendation
Bind the intended recipient into the encrypted message: include the recipient's `Participant` index (and/or the recipient's registered `enc_key`/`enc_pub_key`) in `pop_challenge` inside `encrypt`, and verify the same binding in `decrypt` and `decrypt_with_proof`. Additionally, in the `VerifyBlame` handler, compare the accuser-supplied `share` bytes against the share recorded in `DkgShare` for the `(accused → accuser)` pair before adjudicating blame.

### Proof of Concept
1. Honest Alice (sender `a`) runs `generate_secret_shares`, producing `EncMsg_{a→b}` for Bob and `EncMsg_{a→c}` for Carol; all ciphertexts are broadcast in `Transaction::DkgShares` and stored under `DkgShare::set(from, to, share)` [6](#0-5) .
2. Malicious Carol takes the public bytes of `EncMsg_{a→b}` — a message ECDH-encrypted to Bob's `enc_key`, not hers.
3. Carol computes `proof.key = ecdh(carol_enc_priv, msg.key)` and a `DLEqProof` over `[G, msg.key]` → `[carol_enc_pub, proof.key]` — exactly what `decrypt()` produces for any message [7](#0-6) .
4. Carol submits `VerifyBlame { accuser: carol, accused: alice, share: EncMsg_{a→b}, blame: Some(proof) }`. `pop.verify` passes (challenge binds `from = alice` and the unmodified ciphertext). The DLEq verifies because `enc_keys[carol]` genuinely equals `G * carol_enc_priv` and `proof.key = msg.key * carol_enc_priv`.
5. `cipher(context, proof.key)` decrypts the ciphertext to garbage (the real key was `msg.key * bob_enc_priv`). `C::F::from_repr` fails or `share_verification_statements` is non-identity → `blame_internal` returns `sender` = Alice [8](#0-7) .
6. The coordinator emits `ProcessorMessage::Blame { participant: alice }` and Alice is fatally slashed despite never misbehaving.
