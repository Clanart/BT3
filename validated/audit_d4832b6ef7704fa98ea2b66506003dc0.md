### Title
Unauthenticated reputation-mint beneficiary in `handle_get_requests` allows self-minting of relayer rewards — (File: `modules/pallets/state-coprocessor/src/impls.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-state-coprocessor::handle_unsigned` is a permissionless, unsigned extrinsic (`ensure_none(origin)`) that anyone can submit as long as they supply a valid membership/state proof for a batch of `GetRequest`s. The reputation-minting beneficiary is taken directly from the attacker-controlled `address: Vec<u8>` field of the message, with no cryptographic binding (no signature check, no relation to `msg.signer` in `RequestMessage`/`ResponseMessage`) tying it to whoever actually assembled the proof.

### Finding Description
Compare this to the sibling reward pallet `pallet-messaging-incentives`, which recovers the relayer's account from an sr25519 signature over the message before minting: `Self::relayer_for(&mw.message)` [4](#0-3) , and explicitly tests that an unsigned/garbage-signature message mints nothing [5](#0-4) .

`pallet-state-coprocessor::handle_get_requests`, by contrast, mints `ReputationAsset` directly to whatever 32-byte `address` was included in the unsigned payload, with no equivalent recovery/verification step: [6](#0-5) 

The extrinsic itself is dispatched via `ensure_none(origin)` (no signed origin, no fee, no stake requirement) [1](#0-0) , and `validate_unsigned` only guards against literal duplicate batches (same request-commitment set) via a `provides` tag — it does not check the `address` field at all [2](#0-1) .

Any party can therefore fetch a real state/membership proof (the proof itself is objectively verifiable and not forgeable), but assign the `address` field to their own account instead of the account of whoever actually performed the querying/relaying work. Because minting scales with `total_bytes` (the abi-encoded size of the `GetResponse`s, driven by the size of the requested storage values) [7](#0-6) , an attacker can target `GetRequest`s that resolve to large storage values (e.g., long byte-string account state) to disproportionately inflate `total_bytes` and thus the reputation minted to themselves.

### Impact Explanation
Reputation Asset is not a cosmetic score — it is explicitly used by `pallet-collator-manager` to select the Collator set from the pool of highest-earning operators [8](#0-7) , and it mirrors `$BRIDGE` earnings 1:1 as "a real, transferable asset that reflects your earnings" per the docs (only burn/soulbound-transfer restrictions apply, not mint-authorization restrictions). This lets an attacker who did no genuine relaying/bandwidth-purchasing work mint themselves reputation credit that competes for Collator seats and misrepresents "proven economic activity," diverting reward/selection weight away from legitimate relayers — this is an unauthorized-execution / wrong-beneficiary class bug, not merely a resource-exhaustion DoS.

### Likelihood Explanation
The call requires only a legitimate, verifiable state/membership proof for a batch of `GetRequest`s that have not yet been responded to — obtaining such a proof does not require being the original relayer, any privileged role, or a malicious peer; any unprivileged party observing pending `GetRequest`s and public chain state can construct and submit this unsigned extrinsic themselves, since `ensure_none` origin requires no signature or bond.

### Recommendation
Bind the reward beneficiary to a value that cannot be freely chosen by the submitter — e.g., require a signature (mirroring `pallet-messaging-incentives`'s `relayer_for` signature-recovery model) over the batch, or restrict `handle_unsigned`'s minting path to only credit an account cryptographically recovered from message-level signer data, rather than trusting the caller-supplied `address` field verbatim.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes an outstanding `GetRequest` (from any application) with keys pointing at large on-chain storage values, plus its valid source and destination state proofs (publicly derivable, no privileged access needed).
2. Attacker constructs `GetRequestsWithProof { requests, source, response, address: <attacker_account_bytes> }` and submits it as an unsigned extrinsic to `StateCoprocessor::handle_unsigned` [1](#0-0) .
3. `handle_get_requests` verifies the proofs (which are genuine) and, having verified nothing about who supplied `address`, mints `rate * total_bytes` reputation directly to the attacker's account [9](#0-8) .
4. Repeating against requests with large storage-value payloads inflates `total_bytes` and thus reputation minted per submission, all attributable to the attacker rather than any real relaying/querying party.
