Analysis:

The `order.output.beneficiary` field is fixed at order creation time and is part of the hashed `Order` struct that forms `commitment` [1](#0-0) . A filler cannot alter the beneficiary of someone else's order — they can only choose to fill (or self-create and then self-fill) an order whose beneficiary they control if they were the one who created that order in the first place. So the described "attack" only works when the attacker is both the order's creator (and thus its escrow owner) and its filler — i.e., they are only ever manipulating their own already-escrowed funds, not a victim's.

Walking through the surplus/dust logic in `_fillSameChain`, when `alreadyFilled == 0 && solverAmount > totalRequired` and `order.output.call.length == 0`, the dust is split into `protocolShare = dust * surplusShareBps / 10_000` and `beneficiaryShare = dust - protocolShare` [2](#0-1) . If the filler sets themselves as beneficiary and deliberately overpays, they simply get back `beneficiaryShare` of their own overpayment while the protocol still collects `protocolShare` via `DustCollected` [3](#0-2) . The escrow release amount is computed strictly from the original order's `totalRequired`/`totalAmount`, not from the overpaid `solverAmount`, so overpaying does not inflate `escrowedInputs` [4](#0-3) .

Since the same address is both order creator (owner of the escrowed input) and beneficiary/filler, all value in play — the escrow and the output tokens paid for the fill — originates from that single actor's own funds. Filling one's own order and getting `beneficiaryTotal` back (input tokens via `_withdraw` plus `beneficiaryShare` of one's own overpaid output tokens) does not move any other party's balance, and the protocol's `protocolShare` cut is still correctly retained per `_params.surplusShareBps` [5](#0-4) . There is no unauthorized transfer of a victim's escrow, no double-release, and no reduction of protocol revenue — self-filling one's own order (with or without deliberate overpayment) is a degenerate but harmless case of the intended atomic-swap design, not a fund-theft path against another party. This does not meet the bounty's required impact of wrongful movement of funds belonging to someone other than the caller.
