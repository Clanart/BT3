### Title
Lack of Slippage Protection in `withdraw`/`redeem` of the `PublicDelegation` Staking Vault - (File: contracts/bindings/publicdelegation/PublicDelegation.go)

### Summary
`PublicDelegation` is Kaia's ERC4626-style staking vault contract that lets public stakers deposit KAIA (`stake`/`stakeFor`) and later exit via `withdraw(_recipient, _assets)` or `redeem(_recipient, _shares)`, using `convertToAssets`/`convertToShares`/`previewWithdraw`/`previewRedeem` to price shares against `totalAssets()`. Exactly like the PoolTogether `PrizeVault` finding, neither `withdraw` nor `redeem` accepts a slippage-protection parameter (e.g. `maxSharesIn` or `minAssetsOut`), so a staker's pending transaction can be executed against a worse share/asset exchange ratio than expected.

### Finding Description
The contract exposes the full ERC4626 share-pricing surface: `convertToAssets(uint256 _shares)`, `convertToShares(uint256 _assets)`, `previewWithdraw`, `previewRedeem`, `maxWithdraw`, `maxRedeem`, and `totalAssets()` [1](#0-0) , together with mutating `withdraw(address _recipient, uint256 _assets)` and `redeem(address _recipient, uint256 _shares)` functions that take only a fixed amount and no bound on the counter-value received/burned [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

Because the exchange ratio depends on `totalAssets()` (the underlying CnStaking-delegated balance minus any pending withdrawal requests) relative to total shares, any state change that reduces `totalAssets()` between the time a staker signs and submits a `withdraw`/`redeem` transaction and the time it is actually mined — e.g. commission being taken via `sendCommission`/`updateCommissionTo` flows, a `redelegateByAssets` operation moving funds to another CnStaking with different accounting, or any other operation that decreases the vault's asset backing relative to share supply — will silently change the assets received (for `redeem`) or shares burned (for `withdraw`) versus what the staker observed via `previewRedeem`/`previewWithdraw` off-chain before submitting the transaction. This is functionally identical to the `PrizeVault.withdraw`/`redeem` root cause: no minimum-out / maximum-in check is enforced on-chain at execution time.

### Impact Explanation
A staker who redeems shares can receive strictly fewer KAIA assets than they previewed, or a staker who withdraws a fixed asset amount can have more shares burned than expected, resulting in unauthorized value loss to the staker with no recourse — the transaction still succeeds (no revert), it merely settles at a worse ratio. This is a direct, unprivileged-staker-reachable value-extraction path against the staking module's vault contract.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any staker calling `withdraw`/`redeem` is exposed whenever there is mempool latency and any legitimate on-chain state change that shifts `totalAssets()`/total-shares ratio in between (commission distribution, redelegation, or multiple concurrent stakers withdrawing at once causing rounding-driven ratio drift). No malicious actor coordination is strictly required, though an adversary who can trigger such a ratio shift (e.g. front-running with their own `redeem`/`redelegateByAssets`) can amplify the loss for a targeted pending transaction — making this Medium likelihood, consistent with the original finding's judged severity.

### Recommendation
Add slippage-protected variants of `withdraw`/`redeem` (e.g. `withdraw(recipient, assets, maxSharesBurned)` / `redeem(recipient, shares, minAssetsOut)`) that revert if the executed conversion exceeds/falls short of the caller-supplied bound, mirroring the PoolTogether team's confirmed mitigation (https://github.com/GenerationSoftware/pt-v5-vault/pull/94), while keeping the existing unprotected functions for strict ERC4626 compliance if required by integrators.

### Proof of Concept
1. Staker calls `previewRedeem(shares)` off-chain and observes `assets = convertToAssets(shares)` under the current `totalAssets()`/`totalSupply()` ratio, then signs and broadcasts `redeem(recipient, shares)` [4](#0-3) .
2. Before the transaction is mined, another transaction (e.g. commission distribution, another staker's `redelegateByAssets`, or rounding-affected withdrawal) reduces `totalAssets()` relative to `totalSupply()` [5](#0-4) .
3. The staker's `redeem` transaction then executes and settles using the new, worse ratio via `convertToAssets`, transferring fewer assets than previewed, with no on-chain check to prevent or revert this [6](#0-5) .
