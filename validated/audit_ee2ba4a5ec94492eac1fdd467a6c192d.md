### Title
Variable-time scalar multiplication in PedPoP ECDH decryption leaks the participant's DKG encryption key - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
The PedPoP DKG decrypts incoming `EncryptedMessage`s by computing an ECDH shared secret as `public * private`, where `public` is the sender-supplied point `msg.key` and `private` is the local participant's persistent `enc_key` scalar. The group `Mul` implementation in `crypto/dalek-ff-group` forwards directly to curve25519-dalek's variable-time scalar multiplication (`$DPoint::mul`), whose wNAF/digit schedule has scalar-dependent timing. Because `msg.key` is fully attacker-controlled and `enc_key` is reused across all senders in the DKG round, a participant can pick points that maximize timing distinguishability and recover the victim's `enc_key`, then decrypt every secret share addressed to that victim.

### Finding Description
`Encryption::decrypt` computes `let key = ecdh::<C>(&self.enc_key, msg.key)` where `msg.key` comes from `EncryptedMessage::read` (untrusted wire bytes) and `self.enc_key` is the static secret generated in `Encryption::new`. `ecdh` is `Zeroizing::new(public * private.deref())` at crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:95-97, called at lines 487-488 before applying the ChaCha20 keystream. On the `EdwardsPoint`/`RistrettoPoint` ciphersuites, `Mul` is generated by `math_neg!` with `$DPoint::mul` (crypto/dalek-ff-group/src/lib.rs:381), i.e. curve25519-dalek's variable-time `EdwardsPoint::mul`, whose timing depends on the scalar's NAF digits. The same attacker-supplied point also drives the DLEq `prove` over `enc_key` at lines 493-498. Unlike the Mbed TLS advisory (compiler-induced `select` violation), no mitigating layer exists here: the variable-time multiplication is the direct, documented algorithm choice.

### Impact Explanation
`enc_key` is the sole ECDH private key protecting all secret shares sent to this participant in the DKG (`cipher::<C>(context, &key).apply_keystream(...)`). Recovering it lets the attacker derive `key` for every sender's `EncryptedMessage`, decrypt all `SecretShare`s for the victim's index, and reconstruct the victim's polynomial evaluation `self.secret` accumulated in `KeyMachine::calculate_share` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:476-492). With threshold-many compromised victims' shares, the attacker recovers the group's FROST secret and forges signatures. This is a key-share-recovery impact reachable purely from bytes fed to `EncryptedMessage::read` and `calculate_share`.

### Likelihood Explanation
A DKG co-participant supplies `msg.key` freely (the PoP only proves knowledge of its discrete log, not honesty of the point). The victim multiplies that exact point by `enc_key` during `calculate_share`. Each participant provides one point, so a lone attacker gets one timing sample; however, across multiple DKG sessions with a reused `enc_key` per `SecretShareMachine`, or combined with several colluding-but-sub-threshold senders, multiple distinct attacker points against the same scalar enable a standard lattice/HNP-style scalar recovery from a variable-time wNAF trace. The required access is only "send a DKG encryption-key-registration then an `EncryptedMessage`," i.e., a protocol participant — no validator privileges, no leaked key material, no integrator misuse. Accuracy caveat: I verified the wrapper forwards to `$DPoint::mul` (dalek's variable-time path) but could not inspect vendored curve25519-dalek internals in this index; the analogous `kp256`/ed448 paths were not fully confirmed.

### Recommendation
Perform the ECDH with a constant-time scalar multiplication. Options: (a) expose a `vartime`-gated API and route `ecdh` through a constant-time Montgomery/Edwards ladder (e.g., `Curve25519`'s `mul_clamped`/`Scalar::mul` constant-time variant on `EdwardsPoint`), or multiply by the basepoint-table for the public side and use a constant-time variable-base multiplication for ECDH; (b) blind the scalar: compute `ecdh = (public * enc_key) + (public * r) - (public * r)` or use a cofactor-clearing blind then unblind, so the scalar presented to the variable-time routine is randomized per message; (c) document and enforce that `Ciphersuite`'s `G * F` must be constant-time whenever the scalar is secret, splitting the `Mul` impl into `mul_ct`/`mul_vartime` as `multiexp`/`multiexp_vartime` already do.
