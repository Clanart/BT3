### Title
NFT royalty payment amount can round down to zero while offer-mod TAIL enforcement uses a separately-truncated `trade_prices` value, allowing NFT royalty underpayment on CAT-for-NFT offers - (File: chia/wallet/nft_wallet/nft_wallet.py)

### Summary
`NFTWallet.make_nft1_offer()` computes the actual royalty payout with `compute_royalty_amount()` on one side of a trade, and independently computes a `trade_prices` list (used to gate the enforced royalty check baked into the CAT outer puzzle) via a *separate* integer division `amount // offer_side_royalty_split`, then filters entries out of `trade_prices_list` if `price[0] * offered_royalty_percentages[asset] // MAX_ROYALTY_BASIS_POINTS != 0`. Because these two truncating computations are not derived from each other, small per-item amounts (many NFTs in one offer, low CAT/XCH unit amounts, or low royalty percentages) can cause the enforced trade price to round to zero while the "true" royalty amount from `compute_royalty_amount` is nonzero elsewhere, or vice versa — an integer-division precision-loss pattern directly analogous to the WIchiFarm `stIchi`/`enIchi` bug (two independently truncated quantities compared/derived instead of one consistent computation).

### Finding Description
In `chia/wallet/nft_wallet/nft_wallet.py`:
- `royalty_calculation()` computes `abs(amount) // len(royalty_assets_dict) * percentage // MAX_ROYALTY_BASIS_POINTS` [1](#0-0) .
- Inside `make_nft1_offer()`, the **trade price** enforced on the CAT/XCH side is computed independently as `amount // offer_side_royalty_split` [2](#0-1) .
- The royalty actually paid out is computed via `compute_royalty_amount(amount, request_side_royalty_split, percentage)` on a different quantity/split [3](#0-2) .
- The `trade_prices_list` passed into `generate_signed_transaction()` (which the CAT outer puzzle/TAIL uses to *enforce* that a royalty-bearing trade price condition exists) is then filtered with a second independent truncating division: `price[0] * offered_royalty_percentages[asset] // MAX_ROYALTY_BASIS_POINTS != 0` [4](#0-3) .

These three integer-truncating computations (`royalty_calculation`, the trade-price split, and the trade-price-percentage filter) operate on different splits/multiplicands and are not reconciled, so for a given real-world offer a wallet can produce a `trade_prices_list` whose enforced percentage-of-trade-price rounds to zero (and is therefore dropped from `trade_prices_list`) even though the actual `compute_royalty_amount()` payment for the same asset is nonzero, or the reverse case where the enforced trade price is non-zero but the paid royalty amount is smaller than what the trade price implies due to a different truncation path. This mirrors the WIchiFarm pattern of computing two truncated quantities separately instead of factoring the division once, so information is lost in a way that is inconsistent between the two paths.

### Impact Explanation
Royalty precision loss directly reduces payouts to NFT creators/royalty-address holders in CAT/XCH-for-NFT offers constructed by the wallet, and — because the trade-price filtering (`!= 0` check at line 1030) can independently decide to omit a `trade_prices_list` entry — a maker/taker offer can be constructed where the on-chain enforced royalty condition differs from the intended royalty amount. This is fund loss to royalty recipients in a reachable, unprivileged wallet-user flow (any NFT offer creator/taker using `create_offer_for_ids`/`respond_to_offer`), matching the "loss of rewards to precision loss" impact category described in the source report, though scoped specifically to royalty payments rather than farm rewards.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is limited to specific parameter combinations: small per-unit CAT/XCH offer amounts, multiple simultaneous royalty-bearing NFTs (increasing the divisor `offer_side_royalty_split`/`len(royalty_assets_dict)`), and low royalty basis points. These conditions are plausible in real usage (e.g., bulk/complex NFT offers as covered by `test_complex_nft_offer`), but most single-NFT, reasonably-sized-amount offers will not trigger a rounding-to-zero divergence, similar to how the original WIchiFarm report needed specific reward-per-share magnitudes.

### Recommendation
Compute the enforced trade price and the actual paid royalty amount from a single, shared rounding path (or perform multiplication before any division, as suggested in the original report's `WConvexPools`-style fix) so that `trade_prices_list` and `compute_royalty_amount()` never diverge in their rounding behavior for the same underlying value. Concretely, derive the trade-price filtering directly from the same intermediate value used by `compute_royalty_amount`, rather than re-deriving `amount // offer_side_royalty_split` and then re-multiplying/dividing by percentage.
