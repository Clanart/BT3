### Title
Unvalidated identity/torsion encryption key in PedPoP lets an attacker make every secret share addressed to them publicly decryptable - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to CVE-2020-25459 (sensitive information readable by unauthorized parties during joint computation), Serai's PedPoP DKG lets any participant register an arbitrary group element as their long-term encryption key. `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` only performs canonical-encoding checks via `C::read_G`, and `Decryption::register` stores the key without rejecting the identity or low-order points. A malicious participant who registers `enc_key = identity` causes every honest sender's ECDH to collapse to the identity element, producing a publicly-computable ChaCha20 keystream. Since encrypted shares are published to the coordinator/tributary (`DkgShare::set`), any observer — not just the intended recipient — can decrypt every share addressed to the malicious participant, violating the confidentiality guarantee the encryption layer exists to provide.

### Finding Description
In `crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs`:

- `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` (lines 57-59) reads `enc_key` with `C::read_G`, which only enforces canonical encoding (`crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-101`). The identity point is a canonical, valid encoding and is accepted.
- `Decryption::register` (lines 351-362) inserts `msg.enc_key` into `enc_keys` with no validity check (no `is_identity` rejection, no PoK on the long-term key).
- `encrypt` (lines 135-168) computes `ecdh(&key, to) = to * key`. If `to` is the identity, the shared secret is the identity regardless of the ephemeral `key`, and `cipher(context, identity)` (lines 101-133) derives a keystream any third party can reproduce: `RecommendedTranscript::new(b"DKG Encryption v0.2")` + context (public) + `identity.to_bytes()` + fixed IV `"DKG IV v0.2\0"`.
- `SecretShareMachine::generate_secret_shares` (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:347-380`) encrypts each secret share to `self.decryption.enc_keys[&participant]` without checking the key.
- On the coordinator side, shares are persisted publicly via `DkgShare::set` (`coordinator/src/tributary/handle.rs:361`), so the ciphertexts are observable by everyone, and `AdditionalBlameMachine::new` (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:649-662`) likewise registers enc keys without validation.

There is a PoK on the per-message ephemeral key (`msg.pop`), but none on the registered long-term `enc_key`, so the recipient is never required to prove they know its discrete log.

### Impact Explanation
Every `EncryptedMessage` sent to the malicious participant is encrypted under a keystream derivable by any observer who knows only public data (context, the identity encoding, the static IV). Because the shares-transport layer publishes ciphertexts on-chain, all `n-1` honest participants' secret shares evaluated at the attacker's index are readable by the world, not just the attacker. This breaks the DKG's confidentiality layer exactly as the CVE class describes — secret material of the joint computation becomes readable by unauthorized parties. Beyond direct disclosure, it also forces honest senders to encrypt to a degenerate key, and because the ECDH is the constant identity, the attacker can also reuse a single known keystream relationship across attempts with the same context, weakening the per-message-key guarantee the blame system relies on (a published blame proof for one message trivially decrypts all messages to that participant).

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully reachable by an unprivileged DKG participant using only public inputs: they simply craft their `EncryptionKeyMessage` commitment bytes with the identity encoding in the `enc_key` position. No collusion, key compromise, or protocol violation detectable by existing checks is needed — the message parses cleanly through `read_G` and passes `verify_r1` (the Schnorr PoK covers `commitments[0]`, not `enc_key`). The only requirement is that the attacker be a DKG participant.

### Recommendation
Reject non-contributory public keys at registration: in `Decryption::register` / `verify_r1`, require `!bool::from(msg.enc_key.is_identity())` and ideally require a Schnorr proof-of-possession over the long-term `enc_key` (bound to `context` and `participant`), matching the per-message PoP already enforced. Additionally consider enforcing a contributory ECDH check in `decrypt`/`decrypt_with_proof` (reject identity shared secrets) so a faulty registry key aborts the protocol instead of silently encrypting to a known keystream.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker, as DKG participant `j`, broadcasts a `EncryptionKeyMessage` where `enc_key` serializes to the identity point (e.g., the 32-byte compressed identity for Ristretto). It passes `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` and `Decryption::register`.
2. Every honest sender `i` calls `encrypt(rng, context, i, enc_keys[j] = identity, share_i(j))`. `ecdh = identity * key = identity`, so `cipher(context, identity)` produces a keystream computable by anyone.
3. The coordinator persists the ciphertexts (`DkgShare::set`). Any observer recomputes `RecommendedTranscript::new(b"DKG Encryption v0.2")` → `append_message(b"context", context)` → `domain_separate(b"encryption_key")` → `append_message(b"shared_key", identity_bytes)` → `challenge(b"key")` as the ChaCha20 key with IV `"DKG IV v0.2\0"`, XORs it against `msg`, and recovers each honest dealer's share `f_i(j)` in plaintext — shares that were only ever intended for participant `j`.
