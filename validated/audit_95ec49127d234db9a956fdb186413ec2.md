No vulnerability found for this question.

The reported issue is an ERC4626-style "share inflation" / first-depositor attack, which depends on a vault contract that (1) mints fungible shares proportional to `assets/totalSupply`, and (2) computes share price using a contract's on-chain balance that can be inflated by a direct token transfer bypassing the deposit function.

Chia's architecture has no equivalent mechanism reachable by an unprivileged spend-bundle submitter or wallet action:

- Chia uses a UTXO/coin model, not pooled balance accounting. There is no vault-style contract where a `getIndex()`/`getNetValue()` computes share price from a mutable aggregate balance that can be topped up out-of-band by an attacker [1](#0-0) .
- The closest "shared value" constructs — `PoolWallet` singleton state and `PlotNFT2Wallet` reward claiming — track individual farming-reward coins by exact coin ID and height, and distribute rewards 1:1 to the coins actually farmed, not via a proportional share/index ratio susceptible to donation-based dilution [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .
- CAT/CR-CAT wallets mint/transfer tokens 1:1 based on TAIL-authorized deltas, not based on any share-price index computed from a contract's total balance [4](#0-3) .

Since there is no on-chain construct in scope where a first "depositor" receives shares computed as `amount / currentIndex` and where `currentIndex` can be inflated by directly sending an asset into a pooled balance, the described bug class does not have a reachable analog in this codebase.
