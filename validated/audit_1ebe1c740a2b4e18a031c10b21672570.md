### Title
Identity encryption key accepted in PedPoP `EncryptionKeyMessage` makes a participant's DKG secret shares world-decryptable - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
The bug class from the external report — public keys accepted at a message boundary without structural validation — maps onto Serai's PedPoP DKG encryption layer. `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` parses the per-participant ECDH encryption key with `C::read_G(reader)?`, which only enforces canonical encoding and torsion-freeness. It does **not** reject the identity point (unlike `frost::Curve::read_G`, which does), and unlike the polynomial `Commitments` in the same message, the `enc_key` carries no proof-of-possession at all. A DKG participant can therefore register the identity point as their encryption key, causing every peer's ECDH share-encryption to collapse to a publicly derivable key.

### Finding Description
In `EncryptionKeyMessage::read`, the `enc_key` field is deserialized with the ciphersuite-level `C::read_G` [1](#0-0) . `Ciphersuite::read_G` checks only that the encoding is canonical [2](#0-1) , and dalek-ff-group's `from_bytes` additionally enforces torsion-freeness — but the identity point satisfies both checks [3](#0-2) . The identity-rejecting `read_G` exists only on `frost::Curve`, which pedpop does not use (it is generic over `Ciphersuite`) [4](#0-3) .

`Decryption::register` inserts this key without any further validation or PoP [5](#0-4) , and `verify_r1` only batch-verifies the Schnorr PoK over `commitments[0]` — the `enc_key` is never bound to any proof [6](#0-5) . Encryption then computes `ecdh(key, to) = to * key`; with `to = identity`, the shared point is always identity regardless of the ephemeral scalar [7](#0-6) . The ChaCha20 key is derived purely from `context || identity.to_bytes()` with a static IV [8](#0-7) , so anyone who knows the (public, session-derived) `context` can recompute the keystream.

### Impact Explanation
Every honest participant encrypts their polynomial evaluation `f_j(l)` for the malicious participant `l` to `l`'s identity `enc_key` in `generate_secret_shares` [9](#0-8) . In the processor, these `EncryptedMessage` blobs are routed through coordinator/tributary messages visible to all validators [10](#0-9) . Any observer — including the other validators — can derive the keystream and recover all of `l`'s incoming secret shares. Since `l`'s final secret share is the sum of those shares (`*self.secret += share` in `calculate_share`) [11](#0-10) , `l`'s threshold key share is fully recoverable by any third party. The protocol still completes successfully (decryption yields valid, well-formed shares and `share_verification_statements` pass), so the generated key is used in production while effectively requiring only `t - 1` honest shares to sign. This applies to Ristretto (substrate keys) and Ed25519; secp256k1 identity has no canonical SEC encoding and would fail at `read_G`.

### Likelihood Explanation
A single malicious DKG participant sends one malformed round-1 message — valid commitments/PoK, identity `enc_key` — reachable purely through public protocol input (`EncryptionKeyMessage::read` on peer-supplied bytes). No detection path exists: the message parses, the PoK verifies against `commitments[0]`, and shares encrypted to identity decrypt correctly *for everyone*, so `calculate_share` succeeds and no blame is raised. The only anomaly is observable post-hoc by anyone replaying the published transcript.

### Recommendation
Reject the identity point for `enc_key` in `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` / `Decryption::register` (e.g., `if msg.enc_key.is_identity().into() { Err(...) }`), mirroring `Curve::read_G`'s identity check. Additionally consider requiring a proof-of-possession for `enc_key` bound to the participant index and context, so encryption keys must have a known discrete log.

### Proof of Concept
1. Malicious participant `M` runs `generate_coefficients`, then overwrites `msg.enc_key` with `C::G::identity()` before broadcasting `EncryptionKeyMessage` (no re-signing needed — nothing authenticates `enc_key`; serialization is `cached_msg || sig || enc_key_bytes`).
2. All honest participants `j` accept the message: `read` succeeds, `verify_r1` PoK passes (it only covers `commitments`), and each computes `ecdh(k_j, identity) = identity` when encrypting share `f_j(M)`.
3. Any observer reading the shares on the tributary derives `ChaCha20` key `H(context || 0x00..00)` per the `cipher` function and decrypts every `f_j(M)`, yielding `M`'s secret share `Σ f_j(M)` — a full key-share recovery reducing the effective threshold by one, with the DKG completing cleanly.

  transcript.append_message(b"context", context);

  transcript.domain_separate(b"encryption_key");

  let mut ecdh = ecdh.to_bytes();
  transcript.append_message(b"shared_key", ecdh.as_ref());
