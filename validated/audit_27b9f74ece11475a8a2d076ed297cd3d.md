### Title
Deserialized `ReceivedOutput`s are accepted without verifying the outpoint exists on-chain, letting fabricated inputs inflate the wallet's perceived balance and poison every transaction built from them - (File: networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/mod.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to `Clearinghouse.claimDefaulted` accepting loans it never originated, `ReceivedOutput::read` and `SignableTransaction::new` accept a claimed BTC input (offset, `TxOut`, `OutPoint`) with no check that the outpoint actually exists on-chain or carries the claimed value. The only consistency check, in `SignableTransaction::multisig`, verifies that `p2tr_script_buf(keys.offset(offset).group_key())` equals the claimed `script_pubkey` — i.e., internal consistency of the fabricated claim, not provenance. This lets an attacker who can feed bytes to `ReceivedOutput::read` inject phantom inputs of arbitrary value into the wallet's accounting.

### Finding Description
`ReceivedOutput::read` (`networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/mod.rs:122`) deserializes three fully attacker-controlled fields — `offset` via `Secp256k1::read_F`, then a `TxOut` and `OutPoint` via consensus decode (`mod.rs:123-133`) — with zero validation. Legitimate `ReceivedOutput`s are only minted inside `Scanner::scan_transaction` (`mod.rs:199-214`), which guarantees the outpoint refers to a real transaction output paying to a registered key. The `read` path skips that guarantee entirely.

When such a fabricated output is passed to `SignableTransaction::new` (`send.rs:150`), its claimed `value` is summed into `input_sat` (`send.rs:175`) and drives `NotEnoughFunds`, change computation (`send.rs:224-235`), and the effective fee (`send.rs:139`). In `multisig` (`send.rs:273-285`), the only check is `p2tr_script_buf(offset.group_key())? != self.prevouts[i].script_pubkey` — which a fabricated output trivially satisfies by construction, since the attacker chooses both the offset and the matching P2TR script. Nothing verifies `previous_output` resolves to a confirmed UTXO. The threshold group then signs a sighash committing to `Prevouts::All` including the phantom prevout (`send.rs:375-390`).

### Impact Explanation
- **Funds reported received that are not spendable**: a forged `ReceivedOutput` reports an arbitrary BTC amount to the wallet/scheduler while being unspendable, directly mirroring the report's fictitious debt erasure corrupting treasury reserve accounting.
- **False balance accounting → poisoned transactions**: a single phantom input inflates `input_sat`, causing the wallet to sign transactions funding payments far beyond real reserves. Because the fabricated outpoint doesn't exist, the signed transaction is unbroadcastable — but it is still a validly-produced threshold signature over attacker-shaped content, consuming a signing round and any associated signing budget/rate limits, and corrupting the accounting that scheduling decisions depend on.
- The discrepancy between the "originated by the scanner" invariant and the deserialization surface is the exact analog of the missing `claimDefaulted` provenance check.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable wherever serialized `ReceivedOutput`s transit an untrusted boundary (the prompt's own threat model lists `ReceivedOutput::read` as an untrusted-bytes surface). Exploitation requires only that the attacker compute a scalar offset whose tweaked key produces the claimed `script_pubkey` — trivial since they pick both — and supply a plausible `OutPoint`. No key material, validator status, or collusion is needed. Impact is bounded by Bitcoin consensus (the fake input can't be spent, so the tx can't confirm), limiting the direct loss to corrupted accounting and wasted signature rounds — hence Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
- Bind `ReceivedOutput` provenance: either make `ReceivedOutput::read` private to scanner-verified flows, or require consumers (e.g., `SignableTransaction::new`/`multisig`) to confirm each `outpoint` resolves on-chain to a confirmed UTXO whose `TxOut` equals the claimed one before accounting its value.
- Document that `ReceivedOutput::read` must never be fed untrusted bytes, and treat the scanner as the sole minting authority.
