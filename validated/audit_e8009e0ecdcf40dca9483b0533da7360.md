### Title
`compound()` sweeps the contract's entire reward-token balance and self-directs it to the caller with a caller-controlled slippage floor - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`ManualCompound.compound()` claims rewards on behalf of `msg.sender` via `multiclaimOnBehalf`, but the subsequent conversion/lock/deposit loop does not use the amount actually claimed by the caller. Instead it reads `IERC20(rewards[i].tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` — the whole contract's balance of every registered reward token — and routes the full swept amount to `msg.sender`, using a caller-supplied `_minRec`/`_convertRatio` as the only protection on the underlying AMM swap.

### Finding Description
In `compound()` [1](#0-0) , the caller first triggers `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)`, which claims rewards specifically owed to `msg.sender` for the supplied `_lps`/`_rewards`. Non-compoundable rewards are refunded to `msg.sender` based on the current balance at that point.

The critical flaw is in the second loop [2](#0-1) : it iterates over **every registered reward token** (`rewardTokensLength = rewards.length`), independent of what `_lps`/`_rewards` were passed by the caller, and for each token computes `receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` — the entire contract balance, not the amount actually attributable to `msg.sender`'s claim in this call. That full `receivedBalance` is then unconditionally forwarded to `msg.sender`, whether via `IConverter(_convertor).convertFor(receivedBalance, _convertRatio, _minRec, msg.sender, 2)`, `ILocker(_locker).lockFor(receivedBalance, msg.sender)`, `ISimpleHelper(_helperAddress).depositFor(receivedBalance, msg.sender)`, or a direct `safeTransfer(msg.sender, receivedBalance)`.

There is no accounting distinguishing "balance just claimed for `msg.sender`" from "any balance already sitting in the contract for any reason" (dust from prior conversions, tokens accidentally sent to the contract, or balances associated with other callers/reward flows). Any unprivileged address can call `compound()` with arbitrary or even empty `_lps`/`_rewards` arrays — `multiclaimOnBehalf` will simply claim nothing for them — yet the second loop still executes unconditionally and sweeps 100% of every registered reward token's balance to that caller.

When the configured convertor for a reward is `SmartWomConvert`, the swap-side protection is `if (convertAmount + amountRec < _minRec) revert MinRecNotMatch();` in `_convertFor` [3](#0-2) . Because `_minRec` is fully caller-controlled and applied to the whole swept `receivedBalance` (not the caller's own share), an attacker can set `_minRec = 0` to remove all slippage protection on the AMM leg (`IWombatRouter(router).swapExactTokensForTokens(..., 0, ...)` inside `_convertFor` [4](#0-3) ) while still directing the resulting `obtainedmWomAmount` — derived from the entire shared contract balance — to themselves, bypassing any check that would otherwise reconcile `balanceOf(address(this))` against the caller's true entitlement.

### Impact Explanation
Any balance of a registered reward token sitting in `ManualCompound` for reasons unrelated to the caller's own claim (residual dust, a prior transaction's leftover, or tokens intended for another user's compounding flow) can be fully swept and redirected to an unprivileged caller by simply invoking `compound()`, since the contract has no per-user balance segregation and uses `address(this)` balance as the sole basis for conversion amount and recipient. Combined with a zero `_minRec`, this also strips slippage protection from the underlying swap. This matches "Critical - Direct theft of user funds" since any value that reaches the contract's balance outside the caller's own immediate claim is not safe from an arbitrary subsequent caller.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged role is required — `compound()` is `external` with no access control [5](#0-4) . The attacker only needs to call `compound()` (with trivial or empty `_lps`/`_rewards`) whenever the contract holds any non-zero balance of a registered reward token that isn't already fully attributable to the caller in that same transaction. The precondition of a nonzero shared/residual balance existing at call time determines feasibility; the vulnerable code path itself is unconditionally reachable by any address.

### Recommendation
Track and process only the amount actually claimed for `msg.sender` in the current call (e.g., capture `balanceOf(address(this))` before and after `multiclaimOnBehalf`, and only act on the delta), rather than the full contract balance. Additionally, do not let a caller supply the sole slippage floor (`_minRec`) for a swap whose principal is not verifiably 100% theirs — derive amounts from claim receipts/events rather than raw token balance.

### Proof of Concept
Not applicable to fully validate without deploying the whole MasterMagpie/Wombat stack; a Hardhat PoC would: (1) deploy `ManualCompound` with a registered reward token and `SmartWomConvert` convertor, (2) seed the contract balance with that reward token from a source other than the attacker's own claim (e.g., simulate a stuck/residual transfer), (3) have an unprivileged attacker call `compound([], [], 0, 0, false)` (or minimal claim), and (4) assert the attacker's balance of the converted output token increases while `IERC20(rewardToken).balanceOf(ManualCompound)` and the attacker's rightful share no longer reconcile — confirming the loop at `rewards/ManualCompound.sol:139-160` swept unrelated balance to the caller.
