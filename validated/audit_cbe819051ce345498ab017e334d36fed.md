### Title
Full contract token balance swept to caller in `compound()` instead of caller's actual claimed delta - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`compound()` in `rewards/ManualCompound.sol` computes `receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` for every configured reward token and pays that entire balance to `msg.sender`, rather than only the amount produced by that caller's own `multiclaimOnBehalf` call. Any residual balance of a configured reward token left on the contract (e.g., from a prior partial compound, dust from rounding, or a directly-transferred token) is paid to the next arbitrary, unprivileged caller of `compound()`.

### Finding Description
`compound(address[] _lps, address[][] _rewards, uint256 _convertRatio, uint256 _minRec, bool _lockMgp)` first calls `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` using attacker-supplied `_lps`/`_rewards` [1](#0-0) . It then loops over the caller-supplied `_rewards` to refund non-compoundable tokens based on `balanceOf(address(this))` [2](#0-1) , and separately loops over the contract's own configured `rewards` array (state, independent of what the caller actually claimed) computing `receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` and sending/converting/locking/depositing that full balance for `msg.sender` [3](#0-2) .

Because `_lps` and `_rewards` are fully attacker-controlled, an unprivileged caller can pass empty or unrelated arrays to `multiclaimOnBehalf` (claiming nothing, or claiming from unrelated pools) and the second loop will still iterate over every token in the contract's persistent `rewards` list and forward whatever balance is currently sitting on the contract to `msg.sender`. There is no tracking of a pre/post-claim delta, no per-caller escrow, and no check that the balance being paid out corresponds to value this caller's claim produced. Any prior transaction that leaves a configured reward token balance on the contract (a previous caller's compound that reverted after `multiclaimOnBehalf` succeeded but before full payout, dust from conversion, or even an accidental/intentional direct `transfer` to the contract) becomes stealable by the next caller.

### Impact Explanation
This allows direct theft of user/protocol funds: any residual reward token balance on `ManualCompound` can be swept by an arbitrary caller who supplies unrelated or empty claim parameters, with no relationship enforced between what they claimed and what they receive. This matches Immunefi's Critical - Direct theft of user funds classification.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged role is required — any EOA or contract can call `compound()` with `_lps`/`_rewards` of their choosing (including empty arrays, since the loops are simply skipped when lengths are zero and the payout loop runs regardless based on `rewards.length`). The only precondition is a nonzero balance of a configured reward token being present on the contract, which can arise from normal operational residue (partial fills, rounding, a reverted downstream step) and is fully repeatable each time such residue accumulates.

### Recommendation
Compute the balance delta caused by this specific call: snapshot `balanceOf(address(this))` for each configured reward token before calling `multiclaimOnBehalf`, and after the call only distribute `postBalance - preBalance` (i.e., what this caller's claim actually produced), leaving any pre-existing balance untouched (or sweepable only by owner/rescue function). Apply the same delta-based accounting to the non-compoundable-reward refund loop.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `ManualCompound` with a mock `masterMagpie`, and register a reward token `RWD` with `addReward`.
2. Simulate residue: have the mock `masterMagpie.multiclaimOnBehalf` mint/transfer `100 RWD` to the `ManualCompound` contract as a side effect of an earlier legitimate call by `UserA` (or just directly transfer `100 RWD` to the contract to represent leftover dust from a prior partial compound).
3. From `UserB` (attacker, unprivileged, no prior claim entitlement), call `compound([], [], 0, 0, false)` — i.e., empty `_lps`/`_rewards` so `multiclaimOnBehalf` claims nothing for `UserB`.
4. Assert that after the call, `UserB`'s `RWD` balance increased by `100` (the full residual balance), while `UserB` claimed `0` via `multiclaimOnBehalf`.
5. Assert this violates the invariant: `amount received by UserB` != `amount actually claimed by UserB through multiclaimOnBehalf` (0), confirming residual-balance theft.
