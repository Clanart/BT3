### Title
PedPoP accepts identity group elements for encryption keys, per-message keys, and polynomial commitments — shares encrypted to a malicious participant use a publicly derivable ChaCha20 key, and PoK signatures become forgeable - (File: crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs, crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs, crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
The external report (CVE-2024-25674) is a missing-validation bug: attacker-supplied content is accepted without checking its type/validity. The analog in Serai is that `Ciphersuite::read_G` performs canonical-encoding checks but does not reject the identity point. PedPoP's message readers (`EncryptionKeyMessage::read`, `EncryptedMessage::read`, `Commitments::read`) all use `C::read_G` on untrusted wire bytes, so identity points pass validation everywhere they are used as ECDH public keys and signature public keys. FROST guards against this with `Curve::read_G` (rejects identity, crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131), but PedPoP never applies an equivalent check.

### Finding Description
`Ciphersuite::read_G` (crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-101) verifies only that the encoding is canonical; identity is a canonical encoding, so it is accepted.

Three untrusted-input paths consume it:

1. `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` reads each participant's registered ECDH public key `enc_key` via `C::read_G` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:57-59). This key is stored unverified in `Decryption::register` (encryption.rs:351-362) and later used in `encrypt`: `ecdh(key, to) = key * to` (encryption.rs:95-97, 154).

2. `EncryptedMessage::read` reads the per-message ECDH public `key` via `C::read_G` (encryption.rs:171-177), used by the recipient as `ecdh(enc_priv, msg.key)` in `decrypt` (encryption.rs:487).

3. `Commitments::read` reads all `t` Pedersen commitments via `C::read_G` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:115-125), and `commitments[0]` is then used as the public key for the proof-of-knowledge Schnorr signature in `verify_r1` (lib.rs:323-329).

Consequences of identity acceptance:

- **Identity `enc_key`**: `ecdh(key, identity) = identity` for every message. The cipher derives the ChaCha20 key from `transcript(context || identity.to_bytes())` with the static IV `b"DKG IV v0.2\0"` (encryption.rs:101-133). `context` is public and the IV is fixed, so the keystream is fully publicly computable. Every secret share `f_j(i_attacker)` that honest participants encrypt to the attacker is effectively plaintext to anyone who observes the ciphertexts — including ciphertexts exchanged with third parties during blame resolution (`AdditionalBlameMachine`, lib.rs:639-683). Summing the decrypted shares yields the attacker's complete `ThresholdKeys` secret share. Additionally, since all messages to that participant reuse the identical keystream (same key + same IV), it is a two-time pad: XOR of ciphertexts equals XOR of the share plaintexts, leaking share relationships even where full decryption is not wanted.

- **Identity `EncryptedMessage.key`**: `ecdh(enc_priv, identity) = identity`, so the ciphertext decrypts under the same public keystream, and the proof-of-possession `pop` is verified against public key `msg.key = identity` (encryption.rs:374-379, 479-485). A Schnorr signature under an identity public key is trivially forgeable: `s·G == R + c·0` for any `R = r·G`, `s = r`. The PoP check — added specifically to prevent the key-reuse blame side-channel documented at encryption.rs:84-90 — is vacuous for identity keys.

- **Identity `commitments[0]`**: the PedPoP proof of knowledge is verified as `msg.sig` under public key `commitments[0]` (lib.rs:327). If `commitments[0]` is identity, the PoK is forgeable by anyone (`s·G == R`), so the intended proof of knowledge of the polynomial's constant term is bypassed.

### Impact Explanation
The DKG's encryption layer exists to keep secret shares confidential so that blame proofs can selectively reveal a single message's ECDH key without exposing other messages, and so shares remain private even when ciphertexts are handed to third parties for blame arbitration. An unprivileged participant who registers `enc_key = identity` causes every share destined to them to be encrypted under a publicly derivable key with a reused IV — confidentiality of those shares is lost to any observer of the wire bytes or of blame-protocol transcripts, and the PoP mechanism designed to prevent blame-related key leakage is defeated because signatures under identity public keys are universally forgeable. This degrades the threshold security of the resulting key: any party obtaining the ciphertexts (e.g., a blame arbiter, a relay, or a logging layer) recovers a full participant share of the group secret.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the bug requires only that an attacker join a PedPoP DKG session and broadcast an `EncryptionKeyMessage` whose trailing `enc_key` bytes are the canonical identity encoding (e.g., 32 zero bytes for Ed25519's compressed identity). `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` → `Decryption::register` performs no identity check and no proof of possession on the registered encryption key, so honest participants will unconditionally encrypt their shares to it in `generate_secret_shares` (lib.rs:366-370). All bytes needed are attacker-controlled public inputs to `read`/`calculate_share`, within the permitted reachability surface.

### Recommendation
Reject the identity point wherever a group element is consumed as a public key or commitment in PedPoP — mirroring `Curve::read_G` (crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131): check `is_identity` after `C::read_G` in `EncryptionKeyMessage::read`, `EncryptedMessage::read`, `Commitments::read`, and `EncryptionKeyProof::read`. Consider additionally binding each registered `enc_key` to a proof of possession so participants cannot register keys they do not know the discrete log of.
