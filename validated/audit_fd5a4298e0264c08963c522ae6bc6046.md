### Title
PedPoP encryption key is unauthenticated by the proof of knowledge, enabling interception of a victim's secret shares and false blame against them - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
`EncryptionKeyMessage` carries a `Commitments` message plus a receiver encryption public key (`enc_key`). The Schnorr proof of knowledge that `verify_r1` uses to establish "message authenticity" is computed only over `cached_msg`, which is the concatenation of the polynomial commitments — `enc_key` is appended outside the signed content and is never bound into the PoK challenge. An attacker who can alter the delivered round‑1 message (the exact capability the PoK is documented to defeat) can substitute a victim participant's `enc_key` with their own, causing every other participant to encrypt the victim's secret shares to the attacker's key.

### Finding Description
In `KeyGenMachine::generate_coefficients`, `cached_msg` is built exclusively from `commitments[i].to_bytes()` and the PoK signature is produced as `challenge(context, i, R, cached_msg)` (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:163-184`). The `enc_key` is attached in `Encryption::registration` (`encryption.rs:448-450`) and serialized after the `Commitments` body (`encryption.rs:61-64`), so it is not covered by `cached_msg` or the signature.

In `SecretShareMachine::verify_r1`, `self.encryption.register(l, msg)` stores the unauthenticated `msg.enc_key` under the victim's `Participant` index before the PoK is batch-verified (`lib.rs:315-329`). Later, `Encryption::encrypt` encrypts each recipient's `SecretShare` to `self.decryption.enc_keys[&participant]` (`encryption.rs:460-467`, `135-168`), deriving the ChaCha20 key via ECDH between a per-message key and the registered `enc_key`.

Because the registered `enc_key` was never authenticated, an attacker substituting their own public key learns every `SecretShare` destined for the victim: they compute `ecdh(their_enc_key, msg.key)` exactly as the victim would (`encryption.rs:487-488`) and decrypt the share.

### Impact Explanation
The attacker recovers the sum of all secret shares addressed to the victim — i.e. the victim's final threshold secret share, the same secret that `calculate_share` accumulates into `self.secret` (`lib.rs:484`). This is key share recovery. Worse, the honest victim decrypts garbage (their real `enc_key` no longer matches the registered one), fails `share_verification_statements`, and produces a blame `EncryptionKeyProof` whose DLEq is verified against `self.enc_keys[&decryptor]` — the attacker's substituted key (`encryption.rs:383-390`). The proof check fails with `InvalidProof`, so the honest victim is identified as the faulty party and slashed. This mirrors CVE-2019-9733: an attacker-controlled field (`enc_key`, like `X-Forwarded-For`) is trusted as belonging to a privileged identity, defeating the authenticity check that was supposed to bind it.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the ability to deliver a modified round-1 `EncryptionKeyMessage` for the victim — precisely the threat model the PoK's "message authenticity" claim (`lib.rs:298`) is meant to address. In deployments where the commitment messages are relayed by a coordinator/mempool that treats the Schnorr PoK as the authenticity proof rather than requiring an externally authenticated channel, a single malicious participant or relay performs the substitution once and compromises the entire DKG for that victim.

### Recommendation
Bind `enc_key` into the authenticated content. Either include `enc_key.to_bytes()` in `cached_msg` before computing `challenge()` in `generate_coefficients`, or extend `challenge()`/`Commitments` to cover the encryption key. At minimum, document that the PoK does not authenticate `enc_key` and that the channel must authenticate it independently.

### Proof of Concept
1. Participants run `KeyGenMachine::generate_coefficients`; victim `A` broadcasts `EncryptionKeyMessage { msg: Commitments { commitments, cached_msg, sig }, enc_key: A_enc_pub }`.
2. Attacker `E` (a DKG participant or relay) delivers to each other participant a modified message for index `A`: identical `Commitments` (so `challenge(context, A, sig.R, cached_msg)` still verifies, since `enc_key` is outside `cached_msg`), but `enc_key` replaced with `E_enc_pub` for which `E` knows `e`.
3. `verify_r1` accepts the message: `register(A, msg)` stores `E_enc_pub` under `A` (`encryption.rs:360`), and the PoK batch verifies successfully.
4. Every participant calls `generate_secret_shares`, encrypting `polynomial(coeffs, A)` via `cipher(context, ecdh(key, E_enc_pub))` and sends the `EncryptedMessage` to `A`'s slot.
5. `E` computes `shared = E_enc_pub * e`-equivalent `ecdh(e, msg.key)`, applies `cipher`, and reads each `SecretShare` plaintext; summing them yields `A`'s secret share — key share recovery.
6. `A` calls `calculate_share`; decryption yields garbage, `from_repr` fails, `A` publishes `blame`. `decrypt_with_proof` verifies `proof.dleq` against `enc_keys[A] = E_enc_pub` (`encryption.rs:388`), which does not match `A`'s real `enc_pub_key`, so the blame proof is rejected and `A` is marked faulty — an honest participant is slashed while the attacker holds their secret share.
