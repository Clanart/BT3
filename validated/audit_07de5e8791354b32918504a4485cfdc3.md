### Title
Unrestricted, permissionless `harvestSinglePool` combined with instant vote-stake accounting allows just-in-time vote reallocation to capture a disproportionate share of freshly-harvested bribes - (wombat/WombatBribeManager.sol)

### Summary
`harvestSinglePool` is a public, feeless function that anyone can call to force `wombatStaking.vote()` with zero deltas, which triggers an immediate harvest of accrued Wombat bribes into the pool's `BribeRewardPool` via `queueNewRewards` [1](#0-0) . Because `vote()` updates a voter's staked balance in the `BribeRewardPool` instantly and without any lock-up or time-weighting [2](#0-1) , and because `_provisionReward` distributes the newly harvested amount pro-rata over the *current* `totalStaked()` at the moment of harvest [3](#0-2) , an attacker can reallocate existing vlMGP votes into a target pool immediately before calling `harvestSinglePool`, and capture reward proportional to their large new stake even though they held no vote share during the period the underlying Wombat bribe accrued.

### Finding Description
The premise in the question that claim *order* (attacker calling `claimBribe` before other users) affects payout amounts is incorrect: `BribeRewardPool`/`BaseRewardPoolV2` use an accumulator (`rewardPerTokenStored`) pattern where `earned()` is `balance * (rewardPerToken - userRewardPerTokenPaid) + userRewards` [4](#0-3) . A user's payout is fixed once the accumulator is updated and does not depend on whether other voters have claimed yet, so front-running the *claim* call itself yields no extra funds.

However, the actual reachable exploit is a just-in-time (JIT) stake/harvest/unstake sequence, matching the call sequence given (`vote(+X)` → `harvestSinglePool` → `claimBribe`):
1. Attacker holds vlMGP already allocated to some pool(s), or has unused votable power.
2. Attacker calls `vote(pool, +X)`, which immediately calls `IBribeRewardPool(pool.rewarder).stakeFor(msg.sender, X)`, increasing `totalSupply` and the attacker's `_balances` before any new reward is added [5](#0-4) .
3. Attacker (or anyone) calls `harvestSinglePool([pool])`, which is public/unauthenticated and passes `address(0)` as caller (no cooldown, no access control, no minimum holding time) [6](#0-5) . This forces `wombatStaking.vote()` to harvest Wombat's pending bribe (accrued at the Wombat protocol level over the entire time since the last harvest) and push it into `queueNewRewards` on the pool rewarder [7](#0-6) .
4. `_provisionReward` computes `rewardPerTokenStored += reward * 10**decimals / totalStaked()`, where `totalStaked()` now includes the attacker's freshly added `X` [8](#0-7) . The attacker's `userRewardPerTokenPaid` was checkpointed at stake time (before this rate increase), so the attacker's full new balance earns the entire rate delta for this harvest, i.e. `X * reward / (T + X)`.
5. Attacker calls `claimBribe`/`getReward` to pull out `X/(T+X)` of the harvested reward and can immediately re-vote with a negative delta to withdraw the stake (`vote(pool, -X)`), since `vote()` has no lock/cooldown [9](#0-8) .

Because the Wombat-level bribe accrual is time-based and independent of Magpie's internal per-user vote balances, existing voters who held their vote through the entire accrual window get diluted: their share of the harvested reward becomes `B*reward/(T+X)` instead of `B*reward/T`, i.e., a portion of the accrued yield that should have gone to them is captured by the attacker's momentary, undiluted late stake. No existing modifier (`onlyOperator`, `updateRewards`) prevents this because these mechanisms correctly checkpoint balances at stake/withdraw time but do not enforce any minimum holding duration or time-weighting of stake relative to reward accrual, and `harvestSinglePool` has zero access restriction or fee to discourage repeated forced harvesting.

### Impact Explanation
This is a theft of unclaimed/accruing yield from long-term voters: an attacker with vlMGP voting power (no special privileges) can time a large vote reallocation immediately before triggering a harvest to skim a disproportionate share of bribes that accrued while they held no vote share in that pool, then withdraw. This matches the "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class, quantifiable as `X/(T+X) * reward` extracted per exploit cycle, repeatable every time meaningful Wombat bribes have accrued for a pool.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the attacker to hold/lock vlMGP (the standard precondition for voting) and to be able to reallocate votes across pools and call `harvestSinglePool`, which is public and unrestricted [6](#0-5) . No special role, flash loan, or reentrancy is needed — just monitoring pending bribe accrual (`previewBribes`/`previewBnbAmountForHarvest`) [10](#0-9)  and timing a vote move plus a harvest call in the same block, which is trivially repeatable and cheap (no fee for the harvester since `caller = address(0)`).

### Recommendation
Introduce time-weighted accounting (e.g., checkpoint reward accrual so that stake added in the same block/epoch as a harvest does not participate in that harvest's distribution), or require a minimum holding period/epoch boundary before newly staked votes become eligible for the next bribe distribution, similar to a "cooldown" or "harvest-then-stake" ordering guarantee. Alternatively, restrict `harvestSinglePool` so it cannot be triggered arbitrarily right after a vote change within the same block/transaction by the same address, or snapshot `totalStaked()`/eligible balances at the start of the accrual period rather than at harvest time.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `WombatBribeManager`, `WombatStaking`, and a `BribeRewardPool` for a pool, with mocked `voter.vote()` and `IWombatBribe` returning a fixed pending bribe amount that grows over time (simulate several blocks of accrual).
2. Voter A votes `100` vlMGP on the pool at time `T0` and does nothing else.
3. Advance time so the Wombat mock accrues `R` bribe tokens for the pool.
4. Attacker votes `900` vlMGP on the same pool at `T1` (just before harvest), then immediately calls `harvestSinglePool([pool])`.
5. Attacker calls `claimBribe([pool])` and records payout; assert attacker receives `900/1000 * R` despite holding votes for effectively 0 time during the accrual window.
6. Voter A calls `claimBribe([pool])` and asserts they receive only `100/1000 * R`, i.e., far less than the `R` (or near-`R`) they would have received had the harvest occurred before the attacker's vote was added — demonstrating dilution/disproportionate capture and quantifying the diverted yield.

    /// @notice Cast a zero vote to harvest the bribes of selected pools
    /// @notice this  function has a lesser importance than casting votes, hence no rewards will be given to the caller.
    function harvestSinglePool(address[] calldata _lps) public {
        uint256 length = _lps.length;
        int256[] memory votes = new int256[](length);
        address[] memory rewarders = new address[](length);
        for (uint256 i; i < length; i++) {
            address lp = _lps[i];
            Pool storage pool = poolInfos[lp];
            rewarders[i] = pool.rewarder;
            votes[i] = 0;
        }
        wombatStaking.vote(_lps, votes, rewarders, address(0));
    }
