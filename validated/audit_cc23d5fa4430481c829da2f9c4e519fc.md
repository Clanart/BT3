### Title
Fee/vsize accounting mismatch: OP_RETURN output excluded from `calculate_weight_vbytes`, so `needed_fee` and the minimum-relay check are computed on a smaller transaction than the one actually built - ([File: networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs](networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs))

### Summary
The external bug is a mismatch between a rounded-down derived value (`shares`) and the user-supplied value (`amount`) actually used for payout — the deducted quantity is cheaper than the delivered quantity. The same structural flaw exists in `SignableTransaction::new`: the transaction's real outputs (including a caller-supplied OP_RETURN `data` output of up to ~90+ vbytes) are pushed into `tx_outs`, but `calculate_weight_vbytes` is called with `payments` only, ignoring the OP_RETURN output. The fee actually embedded in the transaction (`input_sat - payment_sat - change`) is therefore too small for the transaction's true vsize, and the `DEFAULT_MIN_RELAY_TX_FEE` check validates a vsize smaller than reality.

### Finding Description
In `SignableTransaction::new`, `data` creates an OP_RETURN `TxOut` appended to `tx_outs` before the weight/vbytes computation, but `calculate_weight_vbytes` reconstructs a transaction solely from `payments` (and optionally `change`), so the OP_RETURN's weight is never counted [1](#0-0) . `needed_fee = fee_per_vbyte * vbytes` and the min-relay floor both use this understated `vbytes` [2](#0-1) . When change is created, its value is `input_sat - payment_sat - fee_with_change`, where `fee_with_change` is likewise computed without the OP_RETURN output [3](#0-2) . The final `Transaction` includes the OP_RETURN output [4](#0-3) , so the realized fee rate is strictly below `fee_per_vbyte` and can fall below the minimum relay fee even though the `TooLowFee` guard passed.

### Impact Explanation
A transaction built with a large `data` payload (up to 80 bytes, ~90+ vbytes of unaccounted weight) will pay a fee materially below the intended `fee_per_vbyte`, and can be below `DEFAULT_MIN_RELAY_TX_FEE` on its true vsize. Such a transaction will not be relayed/confirmed by standard nodes, so the spend stalls: funds committed to the inputs are not spendable until a corrected transaction is constructed and re-signed, and any completion confirmation keyed to this txid will never observe it. Like the staking bug, the amount "charged" (fee deducted via inputs minus outputs) is smaller than what the delivered artifact (the actual larger transaction) requires.

### Likelihood Explanation
`data` is attacker-influenced in flows where the payment's OP_RETURN encodes user-supplied/metadata content, and `fee_per_vbyte` near the minimum relay rate makes the shortfall decisive. No special privileges are needed — just a payment plan carrying a maximal OP_RETURN while the quoted fee is marginal.

### Recommendation
Include the OP_RETURN output (and any other `tx_outs` entries) in the transaction built inside `calculate_weight_vbytes`, or compute weight/vbytes from the actual `tx_outs` vector. Also apply a `DUST`-style sanity that the realized fee rate (`fee() / actual_vbytes`) meets `fee_per_vbyte` and the minimum relay floor on the final transaction.

### Proof of Concept
- Let `fee_per_vbyte` equal the minimum relay rate (1 sat/vB) and craft `payments` + `data` of 80 bytes.
- `calculate_weight_vbytes` omits the OP_RETURN; suppose it returns `vbytes = 200`, so `needed_fee = 200` and the min-relay check `200 >= (1000 * 200)/1000` passes [5](#0-4) .
- The real transaction additionally carries an OP_RETURN output (~93 extra bytes ≈ 93 vbytes of non-witness weight ~ 372 WU ≈ +93 vB), so its actual vsize is ~293 vB while paying only ~200 sat (or change reduced to `input_sat - payment_sat - fee_with_change`, still ~200 sat fee) [6](#0-5) .
- Realized fee rate ≈ 200/293 ≈ 0.68 sat/vB < 1 sat/vB → non-relayable despite the `TooLowFee` check passing; the signed transaction is unspendable-in-practice and must be rebuilt.
