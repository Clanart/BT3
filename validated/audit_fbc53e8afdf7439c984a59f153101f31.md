### Title
DKG secret shares are encrypted to unauthenticated, PoP-less recipient encryption keys, letting a participant redirect their share to a key controlled by anyone - (File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs)

### Summary
Analogous to `MatrixClient.sendSharedHistoryKeys` sharing room key history to unverified devices, Serai's PedPoP DKG sends each participant's FROST secret share encrypted to a long-term `enc_key` that the recipient publishes with **no proof of possession or identity binding**. `EncryptionKeyMessage.enc_key` is a bare group element read directly off the wire and registered unconditionally, so any DKG participant can publish an encryption key whose discrete logarithm is known to an external party (e.g., a key copied from another session, or one whose secret was deliberately revealed). Every honest peer will then encrypt that participant's share to attacker-controlled key material, leaking a threshold key share.

### Finding Description
Each participant generates a long-term encryption keypair in `Encryption::new` and broadcasts only the public half in `EncryptionKeyMessage { msg, enc_key }` via `Encryption::registration` (`encryption.rs:448-450`). There is no Schnorr PoK, no signature, and no transcript binding over `enc_key` — the only proof-of-possession in the protocol is the PoK over `coefficients[0]` inside `Commitments` (`lib.rs:173-184`) and the per-message `pop` inside `EncryptedMessage` (`encryption.rs:159-167`), which protects the *ephemeral* key, not the registered one.

On receipt, `Decryption::register` (`encryption.rs:351-362`) inserts `msg.enc_key` into `enc_keys` with no verification whatsoever. Later, `Encryption::encrypt` (`encryption.rs:460-467`) encrypts the victim's `SecretShare` via `ecdh(random_key, enc_keys[participant])` (`encryption.rs:95-97, 153-154`) and the share is dispatched to `participant` in `generate_secret_shares` (`lib.rs:357-370`).

A malicious participant `m` can therefore register `enc_key = K_ext` where the scalar `k_ext` behind `K_ext` is known to an attacker `A` who is *not* `m` (e.g., `A` is another party in the multisig, or an observer to whom `m` disclosed `k_ext`). All `n-1` honest senders encrypt `m`'s secret share `f_j(m)` to `K_ext`. `A` decrypts every `f_j(m)` copy and recovers `m`'s share. Combined with the shares held by `t-1` colluding/compromised participants — or simply accumulating stolen shares across repeated DKG sessions — `A` reconstructs the group secret key without ever producing a signature.

### Impact Explanation
Recovery of a FROST secret share (and, with `t` total shares, full group private key recovery — the accepted "key share recovery" impact). The DKG's confidentiality guarantee — that `f_j(m)` is known only to `m` — is broken purely through a public `EncryptionKeyMessage` field, exactly mirroring how Matrix sent key material to device keys it never authenticated.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker only needs to be a DKG participant (or collude with one) and submit a crafted `enc_key` in the broadcast `EncryptionKeyMessage`, which every honest peer parses via `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` → `C::read_G` (`encryption.rs:57-59`) and registers without question. No cryptographic failure, collusion beyond key disclosure, or protocol deviation is detectable — the share ciphertext is perfectly well-formed; it's just addressed to the wrong private key. Severity: High.

### Recommendation
Include a proof of possession for `enc_key` inside `EncryptionKeyMessage` — e.g., a `SchnorrSignature` over `(context, i, enc_key, commitments)` verified in `verify_r1`/`register` — so a participant cannot register a public key whose discrete log they don't know. This mirrors the fix applied for the per-message key (`pop` in `EncryptedMessage`), which already solves the identical co-opted-key problem documented at `encryption.rs:83-90`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker `A` generates scalar `a`, computes `K_ext = G*a`, and gives `K_ext` to malicious participant `m` (or `m` is `A` itself).
2. `m` runs `KeyGenMachine::generate_coefficients`, then overwrites the `enc_key` field of its `EncryptionKeyMessage` with `K_ext` before broadcasting. `Decryption::register` in every honest peer stores `K_ext` for `m` (`encryption.rs:360`).
3. Each honest peer `j` calls `Encryption::encrypt(rng, m, share_j(m))` → `ecdh(random, K_ext)` (`encryption.rs:154, 466`). `A`, holding `a`, computes `a * msg.key` = the same ECDH point, derives the ChaCha20 key via `cipher(context, ecdh)` (`encryption.rs:101-133`), and decrypts `f_j(m)` for every `j`.
4. `A` now knows `m`'s share; combined with `t-1` other known shares it interpolates the group secret key via the same `polynomial`/`Interpolation::Lagrange` math in `lib.rs`.
