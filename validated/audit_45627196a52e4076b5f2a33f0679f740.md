### Title
Front-running `castVotes` with `vote()` to inject last-second stake into `BribeRewardPool` and steal a share of freshly-harvested bribes - (File: rewards/BribeRewardPool.sol, wombat/WombatBribeManager.sol)

### Summary
`WombatBribeManager.castVotes` harvests bribes and immediately injects them into each pool's `BribeRewardPool` via `queueNewRewards`, which updates `rewardPerTokenStored` as a lump sum proportional to `totalStaked()` at that exact block [1](#0-0) . Because reward accrual is not time-streamed, an unprivileged holder of vlMGP capacity can call `vote()` to inject a large delta into `userVotedForPoolInVlmgp`/`BribeRewardPool.totalSupply` in the same block immediately before `castVotes` executes, capturing a pro-rata share of the just-harvested bribe despite having staked for zero time, then withdraw and claim in a following transaction.

### Finding Description
`WombatBribeManager.vote()` lets any account with unlocked vlMGP votable capacity (`getUserVotable`) call `stakeFor` on the pool's `BribeRewardPool` with a positive delta [2](#0-1) . `BribeRewardPool.stakeFor` runs the `updateRewards` modifier — which snapshots `userRewardPerTokenPaid` at the *current* `rewardPerTokenStored` (before any new rewards are added) — and only afterwards increases `totalSupply` and the user's `_balances` [3](#0-2) , [4](#0-3) .

When `castVotes` subsequently runs, it calls `wombatStaking.vote`, which harvests bribes and calls `IBaseRewardPool(_rewarders[i]).queueNewRewards(rewardAmount, ...)` for each pool [5](#0-4) . `queueNewRewards`/`_provisionReward` immediately updates `rewardPerTokenStored += (_amountReward * 10**decimals) / totalStaked()` using the *current* `totalStaked()`, which now includes the attacker's just-injected delta [6](#0-5) . Since the attacker's `userRewardPerTokenPaid` was snapshotted *before* this reward injection (at the old, lower `rewardPerTokenStored`), `earned()` for the attacker becomes nonzero immediately: `_earned = userShare * (newRewardPerToken - oldRewardPerTokenPaid) / 1e_decimals` [7](#0-6) . The attacker can then call `unvote`/`vote` with a negative delta to withdraw the injected stake and `claimBribe`/`claimBribeFor` to realize the reward [8](#0-7) , [9](#0-8) .

No mechanism (time-weighting, streaming/`rewardRate`+`periodFinish`, minimum staking duration, or cooldown between vote/unvote) exists to prevent instantaneous capture of a lump-sum reward injection. The `updateRewards`/`updateReward` modifiers correctly checkpoint state but do not protect against a stake injected in the same block as a reward deposit — this is a design flaw in the reward-per-token accounting rather than a missing check that could otherwise block the sequence.

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of unclaimed yield: honest long-term voters who have been accruing votes/stake in a pool for a long period see their share of the newly harvested bribe diluted, while an attacker who stakes for effectively zero economic time captures a disproportionate cut of the same distribution. This matches the "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class referenced in scope. The magnitude scales with (a) the size of bribe harvested per `castVotes` call and (b) the attacker's injected delta relative to existing `totalSupply` in that specific pool's `BribeRewardPool` — an attacker with enough vlMGP votable capacity to dwarf the existing pool's total votes can capture close to 100% of that pool's harvested bribe.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: the attacker must hold (or lock) vlMGP capacity reported by `getUserVotable` sufficient to inject a large delta relative to the target pool's existing `totalVoteInVlmgp`/`totalSupply`; `castVotes` (or `voteAndCast`/`castVotesAndClaimBribes`) must be public and callable by anyone, which it is [10](#0-9) , so the attacker can either front-run a victim's pending `castVotes` transaction or simply call `voteAndCast` themselves in one transaction to guarantee the sequence `vote(+X)` → harvest/distribute → is atomic. This makes the attack fully self-executable without needing to actually front-run anyone, and repeatable every time meaningful bribes accumulate in a pool. Capital requirement is proportional to the desired share of the bribe and is returned immediately after unvoting (only opportunity cost of temporarily allocating vlMGP votes, not principal loss).

### Recommendation
Change reward accrual so that instantly-added lump-sum rewards cannot be claimed by stake added in the same block/transaction as the distribution: (1) stream rewards over time via a `rewardRate`/`periodFinish` model (Synthetix-style) instead of updating `rewardPerTokenStored` as an instantaneous jump, and/or (2) add a minimum holding period or per-block snapshot (e.g., checkpoint balances at the start of the block/epoch before harvesting) so that `stakeFor` calls executed in the same block/epoch as `castVotes` do not count toward that epoch's distribution, and/or (3) disallow `vote`/`unvote` calls in the same block as `castVotes`, or process `castVotes` using pool balances snapshotted prior to any vote changes in the current epoch.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test outline:
1. Deploy `WombatBribeManager`, `BribeRewardPool`, mock `WombatStaking`/`voter`/vlMGP contracts. Fund two users: `victim` (locks vlMGP and votes for pool P well before harvest, accruing time-weighted stake) and `attacker` (locks vlMGP but does not vote yet).
2. Simulate bribe accrual on the mock Wombat voter so that a `castVotes(false)` call will harvest a known `rewardAmount` for pool P.
3. In a single transaction (or two transactions in the same block), have `attacker` call `wombatBribeManager.vote([P], [+X])` with `X` large relative to `victim`'s existing vote, then immediately call `castVotes(false)` (or use `voteAndCast`).
4. Assert `BribeRewardPool(P.rewarder).earned(attacker, rewardToken) > 0` immediately after step 3, despite `attacker` having staked for 0 blocks prior to the reward.
5. Have `attacker` call `unvote(P)` (or `vote([P],[-X])`) followed by `claimBribe([P])`, and assert `attacker` receives more reward token than their pro-rata time-weighted share (i.e., `attacker`'s earned/received amount computed vs. `victim`'s equivalent share for equal staked amount but full accrual period) — showing `attacker`'s effective yield rate per unit stake vastly exceeds `victim`'s despite zero holding time, confirming dilution/theft of `victim`'s unclaimed yield share.
