### Title
Permissionless `donateRewards` lets an attacker sandwich queued/pending reward flushes to steal disproportionate share of unclaimed yield - (File: rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol)

### Summary
`donateRewards(uint256 _amountReward, address _rewardToken)` is callable by anyone and internally calls `_provisionReward`, which folds `rewards[token].queuedRewards` into `rewardPerTokenStored` scaled by `totalStaked()` at the exact block of the call. Because there is no time-weighting, cooldown, or snapshotting tied to when the reward actually accrued, an attacker can inflate their own stake immediately before triggering the flush (via `donateRewards` with as little as 1 wei, or simply by front-running a pending `queueNewRewards` transaction) and capture a disproportionate share of the backlog/incoming reward relative to the long-term stakers who actually earned it.

### Finding Description
`_provisionReward` computes the reward increment as: [1](#0-0) 

When `totalStaked() == 0`, incoming rewards accumulate in `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` instead of being distributed. The very next call to `_provisionReward` (from either `queueNewRewards` — manager-only — or `donateRewards` — permissionless) that observes `totalStaked() != 0` folds the *entire* backlog into `rewardPerTokenStored`, dividing by whatever `totalStaked()` is **at that instant**: [2](#0-1) 

`donateRewards` has no access control beyond the reward token being registered: [3](#0-2) 

Because `totalStaked()` reads live `IERC20(stakingToken).balanceOf(operator)` [4](#0-3) , and rewards are split purely proportional to balance *at the moment of the flush* (not integrated over the period the reward was earned), a staker's share of a newly-released reward (queued backlog or a manager's fresh `queueNewRewards` amount) depends only on their fraction of `totalStaked()` in that single block. An attacker can:
1. Observe a reward-manager `queueNewRewards` transaction pending in the mempool, or observe that `queuedRewards[token] > 0` already sits unflushed.
2. Front-run by staking a large amount of `stakingToken` into the operator (MasterMagpie), inflating `totalStaked()` so they dominate it.
3. Either let the manager's `queueNewRewards` transaction land, or self-trigger the flush immediately with `donateRewards(1, _rewardToken)` (attacker controls `_amountReward` down to 1 wei and which registered token to flush).
4. Because `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` and/or the manager's new amount is divided by the attacker-dominated `totalStaked()`, and the attacker's own balance is the majority of that denominator, the attacker's `earned()` (`balanceOf * Δ rewardPerToken`) captures a disproportionate share of the distribution that should have accrued to previously-staked, long-term holders.
5. The attacker can withdraw immediately afterward, since nothing in `_provisionReward`/`donateRewards` enforces a minimum holding period or time-weighted accounting tied to when the reward was actually earned.

There is no `nonReentrant`, no per-block/staking-duration gate, and no restriction on `_amountReward` (can be 1 wei) or caller identity in `donateRewards`, so nothing stops an attacker from choosing the exact timing of the flush to align with their own inflated stake.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the intended invariant that a reward backlog accrued over time should be distributed to the stake set that actually held positions during that period, not to whoever happens to hold the largest balance at the single block the flush occurs. This directly enables theft of unclaimed/queued yield from legitimate long-term stakers, matching the "High - Theft of unclaimed yield" Immunefi impact class. The magnitude of loss scales with the size of `queuedRewards` backlog and/or the manager's `queueNewRewards` amount, and with how much capital the attacker can temporarily commit (including via flash-style large deposits, subject to whatever transfer/deposit path MasterMagpie exposes).

### Likelihood Explanation
- Precondition: reward manager's `queueNewRewards` pending in mempool, or `queuedRewards[token] > 0` from a period when `totalStaked() == 0`, is a realistic and recurring situation in an active pool operations cadence.
- Capital required: only the capital needed to temporarily dominate `totalStaked()`, which can be arbitrarily small in a newly-launched or low-TVL pool (the class of pool most likely to have periods where `totalStaked() == 0` and backlog builds up).
- No special role or governance is needed — `donateRewards` is explicitly permissionless and unmetered (`_amountReward` can be 1 wei).
- Fully repeatable each time a backlog/queue event coincides with an attacker's ability to transiently dominate stake.

### Recommendation
- Do not allow an instantaneous, single-block snapshot of `totalStaked()` to gate distribution of backlog rewards. Track a time-weighted or checkpointed stake basis (e.g., accrue `queuedRewards` release over a vesting/streaming window, or require the reward to be distributed based on stake integrated over the accrual period rather than the stake present in the flushing block).
- Alternatively/additionally, restrict `donateRewards` to be non-zero-impact on `queuedRewards` folding (i.e., only allow manager-initiated `queueNewRewards` to flush the backlog, and make `donateRewards` only ever add new rewards to be streamed over time rather than instantly triggering `queuedRewards` release), or add a minimum-stake-duration/cooldown before a depositor's stake counts toward reward distribution to prevent flash/instant sandwich capture.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat test outline:
1. Deploy `BaseRewardPool` with `stakingToken`, `rewardToken`, mocked `MasterMagpie` as `operator`, and a `rewardManager`.
2. Simulate a period with zero total stake: have `rewardManager` call `queueNewRewards(1000e18, rewardToken)` while `IERC20(stakingToken).balanceOf(operator) == 0`, causing `rewards[rewardToken].queuedRewards == 1000e18`.
3. Have a "victim" stake a modest amount (e.g., 10e18) through the mocked operator, increasing `totalStaked()` to 10e18, without yet triggering any `_provisionReward` call (so backlog remains unflushed).
4. Have an attacker stake a large amount (e.g., 990e18) through the mocked operator immediately before flushing, inflating `totalStaked()` to 1000e18.
5. Attacker calls `donateRewards(1, rewardToken)`.
6. Assert: `rewardPerTokenStored` increment is `1000e18/1000e18 = 1e18` (scaled), and attacker's `earned(attacker, rewardToken)` ≈ 990e18 * 1e18/1e18 ≈ 99% of the entire backlog, versus victim's `earned(victim, rewardToken)` ≈ only 1% — despite the backlog having accrued entirely before the attacker ever staked.
7. Assert the invariant fails: the backlog was released against a stake set (990e18 attacker + 10e18 victim) assembled by the attacker one block earlier, not the stake set present while the reward accrued (victim only, or nobody).

    /// @param _amountReward Amount of reward token to be distributed
    /// @param _rewardToken Address reward token
    function queueNewRewards(uint256 _amountReward, address _rewardToken)
        override
        external
        onlyManager
        returns (bool)
    {
        if (!isRewardToken[_rewardToken]) {
            rewardTokens.push(_rewardToken);
            isRewardToken[_rewardToken] = true;
        }

        _provisionReward(_amountReward, _rewardToken);
        return true;
    }

    /// @notice Sends new rewards to be distributed to the users staking. Only possible to donate already registered token
    /// @param _amountReward Amount of reward token to be distributed
    /// @param _rewardToken Address reward token
    function donateRewards(uint256 _amountReward, address _rewardToken) external {
        if (!isRewardToken[_rewardToken])
            revert MustBeRewardToken();

        _provisionReward(_amountReward, _rewardToken);
    }
