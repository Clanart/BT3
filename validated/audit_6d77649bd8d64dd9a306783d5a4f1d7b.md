### Title
Missing identity-point rejection in `EncryptedMessage::read`/`EncryptionKeyMessage` registration allows a forged Schnorr proof-of-possession and a degenerate, publicly derivable ECDH key - ([File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs])

### Summary
The bug class of CVE-2023-26852 — a crafted uploaded artifact being accepted and processed as trusted content — maps onto Serai's PedPoP DKG encryption layer: `EncryptedMessage::read` deserializes the per-message encryption key with `Ciphersuite::read_G`, which checks canonical encoding but does *not* reject the identity point (`crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-101`). This contrasts with `Curve::read_G` in FROST, which explicitly rejects identity (`crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131`), showing the codebase knows this check matters. An identity `key` makes (a) the attached Schnorr PoP trivially forgeable, and (b) the ECDH shared point degenerate to identity, so the ChaCha20 keystream is publicly computable.

### Finding Description
`EncryptedMessage::read` reads `key: C::read_G(reader)?` with no identity check (`crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:171-177`). Two consequences:

1. **Forged PoP.** `pop` is a `SchnorrSignature` verified against `msg.key` as the public key (`decrypt`/`decrypt_with_proof`, encryption.rs:374-379, 479-485). With `public_key = identity`, `SchnorrSignature::batch_statements` computes `R + c·A - sG = 0` where `c·A = 0`, so any signature `R = s·G` for an arbitrarily chosen scalar `s` verifies — a forgery requiring no private key (`crypto/schnorr/src/lib.rs:88-99`).

2. **Degenerate encryption.** `ecdh(private, identity) = identity` (encryption.rs:95-97), so `cipher(context, identity)` derives a keystream from a constant public point — the "encrypted" secret share is readable by anyone, and a sender can produce a ciphertext that decrypts to any chosen plaintext without knowing the recipient's `enc_key`. Additionally, `decrypt_with_proof` runs a DLEq over generators `[G, msg.key]`; with `msg.key = identity`, the second element `x·identity = proof.key` is unbinding, so an accuser-supplied `EncryptionKeyProof` can carry an arbitrary `proof.key` and make the message decrypt to arbitrary bytes during blame adjudication (`blame_internal`, `crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:582-608`).

The same gap applies to `EncryptionKeyMessage.enc_key`, which `Decryption::register` stores without validation (encryption.rs:351-362): a participant registering `enc_key = identity` causes all shares "encrypted to them" by honest parties (`ecdh(fresh_key, identity) = identity`, encryption.rs:460-467) to be protected by a publicly known keystream — breaking confidentiality of the share channel, which is the stated purpose of the encryption layer.

### Impact Explanation
- The Schnorr proof-of-possession attached to an `EncryptedMessage` can be forged outright under a key with a discrete log the sender does not know (identity), defeating the mechanism whose entire purpose is to bind a message to a party that knows a key (encryption.rs:83-91 explains why the PoP exists).
- Shares "protected" by ECDH with an identity point are effectively plaintext to any observer — a confidentiality break of the secret-share channel for the DKG.
- In `VerifyBlame` handling (`processor/src/key_gen.rs:504-549` → `BlameMachine::blame`), an identity `msg.key` makes the accuser's `EncryptionKeyProof` DLEq unbinding on its second component, so the "decrypted" share content used to assign blame is attacker-controlled rather than cryptographically bound to the ciphertext.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by an unprivileged party with public inputs: every `EncryptedMessage` and `EncryptionKeyMessage` is attacker-supplied bytes parsed via `read` in `processor/src/key_gen.rs` (lines 268-274, 409, 508-517). The attack only requires the attacker to be a DKG participant (or supply bytes to blame verification) and choose a specific point encoding — no collusion, leaked keys, or broken BFT assumptions needed. Impact is bounded: the forged PoP chiefly degrades blame-attribution and share confidentiality rather than directly yielding the group secret (the sender still only injects values consistent with their own commitments, else they are blamed), which caps severity at Medium.

### Recommendation
Reject the identity point when deserializing keys used for ECDH/PoP: either add an `is_identity` check to `Ciphersuite::read_G` (matching `Curve::read_G` at `crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:123-131`), or check `msg.key.is_identity()`/`enc_key.is_identity()` in `EncryptedMessage::read`, `Decryption::register`, and `decrypt_with_proof`. At minimum, reject identity in `pop_challenge`-verified keys and registered encryption keys.

### Proof of Concept
1. As DKG participant `l`, craft `EncryptedMessage` bytes: `key` = canonical encoding of the identity point (e.g., all-zero bytes for Ristretto/Ed25519 encodings accepted by `from_bytes`); `pop` = `SchnorrSignature { R: s·G, s }` for any scalar `s` (e.g., `s = 1`, `R = generator`); `msg` = `chosen_plaintext ⊕ keystream` where `keystream = cipher(context, identity)` is computable without any secret.
2. Submit the bytes as the share message to a victim during `CoordinatorMessage::Shares` handling (`EncryptedMessage::<C, SecretShare<C::F>>::read` at `processor/src/key_gen.rs:409`).
3. `C::read_G` accepts identity; `decrypt` queues the forged PoP — `s·G == R + c·identity` holds — and `ecdh(enc_key_priv, identity) = identity` decrypts `msg` to the attacker's chosen plaintext. `calculate_share` proceeds to `from_repr`/share verification with attacker-controlled bytes, despite the sender proving knowledge of nothing.

Uncertain: whether downstream callers treat the resulting blame/confidentiality loss as exploitable enough for High; the acceptance of a forged proof and publicly-readable "encrypted" share is concrete, but does not by itself yield key-share recovery without additional protocol context I could not fully verify.
