### Title
Decimals metadata drift after registration lets a token owner amplify cross-chain mint amounts in `hyper-fungible-token` - ([File: modules/pallets/hyper-fungible-token/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
`register_token`/`update_token` enforce the invariant `config.decimals (erc_decimals) >= local_decimals` only once, at configuration time, using whatever `Assets::decimals(asset_id)` metadata value the asset reports *at that moment*. But `send()` re-queries `Assets::decimals(asset_id)` fresh on every call and feeds it straight into `convert_to_erc20` without re-checking the invariant. Since asset metadata decimals in `pallet-assets` are ordinarily mutable by the asset's own owner/admin (a normal, unprivileged action for permissionless/user-created assets, not requiring any relayer, prover, or Hyperbridge governance compromise), an attacker can register a token, then lower the asset's reported decimals after registration to invert the invariant and inflate the amount encoded into the outbound cross-chain `Message`.

### Finding Description
`register_token` validates decimals once: [1](#0-0) 

`send()` re-derives `decimals` dynamically from the asset's live metadata at call time, not from a stored/locked value, and passes it straight into `convert_to_erc20`: [2](#0-1) 

`convert_to_erc20` computes `value * 10^(erc_decimals.saturating_sub(local_decimals))`, i.e. it multiplies the raw burned/escrowed amount up to erc20-precision assuming `erc_decimals >= local_decimals` always holds: [3](#0-2) 

`convert_to_balance` (used symmetrically in `on_accept`/`on_timeout`) has the same one-directional `saturating_sub` assumption: [4](#0-3) 

Because `Precisions::<T>` (the stored `erc_decimals`) is fixed once at registration/update, but the local asset's `decimals` metadata can be changed afterward through the underlying `Assets` pallet (independent of this pallet's `CreateOrigin`), the invariant `erc_decimals >= local_decimals` that `register_token`/`update_token` enforce can silently become false by the time `send()` executes. When `local_decimals` (fetched fresh) becomes larger than the stored `erc_decimals`, or more precisely when the *live* decimals value is lower than what it was at registration, the scaling factor `10^(erc_decimals - decimals)` grows, so a small raw amount that is burned locally is multiplied into a far larger `erc20_amount` placed in the dispatched `Message`. That inflated amount is exactly what the destination `HyperFungibleToken`/`WrappedHyperFungibleToken` EVM contract will mint or release to the recipient on `onAccept`, since the receiving side trusts the encoded `message.amount` (scaled per its own configured `erc_decimals`) as authoritative.

### Impact Explanation
This directly maps to "unauthorized transaction/execution" and "false state acceptance leading to fund loss," matching the bounty scope for bridged-asset custody. An attacker who burns/escrows a negligible amount of a non-native (mint/burn) or native asset locally can, by lowering the asset's decimals metadata after registration, cause the pallet to dispatch a cross-chain post request instructing the destination contract to mint/release a vastly larger amount of the bridged ERC20 token than was ever actually locked or burned on the source chain — a direct steal of funds from bridge custody/mint authority, reachable via the public, unprivileged `send()` extrinsic.

### Likelihood Explanation
The only capability required is being the owner/admin of the underlying local `Assets`-pallet asset used in the bridge registration — a standard, permissionless capability for user-created assets in typical Substrate `pallet-assets` deployments (`set_metadata`), not a relayer, prover, governance, or Hyperbridge-host compromise. The registration-time check gives a false sense of safety because it is never re-validated at the point where the untrusted, mutable value is actually used for the scaling math.

### Recommendation
Do not re-fetch metadata decimals dynamically at `send()`/`on_accept`/`on_timeout` time for the scaling computation. Instead, snapshot and store `local_decimals` alongside `erc_decimals` in `Precisions` at registration/update time (or re-validate `ensure!(erc_decimals >= current_local_decimals, Error::<T>::ErcDecimalsBelowLocal)` immediately before every `convert_to_erc20`/`convert_to_balance` call), so that any post-registration change to an asset's decimals metadata cannot desynchronize the scaling factor from the invariant the registration path relies on.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker creates asset `A` via `pallet-assets` with `decimals = 6`, and registers it in `hyper-fungible-token::register_token` for chain `C` with `config.decimals = 6` (passes `ensure!(6 >= 6)`).
2. Attacker calls `pallet-assets::set_metadata` on asset `A` (as its owner/admin) to change its decimals to `0`.
3. Attacker calls `send()` with `params.asset_id = A`, `params.amount = 1` (raw unit). `decimals` is now fetched fresh as `0`; `erc_decimals` stored is `6`.
4. `convert_to_erc20(1, erc_decimals=6, decimals=0)` = `1 * 10^(6-0)` = `1_000_000`.
5. The dispatched `Message.amount` cross-chain is `1_000_000` erc20-base-units, even though only `1` raw unit of the asset was burned/escrowed locally — the destination `HyperFungibleToken` contract mints/releases `1_000_000` units to the attacker's chosen recipient for a burn that was 1,000,000× smaller.

/// Converts a local u128 balance to an ERC20 U256 amount
///
/// Multiplies by 10^(erc_decimals - local_decimals) to scale up to ERC20 precision
pub fn convert_to_erc20(value: u128, erc_decimals: u8, local_decimals: u8) -> U256 {
	U256::from(value) * U256::from(10u128.pow(erc_decimals.saturating_sub(local_decimals) as u32))
}
