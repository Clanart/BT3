### Title
Unauthenticated sender binding in PedPoP blame lets anyone convict an innocent participant - ([File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs])

### Summary
`BlameMachine::blame` / `AdditionalBlameMachine::blame` adjudicate fault using only the `EncryptedMessage`'s proof-of-possession (`pop`) and the accuser-supplied `EncryptionKeyProof`. The `pop` is a Schnorr signature verified against the ephemeral `msg.key`, whose challenge merely *labels* a `sender: Participant` index — it is not tied to any long-term identity key of the claimed sender. Any party (including a non-participant via `AdditionalBlameMachine::new`) can therefore fabricate a message "from" victim `sender`, supply a valid DLEq for their own ECDH key, have the ciphertext decrypt to a non-canonical or invalid share, and cause `blame_internal` to return the innocent `sender` as faulty — triggering that participant's removal, mirroring the reported "anyone can trigger another user's removal" bug.

### Finding Description
In `decrypt_with_proof` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:366-397), the message's authenticity for `from` is established solely by `msg.pop.verify(msg.key, pop_challenge(context, R, key, from, msg))`. `pop_challenge` (lines 302-324) appends `sender.to_bytes()` to the transcript, but the signature only proves knowledge of the discrete log of the attacker-chosen `msg.key` — anyone can produce it for any `sender` index. The subsequent DLEq check (lines 381-390) only requires proving `log_G(enc_keys[decryptor]) == log_{msg.key}(proof.key)`; when the accuser is the recipient, they know their own `enc_key` scalar and produce a valid proof over their self-chosen `msg.key` trivially.

In `blame_internal` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:575-609), a successful decryption followed by `from_repr` failure (lines 590-593) or a failed `share_verification_statements` multiexp (lines 595-605) returns `sender` as the faulty party. The library explicitly supports third-party adjudication: `AdditionalBlameMachine::new` (lines 649-662) is documented as usable "regardless of if the caller was a member in the DKG protocol," and such a caller has no view of any external authenticated channel — the forgeable `pop`/`dleq` pair is the sole evidence convicting the sender.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious participant Eve (or any third party reconstructing `AdditionalBlameMachine` from the public commitment messages) can generate a blame verdict naming an innocent participant Alice as the sender of an invalid secret share. In the intended usage this verdict aborts the DKG and identifies Alice for removal/slashing, exactly the reported impact: an unprivileged party causing another's removal. Eve needs only public data — the protocol `context`, her own encryption key, and the broadcast commitment messages.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully deterministic: Eve controls `msg.key`, the ciphertext, the `pop` signature (she knows `log(msg.key)`), and the `EncryptionKeyProof` DLEq (she knows her `enc_key` scalar). Every check in `decrypt_with_proof` and `blame_internal` passes or fails in the way that convicts `sender`. The only caveat is the documented expectation that the message "must have been authenticated as actually having come from the sender" — but for `AdditionalBlameMachine` (explicitly for non-participants) and for any re-verification of blame, that channel check cannot be performed, so the in-code cryptographic conviction is the effective guardrail, and it is forgeable.

### Recommendation
Bind the per-message `pop` to the claimed sender's authenticated identity — e.g., require the `EncryptedMessage` be countersigned under the sender's registered commitments/encryption key, or include the sender's `enc_key` (registered in round 1) in `pop_challenge` and verify consistency. At minimum, `blame_internal` should not convict `sender` on the strength of a `pop` over an attacker-selected ephemeral key alone; a blame verdict should require proof elements only the real sender could have produced (e.g., a signature under the key established by their verified `Commitments` PoK).

### Proof of Concept
Setup (with `C` a `Ciphersuite`, DKG `context`, participant set of size `n`):

1. Eve (participant `i_e`, encryption scalar `e`, public `E = e·G` registered in round 1) obtains a `BlameMachine` via `calculate_share`, or any party builds `AdditionalBlameMachine::new(context, n, commitment_msgs)` from the public round-1 messages (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/lib.rs:649-662).
2. Eve chooses scalar `k`, sets `K = k·G`, and plaintext `p = [0xFF; repr_len]` (guaranteed non-canonical for `C::F`).
3. `shared = e·K`; `ct = cipher(context, shared).apply_keystream(p)` (encryption.rs:101-133, public computation since Eve knows `e`).
4. `pop = SchnorrSignature::sign(&k, r, pop_challenge(context, R, K, sender = i_a, ct))` — passes `pop.verify(msg.key = K, …)` at encryption.rs:374-379 despite naming Alice as `from`, because the signature only proves knowledge of `k`.
5. `proof = EncryptionKeyProof { key: shared, dleq: DLEqProof::prove(rng, encryption_key_transcript(context), &[G, K], &e) }` — satisfies `dleq.verify(&[G, K], &[E, shared])` at encryption.rs:383-390.
6. Call `blame(sender = i_a /*Alice*/, recipient = i_e /*Eve*/, msg, Some(proof))` (lib.rs:623-632 / 674-682). Decryption yields `p`; `C::F::from_repr` returns `None`; `blame_internal` returns `sender` — Alice is adjudicated faulty (lib.rs:590-592) despite having sent a perfectly valid share, and is removed from the set.
