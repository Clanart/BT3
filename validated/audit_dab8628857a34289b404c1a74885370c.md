### Title
Crafted FROST preprocess with malicious nonce commitments makes aggregate nonce the point at infinity, panicking the signer in `Hram::hram` - (File: networks/bitcoin/src/crypto.rs)

### Summary
The Bitcoin BIP-340 `Hram` used by the FROST `Schnorr` algorithm panics when the aggregate nonce point `R` (or group key `A`) is the point at infinity, via `x()`'s `.expect("point at infinity")`. An unprivileged co-signer can submit a crafted `Preprocess` whose nonce commitments (`D`, `E`) negate the honest commitments, forcing the summed nonce `R` to the identity element and deterministically crashing the honest signer's `sign()` call. This is a denial-of-service reachable entirely through attacker-controlled `read_preprocess` bytes, analogous to the crafted-file segmentation fault in CVE-2020-21605.

### Finding Description
In `AlgorithmSignMachine::sign`, each participant's preprocess is deserialized via `Commitments::read`, which performs `C::read_G` on attacker-supplied encodings without rejecting the identity point (crypto/frost/src/nonce.rs:34, crypto/frost/src/sign.rs:276-281). The aggregate nonce is then computed as a plain sum of all participants' `D`/`E` commitments in `BindingFactor::nonces` (crypto/frost/src/nonce.rs:194-211), so a malicious participant who sees the honest commitments can choose `D = -(Σ honest D)` and `E` arbitrarily, yielding `R = identity`.

`Schnorr::sign_share` then calls `H::hram(&nonce_sums[0][0], &params.group_key(), msg)` (crypto/frost/src/algorithm.rs:208). For the Bitcoin algorithm, `Hram::hram` calls `x(R)`, which executes `key.to_encoded_point(true)` followed by `.expect("point at infinity")` (networks/bitcoin/src/crypto.rs:13-16), panicking on the identity point. The doc comment explicitly documents this panic behavior ("If either `R` or `A` is the point at infinity, this will panic", crypto.rs:54).

### Impact Explanation
Any honest participant in a Bitcoin FROST signing session can be crashed by a malicious co-signer before producing its signature share. Since `sign()` panics rather than returning a `FrostError`, the process aborts mid-protocol, denying signing progress for the threshold group — availability loss analogous to the CVSS 6.5 crafted-file crash. Note the panic happens after the local node's preprocess/nonces are committed, and no secret material is leaked.

### Likelihood Explanation
Fully attacker-controlled and deterministic: the malicious signer only needs to submit two `ProjectivePoint` encodings chosen to cancel the sum of the other participants' `D` commitments (public, transmitted preprocess values). `read_G` on Secp256k1 encodings accepts the identity point, and no identity check exists anywhere along `Commitments::read` → `BindingFactor::nonces` → `sign_share` → `x()`. No special privileges or collusion are required beyond being a valid participant.

### Recommendation
Reject identity nonce commitments in `GeneratorCommitments::read`/`Commitments::read` (crypto/frost/src/nonce.rs:34-39, 133-139), or check `R.is_identity()` in `BindingFactor::nonces` and surface a `FrostError`. Alternatively, make `x()`/`x_only` return `io::Result`/`Option` instead of panicking (networks/bitcoin/src/crypto.rs:13-23).
