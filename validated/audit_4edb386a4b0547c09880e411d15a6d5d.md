### Title
Malformed `ThresholdKeys` can bind an attacker-controlled group key to an unrelated secret share - (File: crypto/dkg/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`ThresholdKeys::read` deserializes a participant’s `secret_share` and the set of `verification_shares`, but `ThresholdKeys::new` never verifies that the local secret share corresponds to `verification_shares[i]`. An attacker can therefore supply bytes whose advertised group key is controlled by the attacker while the embedded local secret share cannot sign for it.

### Finding Description
`ThresholdKeys::read` accepts threshold parameters, an interpolation policy, a scalar `secret_share`, and serialized verification shares before passing them to `ThresholdKeys::new` [1](#0-0) . `ThresholdKeys::new` only checks the number and range of verification-share participant indexes [2](#0-1) . It then derives `group_key` exclusively from the first `t` verification shares and the interpolation factors [3](#0-2) .

There is no check equivalent to:

```rust
C::generator() * secret_share == verification_shares[&params.i()]
```

Consequently, untrusted bytes can claim that participant `i` owns secret `s`, while the group key is derived from attacker-selected verification shares unrelated to `s`. During signing, the view interpolates the unrelated `original_secret_share` [4](#0-3) , but public verification uses the independently supplied verification shares and attacker-selected `group_key` [5](#0-4) .

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can cause a service to advertise or scan for a threshold address controlled by the attacker, while the service’s deserialized share cannot authorize that address. Funds sent to the resulting group key can be taken by the attacker if they know the discrete logarithm behind the supplied verification shares, while the victim’s signing operation produces an invalid share or an internal error.

For a `1-of-1` key, the impact is direct theft: the attacker chooses secret scalar `a`, supplies verification share `aG`, supplies unrelated local share `s`, waits for funds to arrive at group key `aG`, and spends them with `a`. For larger thresholds, the malformed share can also make otherwise received funds unspendable by the honest participant set.

### Likelihood Explanation
The malformed condition is reachable entirely through public bytes passed to `ThresholdKeys::read`. The deserializer accepts all independent fields needed for the attack, and neither `read` nor `new` performs the semantic binding check between the private share and its public verification share.

This mirrors the missing-owner-binding bug class: an attacker-controlled authorization value is accepted separately from the value that determines the receiving identity or address.

### Recommendation
In `ThresholdKeys::new`, reject a `secret_share` unless:

```rust
C::generator() * secret_share == verification_shares[&params.i()]
```

For tweaked keys, this check should remain against the untweaked/original share because `scale` and `offset` are applied separately to both the group key and signing view [6](#0-5) . Alternatively, make `ThresholdKeys::read` private or explicitly trusted-only, though the safest API behavior is to enforce consistency during construction.

### Proof of Concept
The following construction demonstrates the missing binding for a `1-of-1` Lagrange key:

```rust
let attacker_secret = C::F::random(&mut rng);
let unrelated_local_secret = C::F::random(&mut rng);
let attacker_verification_share = C::generator() * attacker_secret;

let mut verification_shares = HashMap::new();
verification_shares.insert(
  Participant::new(1).unwrap(),
  attacker_verification_share,
);

let keys = ThresholdKeys::new(
  ThresholdParams::new(1, 1, Participant::new(1).unwrap()).unwrap(),
  Interpolation::Lagrange,
  Zeroizing::new(unrelated_local_secret),
  verification_shares,
).unwrap();

assert_eq!(keys.group_key(), C::generator() * attacker_secret);
assert_ne!(
  C::generator() * *keys.original_secret_share(),
  keys.group_key(),
);
```

The same condition is reachable from serialized input because `ThresholdKeys::read` deserializes `secret_share` and each verification share independently [7](#0-6) . A service using the resulting `group_key` as a receive address reports funds to an attacker-controlled point, while signing attempts interpolate the unrelated local secret share and fail verification [8](#0-7) .
