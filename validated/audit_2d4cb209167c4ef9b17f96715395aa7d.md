The vulnerability is real. `compound()` in `rewards/ManualCompound.sol` computes the amount to convert/lock/deposit for each configured reward token from the contract's total balance rather than from what was claimed in the current call. [1](#0-0) 

### Title
Full-balance sweep in `compound()` lets any caller claim residual reward-token dust belonging to previous users - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`compound(address[] _lps, address[][] _rewards, uint256 _convertRatio, uint256 _minRec, bool _lockMgp)` reads `IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` for each configured reward token and routes the *entire* balance to `msg.sender` via `convertFor`, `lockFor`, `depositFor`, or a plain transfer. Because the contract does not track per-user or per-call claimed amounts, any token left on the contract from a prior `compound()` call (rounding dust in `SmartWomConvert._convertFor`, a helper/locker not consuming its full approval, or simply an unclaimed remainder) is fully attributed to whichever unprivileged address calls `compound()` next, using that caller's own `_convertRatio`/`_minRec`/`_lockMgp` parameters.

### Finding Description
`compound()` first calls `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` to push newly earned rewards for `msg.sender`'s positions into the contract [2](#0-1) . It then loops over the globally configured `rewards` array (not tied to `_lps`/`_rewards` inputs) and for each reward token computes `receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this))` — the whole contract balance, not an amount scoped to `msg.sender`'s claim [3](#0-2) . That balance is then approved and forwarded entirely to `msg.sender` through `convertFor`, `lockFor`, `depositFor`, or `safeTransfer` [4](#0-3) .

Dust/residue can legitimately accumulate from a normal, honest use of the contract: `SmartWomConvert._convertFor` (the `IConverter` implementation wired into `ManualCompound`) computes `convertAmount`/`buybackAmount` via integer division of `_amount * _convertRatio / DENOMINATOR`, which can leave WOM/mWOM rounding remainders in the converter or leftover tokens routed back and forth [5](#0-4) ; a helper (e.g. `SimplePoolHelper.depositFor`) that only pulls a caller-specified `_amount` via `transferFrom` would also leave any surplus balance sitting on `ManualCompound` [6](#0-5) . Since `compound()` has no owner/role restriction, once dust exists for a configured `tokenAddress`, any unprivileged caller can invoke `compound()` (even with empty or unrelated `_lps`/`_rewards`) and the second loop unconditionally sweeps that stranded balance to themselves with a `_convertRatio` of their own choosing — decoupled from the ratio/config that produced the original dust for another user. There is no accounting structure (e.g., a `lastBalance` snapshot, per-claim delta tracking, or pro-rata attribution) to prevent this.

### Impact Explanation
Any residual reward-token balance on the contract — arising from routine rounding/partial-consumption during normal compounding — becomes directly claimable by an unrelated, unprivileged caller, who additionally controls the `_convertRatio`/`_minRec`/`_lockMgp` applied to that stolen balance. This is a direct theft of value that rightfully belongs to whichever prior user's claim generated it, matching the Critical "Direct theft of user funds" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
No special capital or privilege is required: the attacker only needs to call the public `compound()` function after any dust has accumulated (which routine operation of the converter/helper/locker chain is prone to produce due to integer-division rounding). The exploit is repeatable every time dust reappears, and the attack requires only a single transaction.

### Recommendation
Track amounts per call instead of relying on `balanceOf(address(this))`: capture pre/post balances of tokens received specifically from the `multiclaimOnBehalf` call for the caller, or maintain an explicit ledger crediting stranded/rounding residue back to the account whose claim produced it (or sweep it via an owner-only rescue path rather than as part of the public `compound()` flow).

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat plan:
1. Deploy `ManualCompound`, a mock `masterMagpie` supporting `multiclaimOnBehalf`, a mock reward token, and a mock `IConverter`/`SmartWomConvert`-like converter that intentionally leaves 1 wei of dust behind after `convertFor` (simulating rounding).
2. User A calls `compound()` with `_lps`/`_rewards` that claim reward tokens; the converter consumes `receivedBalance - 1` and leaves 1 wei of the reward token on `ManualCompound`.
3. Attacker (unprivileged, no prior deposit/claim) calls `compound()` with empty `_lps`/`_rewards` arrays and an attacker-chosen `_convertRatio`.
4. Assert: the 1 wei dust balance is transferred/converted to the attacker's benefit (`msg.sender == attacker` in the `convertFor`/`safeTransfer` call), even though the attacker made no claim contributing to it — demonstrating `_convertRatio` supplied by the attacker is applied to value that should belong to User A, and the attacker's balance delta is positive with zero cost, confirming the theft.
