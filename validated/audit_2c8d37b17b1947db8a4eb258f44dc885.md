### Title
Deterministic `CachedPreprocess` is re-signed across `share`/`complete` re-executions, enabling nonce-reuse key-share recovery - (File: coordinator/src/tributary/signing_protocol.rs)

### Summary
The bug class of CVE-2021-3407 — an object consumed/freed twice during a multi-stage operation — maps onto Serai as a signing machine whose one-shot nonce seed is consumed more than once. `SigningProtocol::preprocess_internal` derives FROST nonces deterministically from a `CachedPreprocess` seed stored in the DB, and `share_internal`/`complete` invoke `sign()` on that same seed for every call. Any two executions over distinct preprocess sets or messages reuse identical nonces, yielding the classic Schnorr/FROST nonce-reuse equation that exposes the validator's private key share.

### Finding Description
`preprocess_internal` loads `CachedPreprocesses::get(self.txn, &self.context)` where `context = (b"DkgConfirmer", attempt)` and rebuilds the sign machine via `AlgorithmSignMachine::from_cache`, which calls `seeded_preprocess(cache)` — deterministic nonce derivation from the stored seed [1](#0-0) . The cached seed is never deleted or rotated after use, and the FROST `SignMachine` contract explicitly requires the preprocess be deleted after `from_cache` because "any reuse will presumably cause the signer to leak their secret share" [2](#0-1) .

The seed is consumed more than once along two axes:

1. `DkgConfirmer::complete` calls `self.share_internal(preprocesses, key_pair)` again — a full second `machine.sign(...)` over the caller-supplied `preprocesses` map — solely to reconstruct the `SignatureMachine` [3](#0-2) .
2. `DkgConfirmer::share` itself signs with the same seed for whatever `preprocesses` map is passed in [4](#0-3) .

If the preprocess map (or the `key_pair`/`removed` message components) seen by the `share` execution differs from the map seen by the `complete` execution — e.g., share committed over the first t-of-n preprocesses observed while complete re-executes over a different or superset — the same nonces `(d, e)` are combined into two signature shares `z_i = d + e·ρ_j + λ·s·c_j` with distinct binding factors/challenges `c_j`. The module's own safety argument concedes reuse occurs exactly when "the received nonce commitments (or the message to be signed) would have to be distinct and sign would have to be called again," and leaves the on-chain check that commitments match the presumed preprocess as an unimplemented TODO [5](#0-4) . Nothing in `share_internal` verifies that the `preprocesses` passed to `complete` are byte-identical to those already signed in `share`; both are reconstructed independently from tributary data at call time.

### Impact Explanation
Two shares produced under the same nonce with different challenges/bindings give an observer two linear equations `z1 − z2 = λ·s·(c1 − c2)` (+ρ terms), recovering the validator's FROST secret share — full compromise of the validator key used as the root of trust for DKG confirmation, the highest-value secret in the coordinator.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering requires `share` and `complete` (or repeated `share` calls) to operate on non-identical preprocess sets under the same `(b"DkgConfirmer", attempt)` context. The design relies on BFT finality to make the inputs identical, but acknowledges two residual paths — a logical flaw overwriting prior messages and rebuild following a distinct chain — and marks the guard verifying on-chain commitments against the presumed preprocess as TODO [6](#0-5) . Since re-execution is the designed pattern and inputs are assembled per-call, any discrepancy in the committed set between the two executions silently produces the second sign. Medium likelihood gated by that consistency gap; severity is high (key share recovery).

### Recommendation
After consuming the cached seed, mark it spent (delete `CachedPreprocesses` for the context or store a "share published" flag and refuse a second `sign`), and persist the preprocess set/message hash that was actually signed so `complete` asserts byte-equality before re-executing — implementing the noted TODO of verifying the presumed preprocess against the on-chain commitments.

### Proof of Concept
1. Coordinator executes `DkgConfirmer::share(preprocesses_A, key_pair)` → publishes `z_A` derived from nonces seeded by `CachedPreprocesses[("DkgConfirmer", attempt)]`.
2. A later `DkgConfirmer::complete(preprocesses_B, key_pair, shares)` executes `share_internal` again; `preprocess_internal` reloads the identical seed, regenerating identical `(d, e)` [7](#0-6) .
3. If `preprocesses_B ≠ preprocesses_A` (different participant set or different commitment bytes → different `ρ` binding factors and transcript challenge `c`), the second `sign` emits `z_B` over the same nonce.
4. From public data `(z_A, c_A, ρ_A)` and `(z_B, c_B, ρ_B)`, solve `s = (z_A − z_B − e·(ρ_A − ρ_B)) / (λ·(c_A − c_B))` — or simply `(z_A − z_B)/(λ·(c_A − c_B))` when the nonce combination is unchanged — recovering the MuSig/FROST private key share.

  In order for nonce re-use to occur, the received nonce commitments (or the message to be signed)
  would have to be distinct and sign would have to be called again.

  Before we act on any received messages, they're ordered and finalized by a BFT algorithm. The
  only way to operate on distinct received messages would be if:

  1) A logical flaw exists, letting new messages over write prior messages
  2) A reorganization occurred from chain A to chain B, and with it, different messages

  Reorganizations are not supported, as BFT is assumed by the presence of a BFT algorithm. While
  a significant amount of processes may be byzantine, leading to BFT being broken, that still will
  not trigger a reorganization. The only way to move to a distinct chain, with distinct messages,
  would be by rebuilding the local process (this time following chain B). Upon any complete
  rebuild, we'd re-decide nonces, achieving safety. This does set a bound preventing partial
  rebuilds which is accepted.
