### Title
`ManualCompound.compound` sweeps the contract's entire token balance to whoever calls it, regardless of whether that caller actually claimed the funds - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`compound()` performs two unconditioned "sweep entire balance" loops: one over caller‑supplied `_rewards[i][j]` addresses for non‑compoundable tokens, and a second, unconditional loop over every admin‑registered compoundable token in `rewards`. Both loops read `IERC20(token).balanceOf(address(this))` and forward the *whole* balance to `msg.sender`, with no bookkeeping that ties the swept amount to what was actually claimed for that caller in the current call.

### Finding Description
`compound()` first calls `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` [1](#0-0)  to claim `msg.sender`'s pending rewards for the pools/tokens named in `_lps`/`_rewards`.

It then sweeps back any *non‑compoundable* token named by the caller: [2](#0-1) 
Note that `compoundableRewards[_rewards[i][j]]` is only a boolean flag set by the owner via `addReward`/`removeReward` [3](#0-2) ; it does **not** restrict which token addresses the caller may place in `_rewards`, nor does it check that the balance being transferred was produced by the `multiclaimOnBehalf` call that just executed. The transferred amount is whatever `balanceOf(address(this))` happens to return for that address at that moment — any pre‑existing balance (dust from an earlier `convertFor`/`lockFor`/`depositFor` call that didn't consume 100% of `receivedBalance`, or tokens otherwise sitting in the contract) is swept out along with it.

The same flaw exists — even more clearly — in the second, *unconditional* loop: [4](#0-3) 
This loop iterates over the fixed, admin‑registered `rewards` array regardless of what `_lps`/`_rewards` the caller supplied (they can even be empty arrays), and unconditionally forwards/converts/locks the *entire* current balance of every registered compoundable token to `msg.sender`. There is no per‑account accounting anywhere in `ManualCompound` that tracks how much of the contract's balance belongs to which caller's claim.

`multiclaimOnBehalf` itself is gated by `_onlyCompounder` in `MasterMagpie.sol` [5](#0-4)  and updates reward-debt/`unClaimedMgp` before external transfers (checks‑effects‑interactions) [6](#0-5) , so a caller cannot double‑claim the *same* reward twice through re‑entrancy. However, this only protects the claiming step inside `MasterMagpie` — it does nothing to prevent `ManualCompound.compound()` from later sweeping out balances that were never generated by the caller's own claim at all.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged caller who invokes `compound()` — even with empty `_lps`/`_rewards` arrays, claiming nothing of their own — will receive 100% of whatever balance of any registered "compoundable" token currently sits in `ManualCompound`, and can additionally name any arbitrary token address in `_rewards[i][j]` to sweep its full balance too. If any residual/dust balance of a valuable token is ever present in the contract (from imperfect conversion by `_convertor`/`_locker`/`_helperAddress`, from another user's not‑yet-collected leftover, or from anyone accidentally/intentionally transferring tokens to the contract), the first caller to invoke `compound()` can steal it outright. This matches Critical - Direct theft of user funds, since the invariant "a caller receives only what it legitimately claimed via `multiclaimOnBehalf`" does not hold for either sweep loop.

### Likelihood Explanation
No special privileges are required — any EOA can call `compound()` directly. The only precondition is that the contract hold a non‑zero balance of a swept token that isn't attributable 100% to the caller's own current claim; this is plausible whenever conversion/locking legs (`convertFor`, `lockFor`, `depositFor`) don't consume the full approved balance, or whenever tokens land in the contract outside of the exact call that spends them. The attack is trivially repeatable (call `compound()` repeatedly, potentially with empty arrays, to sweep dust as it accumulates) and requires no capital.

### Recommendation
Track per‑call claimed amounts explicitly (e.g., capture the pre/post balance delta produced specifically by the `multiclaimOnBehalf` call for `msg.sender`, or have `multiclaimOnBehalf`/`_claimBaseRewarder` return the exact amounts claimed) and forward only those amounts, rather than sweeping `balanceOf(address(this))`. Additionally, restrict `_rewards[i][j]` sweeping to tokens that are verified members of the target pool's actual registered reward-token set (`isRewardToken` in `BaseRewardPool`), and never let a caller trigger a full-balance sweep of the admin‑registered `rewards` list unconditionally regardless of their own claim.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat/Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, a `BaseRewardPool` with a compoundable reward token `RWD`, and `ManualCompound` with `compounder` set to `ManualCompound`.
2. Have User A stake and legitimately accrue `RWD` rewards; call `compound()` with a converter (`_convertor`) that intentionally leaves a small unconsumed remainder of `RWD` in `ManualCompound` after `convertFor` (simulate slippage/partial fill).
3. Assert `IERC20(RWD).balanceOf(ManualCompound)` is now non‑zero (leftover dust) though User A's `compound()` transaction completed successfully and had `receivedBalance` fully approved to the convertor.
4. From an unrelated address (User B, or an attacker contract with zero stake), call `compound(_lps=[], _rewards=[], _convertRatio=0, _minRec=0, _lockMgp=false)`.
5. Assert User B receives the full leftover `RWD` balance via the unconditional second loop, despite having claimed nothing via `multiclaimOnBehalf` — demonstrating `IERC20(rewardToken).balanceOf(address(this))` no longer reconciles with the amount actually claimed by the caller through `multiclaimOnBehalf`.
6. Repeat with a caller‑supplied arbitrary token address placed in `_rewards[i][j]` (not registered as compoundable) to show the first loop exhibits the identical unrestricted sweep behavior.
