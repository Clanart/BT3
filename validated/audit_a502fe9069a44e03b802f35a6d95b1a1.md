Based on the code, `vlMGPBaseRewarder` implements a classic reward-per-share pattern that is genuinely vulnerable to the "first staker after a zero-totalStaked epoch" issue described in the question.

### Title
First locker after totalStaked()==0 epoch captures entire accumulated queuedRewards (including forfeited amounts) disproportionate to stake - (File: rewards/vlMGPBaseRewarder.sol)

### Summary
`queueNewRewards` and `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` in `vlMGPBaseRewarder.sol` accumulate incoming rewards (including forfeited-lock penalties routed via `queueMGP`/`_sendReward`) into `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` whenever `totalStaked() == 0`. When the next reward provisioning happens after `totalStaked()` becomes non-zero, the *entire* accumulated `queuedRewards` is folded into `rewardPerTokenStored` divided by the then-current (potentially minimal) `totalStaked()`. Because `_earned()` multiplies `rewardPerToken` delta by the user's own balance, a user who is the sole staker at that moment receives the full converted reward regardless of how small or short-lived their stake is.

### Finding Description
- `totalStaked()` returns `IERC20(vlMGP).totalSupply()` [1](#0-0)  which can genuinely reach zero if all lockers fully unlock.
- While `totalStaked() == 0`, both `queueNewRewards` (manager-provided rewards) and `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` (forfeited-lock penalties from `queueMGP`/`_sendReward`) route their amounts into `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` instead of updating `rewardPerTokenStored`: [2](#0-1)  and [3](#0-2) .
- When `totalStaked()` later becomes non-zero and a new `queueNewRewards`/`_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` call fires, the whole `queuedRewards` balance is merged into the new amount and divided by the current `totalStaked()`: `rewardPerTokenStored += (_amountReward * 10**vlMGPDecimal) / totalStaked()`.
- `_earned()` computes a user's share as `userVlmgpShare * (rewardPerToken - userRewardPerTokenPaid) / 10**decimals` [4](#0-3) . If the attacker is the *sole* staker at the time of this update (`userVlmgpShare == totalStaked()`), the division and multiplication cancel out and the attacker's earned amount equals the entire distributed `_amountReward` (which now includes all previously queued/forfeited rewards) — independent of how large or how long their stake actually is.
- Locking is a normal, permissionless action via `lock()`/`lockFor()` in `VLMGP.sol` [5](#0-4) , requiring no special privilege — only ordinary MGP token capital. No modifier in `queueNewRewards`, `updateRewards`, or `_updateFor` protects against this scenario since none of them check for "was totalStaked ever zero recently" or otherwise dilute credit by time-weighting.
- The forfeiture path (`queueMGP` -> `_calExpireForfeit` -> `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer`) confirms that forfeited amounts from previously-exited large lockers do flow into this same `queuedRewards` bucket [6](#0-5) , so the pool an attacker can capture is not merely "manager donations" but also prior users' forfeited yield.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who front-runs the next `queueNewRewards`/reward-crediting event with a minimal `lock()` after a `totalStaked()==0` window captures the entire pending `queuedRewards` balance (Immunefi impact class: theft of unclaimed/forfeited yield), regardless of contribution size or duration. This is a real, quantifiable diversion of yield away from future genuine lockers who would otherwise have shared it proportionally as they staked.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Requires `totalStaked() == 0` to actually occur (all vlMGP holders having fully unlocked) — plausible for a nascent, low-TVL pool or gradual full exit, but not guaranteed to happen in a mature high-TVL protocol.
- Requires the attacker to observe the mempool and front-run the manager's `queueNewRewards` call (or a user's forfeit-triggering `_sendReward`/`queueMGP` call) with their own `lock()` transaction — feasible with basic MEV/front-running capability on a public mempool, no flash loan needed since MGP itself must be genuinely acquired and locked (though the amount needed can be as little as 1 wei of MGP).
- No special privilege is needed; `lock()` is fully permissionless [7](#0-6) , and the reward distribution logic itself does not distinguish stake duration or size when there is only a single staker.
- Repeatable any time the zero-totalStaked condition recurs.

### Recommendation
Redesign the reward accounting so that rewards accrued while `totalStaked() == 0` are not attributed entirely to whichever single account happens to be the first staker afterward. Options: (1) require a minimum bootstrap stake / minimum staking duration before newly queued rewards can be claimed, (2) time-weight or vest rewards distributed immediately after a zero-supply transition, or (3) route queuedRewards from a zero-supply period to a separate pool/treasury rather than merging fully into `rewardPerTokenStored` on the very next non-zero-supply update.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `VLMGP`, `vlMGPBaseRewarder`, `MasterMagpie`, MGP token and a reward token; register the rewarder's manager.
2. Have User A lock a large amount of MGP, then fully `startUnlock`/`unlock` (after cooldown) so that `totalStaked() == 0`.
3. Simulate a manager forfeited-reward/queueNewRewards call while `totalStaked() == 0`, verifying `rewards[token].queuedRewards` accumulates the amount (assert via `rewards(token)` getter).
4. Attacker calls `lock(1)` (1 wei of MGP) to become the sole staker, right before the manager's next `queueNewRewards(amount, token)` call is mined (simulate front-run ordering in the test).
5. Manager calls `queueNewRewards`; assert `rewardPerTokenStored` update.
6. Attacker calls `getReward`/`multiclaim` via `MasterMagpie` and assert they receive the entire `queuedRewards + amount` reward, while User A (who had contributed none of the new stake) receives nothing, proving disproportionate capture of forfeited/queued yield.
