Potential first-depositor share inflation in `PublicDelegation` staking vault via direct asset donation - ([File: contracts/bindings/publicdelegation/PublicDelegation.go])

### Summary
`PublicDelegation` is an ERC4626-style staking vault exposed as a system contract binding, with `stake`/`Staked(user, assets, shares)`, `convertToShares`/`convertToAssets`, `previewDeposit`/`previewRedeem`, `totalAssets()` and `totalSupply()` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . This structure is architecturally identical to the vulnerable BakerFi Vault: shares are minted as a function of `assets * totalSupply() / totalAssets()`, which is the exact share-price formula that enables the first-depositor inflation attack when `totalAssets()` can be inflated independently of `totalSupply()` (e.g., by staking rewards accruing to the pool, or direct native-token transfer to the contract, before any/enough shares have been minted).

Without the source, I cannot confirm:
- Whether `previewDeposit`/`convertToShares` use OpenZeppelin's ERC4626 virtual-offset defense (`decimalsOffset`) that mitigates first-depositor inflation.
- Whether `totalAssets()` includes externally-donatable balances (e.g., raw native KAIA balance of the contract, or accrued `reward()` [5](#0-4) ) that an attacker could inflate by sending KAIA directly to the pool address before a second staker deposits.
- Whether there's a minimum-deposit or dead-shares seeding mechanism at pool creation.

Because Kaia's `PublicDelegation` pools are created for individual validators/staking addresses (per the CL/staking pool model referenced in `kaiax/staking`), each pool could plausibly start with zero supply and zero/near-zero assets — the precise precondition the BakerFi bug requires. An unprivileged staker (the first depositor of a newly created `PublicDelegation` pool) could be the attacker in this analog: stake a minimal amount, then send additional native KAIA directly to the pool contract address to inflate `totalAssets()` relative to `totalSupply()`, then let a victim stake, and withdraw disproportionately more than fair share.

### Impact Explanation
If exploitable, an attacker could steal staked KAIA from subsequent depositors into the same `PublicDelegation` pool — a direct unauthorized value transfer, analogous in severity to the original finding (High). However, this is **speculative** given the missing source.
