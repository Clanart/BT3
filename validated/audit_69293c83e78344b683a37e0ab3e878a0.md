### Title
Attacker can front-run `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` with a dust deposit to snipe the entire `queuedRewards` balance accrued while `totalStaked() == 0` - (`File: rewards/BaseRewardPool.sol` / `rewards/BaseRewardPoolV2.sol`, function `_provisionReward`)

### Summary
`_provisionReward` in both `BaseRewardPool.sol` and `BaseRewardPoolV2.sol` accumulates all rewards into `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` whenever `totalStaked() == 0`, and unlocks the entire accumulated balance into `rewardPerTokenStored` divided by whatever `totalStaked()` happens to be at the moment the next reward is queued. An attacker who monitors the mempool can deposit a dust amount of staking token right before that unlocking call, becoming (or dominating) the sole staker at that instant, and capture nearly 100% of rewards that accrued for the broader staker base while the pool was empty.

### Finding Description
When `totalStaked() == 0`, `_provisionReward` takes the branch: [1](#0-0) 
which simply accumulates `_amountReward` into `rewardInfo.queuedRewards` without updating `rewardPerTokenStored`. On the next call to `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` where `totalStaked() > 0`, the entire queued balance is flushed into the global index: [2](#0-1) 

The index update divides `(_amountReward + queuedRewards)` by `this.totalStaked()` *at that exact moment*. Since staking/unstaking in `MasterMagpie` is permissionless, has no lockup and no minimum holding period (`deposit`/`withdraw` in `MasterMagpie.sol`), an attacker can:
1. Wait until `totalStaked() == 0` for a pool (pool fully drained) and a reward manager begins queuing/donating rewards into `queuedRewards`.
2. Front-run the next `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` call (the one that will occur once `totalStaked() > 0` again) by calling `MasterMagpie.deposit(stakingToken, 1)` with a dust amount, making themselves the sole (or dominant) staker.
3. Let the manager's transaction execute: `rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored` jumps by `(queuedRewards + newAmount) * 10**decimals / totalStaked()`, where `totalStaked()` ≈ attacker's dust stake.
4. Immediately call `withdraw`/`getReward` (via `MasterMagpie._harvestBaseRewarder` triggered on withdraw, or `multiclaim`) to realize `earned()`, which computes `balanceOf(_account) * indexDelta / 10**decimals` — since the attacker held essentially all of `totalStaked()` at the moment of the index bump, their `earned()` equals nearly the entire flushed reward, regardless of how briefly they held their dust stake.
This works because there is no time-weighting, no minimum stake duration, and no per-second reward streaming for the branch handling the zero-`totalStaked()` catch-up — the full backlog is minted into the index atomically based on instantaneous stake, and `getReward`/`updateFor` don't require historical presence, only current `balanceOf` at index-update time via `_earned`. [3](#0-2) 

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of unclaimed yield belonging to legitimate future stakers. Any reward that accumulated in `queuedRewards` while the pool was empty (potentially significant, since managers/donors have no visibility into whether an attacker is about to snipe) can be redirected almost entirely to an attacker's 1-wei stake instead of being distributed to the pool's actual staker base once it repopulates. This matches the Immunefi "theft of unclaimed yield" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: a pool must reach `totalStaked() == 0` (fully drained — possible for smaller/niche pools or newly created pools before initial stakers arrive, or after a mass-exit event) and a reward manager/donor must call `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` while stake resumes. The attack requires only mempool visibility and a trivial-cost dust deposit/withdraw (gas plus 1 wei of staking token), no special privileges, and is repeatable every time the zero-total-staked condition recurs. Front-running is straightforward since `queueNewRewards`/`donateRewards` are public/permissioned-but-external calls visible in the mempool before confirmation.

### Recommendation
Do not let a single instantaneous depositor capture backlog rewards accrued during a zero-`totalStaked()` window. Options: (1) require a minimum staking duration or checkpoint-based (block/time) reward streaming instead of atomic full-flush division by instantaneous `totalStaked()`; (2) when transitioning out of `totalStaked() == 0`, snapshot `totalStaked()` at the pool-repopulation event (e.g., in `_deposit`) rather than at the next `queueNewRewards` call, or stream the queued backlog gradually; (3) add a cooldown/vesting on rewards earned immediately after a deposit that occurred while `queuedRewards > 0` was pending flush.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, `BaseRewardPoolV2` pool with a staking token and reward token; register a reward manager.
2. Have all existing stakers withdraw fully so `totalStaked() == 0`.
3. Reward manager calls `queueNewRewards(1000e18, rewardToken)` several times — assert `rewards[rewardToken].queuedRewards == 1000e18` (or cumulative) and `rewardPerTokenStored` unchanged.
4. Simulate mempool visibility: attacker calls `MasterMagpie.deposit(stakingToken, 1)` (1 wei) just before the manager's next `queueNewRewards(1e18, rewardToken)` call is mined.
5. Manager's `queueNewRewards` executes: assert `rewardPerTokenStored` jumps by `(1000e18+1e18)*10**decimals / 1`.
6. Attacker calls `MasterMagpie.withdraw(stakingToken, 1)` (triggers `_harvestBaseRewarder` → `getReward`), or calls `multiclaim`; assert attacker receives ≈`1001e18` reward tokens (minus negligible rounding) despite contributing 0 economic value and holding stake for a single block.
7. Assert this violates conservation: reward tokens transferred to attacker ≈ 100% of rewards intended for the broader staker base, while attacker's principal stake (1 wei) and time held are negligible — demonstrating disproportionate capture of pooled unclaimed yield.
