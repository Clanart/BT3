### Title
Reward-per-token accounting lets a lone/minimal locker capture an entire forfeited/queued reward batch when `totalStaked()` transitions from zero to non-zero right before accrual - ([File: rewards/vlMGPBaseRewarder.sol])

### Summary
`_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` and `queueNewRewards` branch on `totalStaked() == 0` at the moment they execute: if it is zero the amount is parked in `queuedRewards`, otherwise it is folded straight into `rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored` using whatever `totalStaked()` currently is as the sole denominator. Because this is a discrete, point-in-time update rather than a continuously time-weighted accrual, whoever holds (nearly) 100% of `totalStaked()` at the exact instant the queuing call executes captures (nearly) 100% of that reward slice, independent of how long they were staked or how large their stake is relative to the accrual period.

### Finding Description
`totalStaked()` returns `vlMGP.totalSupply()` [1](#0-0)  . Both reward-injection paths use it as the divisor at call time: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

A user's earned amount is computed as `balanceOf(_account) * (rewardPerTokenStored - userRewardPerTokenPaid) / 1e18`, i.e. purely from the delta in `rewardPerTokenStored` since the user's last checkpoint [4](#0-3) . `userRewardPerTokenPaid` is only refreshed via `updateReward`/`updateRewards` modifiers on `getReward`/`getRewards`, and via `_updateFor`, all of which read `balanceOf(_account)` at call time [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) . Neither `lock()` on `vlMGP` (which changes `balanceOf`) nor the reward-queuing functions checkpoint reward-per-token per unit time held; the increment is applied as a lump sum against the current `totalStaked()`, so if the attacker is the only staked balance (or the overwhelming majority) at the moment `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer`/`queueNewRewards` executes, the entire `rewardPerTokenStored` increase is sized to `_amountReward / attackerBalance`, and multiplying back by `attackerBalance` returns (approximately) the full `_amountReward` to the attacker — regardless of how many blocks/seconds the attacker actually held that balance.

Exploit flow:
1. Attacker monitors `totalStaked()` (i.e., `vlMGP.totalSupply()`) and waits for/observes it approaching 0, or more realistically front-runs a pending `queueMGP`/`queueNewRewards`/forfeiting `getReward` transaction in the mempool.
2. Attacker calls `lock()` on `vlMGP` for a minimal amount, becoming the (near) sole entry in `totalStaked()`.
3. A manager's `queueNewRewards` call, or any user's expiry-forfeiture path through `queueMGP` → `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer`, or another user's `getReward` → `_sendReward` → `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` executes while `totalStaked()` is dominated by the attacker's stake.
4. `rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored` jumps by `_amountReward * 1e18 / totalStaked()` where `totalStaked()` ≈ attacker's balance.
5. Attacker calls `getReward()`; `_earned` computes `attackerBalance * delta / 1e18` ≈ `_amountReward`, i.e., the attacker collects (almost) the whole batch.

Existing checks do not prevent this: there is no `nonReentrant`/time-lock guard on `lock()` itself relative to reward accrual, no minimum holding-period requirement before a balance counts toward `rewardPerToken` accrual, and the `totalStaked()==0` branch (meant to avoid division by zero) actually exacerbates the issue by deferring the whole reward into a single lump `queuedRewards` bucket that gets applied against whatever tiny `totalStaked()` exists on the very next queuing call.

### Impact Explanation
This is theft of previously forfeited/queued yield that should be shared among (or eventually accrue to) legitimate long-term lockers. Best matches "theft of unclaimed yield / forfeited share of other lockers." The magnitude is bounded by the size of the reward batch at the time of the attack (forfeiture amounts from `queueMGP`, or reward-token batches from `queueNewRewards`), which can be significant relative to a single distribution cycle.

### Likelihood Explanation
The most damaging variant (`totalStaked()` literally at 0, requiring every vlMGP holder to have fully unlocked) is a rare/edge bootstrap condition and hard to force externally without privileged control over other users. However, a much more feasible and repeatable variant does not require `totalStaked()==0` at all: any attacker who front-runs a manager's `queueNewRewards` or another user's forfeiting `getReward`/`queueMGP` transaction with a `lock()` sized to be large relative to current `totalStaked()` at that instant captures a disproportionate share of that specific reward increment, because the update is a lump-sum snapshot rather than time-weighted. This only requires: (a) mempool visibility of the triggering transaction, (b) enough MGP to temporarily dominate `totalStaked()` at that moment, and (c) a subsequent `getReward()` call. This is capital-efficient (funds can be immediately at risk of unlock penalties but that's separate from reward capture) and repeatable each time a reward/forfeiture batch is queued.

### Recommendation
Replace the lump-sum, snapshot-based `rewardPerTokenStored` update with continuous/time-weighted accrual (e.g., track `lastUpdateTime` and accrue reward-per-second based on `totalStaked()` integrated over time, as in standard Synbthetix-style `rewardRate`/`periodFinish` designs), so that a balance change immediately before a reward event cannot retroactively claim a share of the accrual period. At minimum, checkpoint (`_updateFor`) all reward tokens for the *entire staking set* — or require a minimum bonding period before a newly-locked balance can accrue rewards from already-forfeited/queued amounts — to prevent a last-moment lock from capturing rewards backed by other users' historical stake.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `VLMGP`, `MasterMagpie`, and `vlMGPBaseRewarder`; register a reward token.
2. Have `userA` lock a large MGP amount and let time pass such that some other locker's cooldown triggers a forfeiture (or simulate by calling `queueMGP` from an authorized manager mock with a forfeit amount) while `totalStaked()` is dominated by `userA`.
3. Have `userA` fully unlock so `totalStaked()` drops to (near) zero.
4. `attacker` calls `lock(1)` (minimal amount) right before a pending `queueNewRewards(amountReward, token)` call from the manager (simulate front-run by ordering transactions in the test).
5. Manager executes `queueNewRewards(amountReward, token)`.
6. Assert `rewarder.earned(attacker, token) ≈ amountReward` (within rounding), while `attacker`'s actual `balanceOf` and holding duration are negligible compared to the reward-generating period.
7. Assert that other historically staked users who contributed to `totalStaked()` over time but were not staked at the exact queuing block receive 0 for that reward increment, confirming reward share is not proportional to time-weighted stake (violating the conservation invariant).
