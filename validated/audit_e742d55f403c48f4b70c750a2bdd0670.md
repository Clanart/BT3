### Title
Arbitrary-token balance sweep in `ManualCompound.compound()` allows draining any ERC20 held by the contract - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`compound()` forwards the caller-supplied `_rewards[i][j]` addresses unchecked into a sweep that transfers `IERC20(_rewards[i][j]).balanceOf(address(this))` — the contract's entire balance of that token — to `msg.sender` whenever `compoundableRewards[_rewards[i][j]]` is false. Because `_rewards[i][j]` is never validated against the actual reward tokens registered for `_lps[i]` in `MasterMagpie`/the underlying rewarder, and because the sweep uses total contract balance rather than an amount attributable to the caller's own claim, any address can name an arbitrary ERC20 the contract happens to hold and receive its full balance.

### Finding Description
`compound()` first calls `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)` [1](#0-0) , which resolves to `_multiClaim(_stakingTokens, _account=msg.sender(attacker), _receiver=address(ManualCompound), _rewardTokens)` inside `MasterMagpie` [2](#0-1) . Reward-token claiming is delegated to `_claimBaseRewarder`, which passes the caller-supplied `_rewardTokens[i]` array straight into `rewarder.getRewards(_account, _receiver, _rewardTokens)` with no validation that these addresses are registered reward tokens of that pool's rewarder [3](#0-2) . Unrecognized token addresses simply resolve to zero `earned()`/`_sendReward` amounts rather than reverting, as seen in the analogous `getRewards` implementation [4](#0-3) .

Back in `ManualCompound.compound()`, the very next loop reads `rewardBalance = IERC20(_rewards[i][j]).balanceOf(address(this))` for every caller-chosen address in the nested `_rewards` array and, if `compoundableRewards[_rewards[i][j]]` is false, transfers the **entire current balance** to `msg.sender` [5](#0-4) . There is no check that:
- `_rewards[i][j]` is actually a reward token registered for `_lps[i]`,
- the balance being swept was produced by the caller's own claim in this same transaction, or
- `compoundableRewards[token]` stays reconciled with the canonical `rewards[i].tokenAddress` registry (`compoundableRewards` is only ever set in `addReward`/`removeReward` [6](#0-5) , and is never cross-checked against `_rewards[i][j]` at call time).

Since `ManualCompound` is a shared contract acting as `_receiver` for many users' claims, and since conversions (`convertFor`/`lockFor`/`depositFor`) or fee/rounding behavior of external tokens can leave residual balances after a transaction, or an admin/other flow can leave dust, the contract can hold non-zero balances of arbitrary tokens across blocks. Any caller can then invoke `compound()` naming that token in `_rewards[i][j]` for any `_lps[i]` they are eligible to call multiclaim for (even with zero real rewards pending) and receive the contract's full balance of that unrelated token, regardless of whether it was ever meant for them.

### Impact Explanation
This is a direct theft primitive: an unprivileged caller can drain the ManualCompound contract's holdings of any ERC20 token it is not tracking as "compoundable," by simply naming that token address in an otherwise unrelated `compound()` call. This matches Critical - Direct theft of user funds, since balances belonging to other users' pending/residual claims or protocol-held tokens can be redirected entirely to the attacker.

### Likelihood Explanation
No special capital or privileged role is required — only a normal EOA capable of calling `compound()` with self-chosen arrays; the LP pool referenced merely needs to be one the caller can query multiclaim for (even with zero pending reward), and the target token just needs a non-zero balance sitting in the contract (achievable through natural dust/rounding accumulation over the contract's lifetime, not strictly a same-block race). This is repeatable each time the contract accrues a residual balance of an unregistered token.

### Recommendation
Validate that every `_rewards[i][j]` address is an actual reward token registered by the corresponding `_lps[i]` pool/rewarder before performing any balance read or transfer, and track a per-call claimed amount (e.g., balance-before/after delta scoped to *this* transaction's `multiclaimOnBehalf` call) instead of sweeping the full `balanceOf(address(this))`. Additionally, reconcile `compoundableRewards[token]` strictly against the `rewards[]` registry and reject any `_rewards[i][j]` entry not present in that registry or not returned as an actual reward token by the target rewarder.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat/Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie`, a rewarder, and `ManualCompound`; register `ManualCompound` as an authorized compounder in `MasterMagpie`.
2. Seed `ManualCompound` with a non-zero balance of an arbitrary ERC20 token `X` that is not registered via `addReward` (simulate residual/dust balance, e.g., direct `transfer` to the contract or leftover from a prior `convertFor` call).
3. As an unprivileged attacker with a valid but empty/zero-reward stake in some `_lps[0]`, call `compound(_lps=[lp0], _rewards=[[X]], _convertRatio=0, _minRec=0, _lockMgp=false)`.
4. Assert: `compoundableRewards[X] == false` while `IERC20(X).balanceOf(attacker)` increases by the entire prior contract balance of `X`, and `ManualCompound`'s balance of `X` becomes zero — demonstrating that `compoundableRewards[token]` is not reconciled with any registry check tying `_rewards[i][j]` to real reward tokens of `_lps[i]`, and that the attacker profited with a non-positive cost (only gas).
