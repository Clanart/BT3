### Title
PedPoP accepts the identity point as an encryption public key, making encrypted secret shares publicly decryptable - ([File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs](crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs))

### Summary

Analog of CVE-2025-45768 ("weak encryption; the library does not enforce a minimum/strength requirement on the supplied key"). `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` deserializes the ECDH public key with `C::read_G` (`encryption.rs:58`), which only enforces canonical encoding (`crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-100`) and, unlike `Curve::read_G` (`crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131`), does **not** reject the identity point. A participant who registers the identity as `enc_key` forces every ECDH computed against it to equal the identity (`encryption.rs:95-97`), so the ChaCha20 keystream used to encrypt secret shares to them (`encryption.rs:101-133`, `135-168`, `487-488`) is derived from a publicly known input. Anyone who obtains the ciphertext (relayed/broadcast `EncryptedMessage`s) can decrypt the victim's secret share.

### Finding Description

In `EncryptionKeyMessage::read`:

```rust
Ok(Self { msg: M::read(reader, params)?, enc_key: C::read_G(reader)? })
```

`C::read_G` performs canonical-encoding checks only. `Curve::read_G` exists precisely to reject identity, but PedPoP uses `Ciphersuite` bounds (`C: Ciphersuite`), not `Curve`, so the identity check is never applied to `enc_key`. The registered key is stored in `Decryption.enc_keys` without validation (`encryption.rs:360`) and later used as the ECDH peer in `encrypt` (`encryption.rs:154`, `466`):

```rust
cipher::<C>(context, &ecdh::<C>(&key, to)).apply_keystream(...)
// ecdh: Zeroizing::new(public * private.deref())
```

With `to = identity`, `ecdh = identity` regardless of the random per-message key. `cipher` then builds the ChaCha20 key solely from `transcript(DKG Encryption v0.2 || context || shared_key)` with a fixed IV — fully computable by any third party holding the context (context is a public, predictable Blake2s digest in `processor/src/key_gen.rs:184-197`).

There is also no PoP binding the registered `enc_key` (the PoP in `EncryptedMessage` covers only the per-message key, `encryption.rs:83-91`), so the malicious participant isn't even required to know a discrete log for the key they register — identity is fine.

### Impact Explanation

Every `EncryptedMessage` addressed to the malicious participant encrypts a FROST/PedPoP secret share. With identity ECDH, the ciphertext is plaintext-equivalent to any observer of the relayed shares. The revealed share `f_sender(malicious_i)` is an input to the victim sender's share verification and, combined with the other shares an attacker accumulates across attempts/participants, enables reconstruction of secret key material and ultimately the threshold group key. This is key share recovery — the encryption layer of the DKG provides zero confidentiality against it.

### Likelihood Explanation

Reachable by any DKG participant with only public inputs: they control the `enc_key` bytes inside their broadcast `EncryptionKeyMessage<Commitments>` in round 1 (read via `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` → `C::read_G` at `processor/src/key_gen.rs:268-274`). No collusion or privileged access required; a single honest participant encrypting to them suffices to leak that share to all observers of the ciphertext.

### Recommendation

Reject the identity point when reading/registering `enc_key` (apply the same `is_identity` check as `Curve::read_G`, or check in `Decryption::register`). Optionally add a proof-of-possession for `enc_key` so registered keys are known-discrete-log values, mirroring the PoP on per-message keys.

### Proof of Concept

1. Malicious participant `m` runs `KeyGenMachine::generate_coefficients`, then rewrites the `enc_key` field of its `EncryptionKeyMessage` to `C::G::identity().to_bytes()` (identity passes `C::read_G` canonical check) before broadcasting.
2. Honest participant `h` completes round 1, calls `generate_secret_shares`, producing `EncryptedMessage` to `m` where `cipher` is keyed by `ecdh(r, identity) = identity` — a keystream computable by anyone knowing the public `context`.
3. Any party relaying/observing the ciphertext decrypts `h`'s share to `m` with `ChaCha20::new(H(context || identity_bytes)[..32], "DKG IV v0.2\0")`.
