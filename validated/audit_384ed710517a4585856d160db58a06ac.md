### Title
Attacker-inflated block median fee causes Serai to sign Bitcoin transactions paying arbitrarily high fees - (File: processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs)

### Summary
The DeversiFi incident was a fee-calculation defect: malformed handling of fee parameters produced a transaction paying ~7676 ETH in gas for a trivial transfer. The analog in Serai is `Bitcoin::median_fee`, which derives the fee rate used for all outbound transactions directly from the per-transaction fee rates of the block being processed — data fully controllable by any unprivileged party who can broadcast Bitcoin transactions. `SignableTransaction::new` then multiplies this untrusted rate by the transaction vsize with no upper bound or sanity cap.

### Finding Description
`make_signable_transaction` fetches `block_for_fee` and calls `median_fee`, which computes, for every non-coinbase transaction in the block, `(sum(prevouts) - sum(outputs)) / vsize`, sorts the resulting fee rates, and takes `fees[fees.len() / 2]` as the sat/vbyte rate, floored at 1 [1](#0-0) . That `fee.0` is passed verbatim as `fee_per_vbyte` into `BSignableTransaction::new` [2](#0-1) .

Inside `SignableTransaction::new`, `needed_fee = fee_per_vbyte * vbytes` and, when a change output exists, `change = input_sat - payment_sat - fee_per_vbyte * vbytes_with_change` — the only checks are a minimum-fee floor and `input_sat >= payment_sat + needed_fee`. There is no maximum fee rate, no absolute fee cap, and no bound relative to the payment amounts [3](#0-2) .

The median inputs are entirely attacker-controlled: `median_fee` reads every transaction in `block.txdata[1..]` and trusts their fee/vsize ratios [4](#0-3) . An attacker who contributes more than half of the non-coinbase transactions in the sampled block with a high fee/vsize ratio sets the median arbitrarily high (a small transaction with a large fee yields a huge per-vbyte rate due to the division at line 406).

### Impact Explanation
Once the median is inflated to `R` sat/vbyte, every Serai Bitcoin plan processed against that block constructs transactions paying `R * vsize` in fees. If the Serai wallet's inputs can cover it, the transaction is signed by the FROST threshold and broadcast — burning `R * vsize` satoshis to miners per transaction, regardless of the actual payment amounts. This is signing of an economically unintended transaction: the threshold signs a tx whose fee field the attacker effectively chose. Repeated across plans/blocks it drains the wallet at roughly parity with the attacker's own fee expenditure (which they may partially recoup via mining). If inputs can't cover the inflated fee, `make_signable_transaction` returns `None` and the plan is dropped as unfulfillable, a secondary availability impact [5](#0-4) .

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires no privileges, no collusion, and no malformed data — only broadcasting ordinary valid Bitcoin transactions with self-chosen fees so they land in the block Serai samples for fee estimation. The fee-estimation block is the block currently being processed, so an attacker who can observe or anticipate Serai's processing cadence can time their high-fee transactions. The attacker's cost is the fees on their own transactions, and dominance requires exceeding half the block's non-coinbase transaction count; on low-traffic windows this is a small number of transactions. The economic loss to Serai is bounded by its spendable input value per transaction but repeats across every plan.

### Recommendation
Clamp the fee rate to a sane bound: e.g., `median_fee` should cap the result at a hard-coded maximum sat/vbyte (or a multiple of a long-term average) and/or reject per-transaction samples whose fee/vsize exceeds a threshold before taking the median. Alternatively use a trimmed mean or a fixed conservative fee rate. Additionally, `SignableTransaction::new` should enforce a maximum acceptable fee (absolute or relative to `payment_sat`) so that even a corrupted fee input fails closed rather than producing an overpaying transaction.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker monitors the Bitcoin chain for the block height Serai will process next (`block_number` used in `make_signable_transaction`).
2. Attacker broadcasts `k` small transactions (~110 vbytes each) each paying, e.g., 0.001 BTC fee (≈909 sat/vbyte), such that `k` exceeds half of the block's non-coinbase transaction count. In a block with few organic transactions, a handful suffices.
3. `median_fee` sorts the per-tx rates and returns ≥909 sat/vbyte [6](#0-5) .
4. For any pending Serai payment plan, `BSignableTransaction::new` computes `needed_fee = 909 * vsize`; for a ~200-vbyte transaction this is ~181,800 sat burned as fee, taken out of the change output or added to inputs consumed [7](#0-6) .
5. The FROST threshold signs and broadcasts the transaction; the excess fee is irrecoverable (modulo miner goodwill, as in the original incident).

    let mut fees = vec![];
    if block.txdata.len() > 1 {
      for tx in &block.txdata[1 ..] {
        let mut in_value = 0;
        for input in &tx.input {
          let mut input_tx = input.previous_output.txid.to_raw_hash().to_byte_array();
          input_tx.reverse();
          in_value += self
            .rpc
            .get_transaction(&input_tx)
            .await
            .map_err(|_| NetworkError::ConnectionError)?
            .output[usize::try_from(input.previous_output.vout).unwrap()]
          .value
          .to_sat();
        }
        let out = tx.output.iter().map(|output| output.value.to_sat()).sum::<u64>();
        fees.push((in_value - out) / u64::try_from(tx.vsize()).unwrap());
      }
    }
    fees.sort();
    let fee = fees.get(fees.len() / 2).copied().unwrap_or(0);

    // 1000 sat/kilo-vbyte minimum fee.
