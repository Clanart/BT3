### Title
Stranded reward dust from a previous `compound` call is swept and stolen by the next caller - (File: rewards/ManualCompound.sol)

### Summary
`compound()` computes the amount to convert/lock/deposit/forward for each registered reward token as the contract's *entire* `balanceOf(address(this))`, not the amount actually claimed by `msg.sender` in that specific call via `multiclaimOnBehalf`. Any dust left behind by a prior caller's partial convert, rounding in `lockFor`/`depositFor`, or an under-consuming helper/convertor accumulates on the contract and is fully swept to the very next caller, even one who supplies `_lps` pools where they hold no stake at all.

### Finding Description
`compound(address[] calldata _lps, address[][] calldata _rewards, uint256 _convertRatio, uint256 _minRec, bool _lockMgp)` [1](#0-0)  first calls `IMasterMagpie(masterMagpie).multiclaimOnBehalf(_lps, _rewards, msg.sender)`. This claims whatever rewards `msg.sender` is actually entitled to for the pools listed in `_lps`/`_rewards` — if the caller supplies pools where they have zero stake, or an empty `_lps` array, this claim yields zero tokens for them, and the call does not revert.

The function then iterates over `compoundableRewards`/`rewards` (state that is independent of the `_lps`/`_rewards` arguments passed in this call) and computes, for every registered reward token:
```
uint256 receivedBalance = IERC20(_tokenAddress).balanceOf(address(this));
``` [2](#0-1) 

This `receivedBalance` is treated as if it were entirely attributable to the current caller, and the full amount is either approved+forwarded into a convertor (`IConverter.convertFor`), a locker (`ILocker.lockFor`), a helper (`ISimpleHelper.depositFor`), or transferred directly via `safeTransfer(msg.sender, receivedBalance)` [3](#0-2) .

Because the contract never tracks per-call deltas (e.g. balance-before vs balance-after `multiclaimOnBehalf`), any residual token balance held by the contract before this call — arising from:
- rounding in a previous `convertFor`/`lockFor`/`depositFor` call that didn't consume 100% of the approved amount,
- a helper/convertor that under-consumes its input,
- or a partial conversion due to `_convertRatio`/`_minRec` slippage bounds from a prior caller,

is indistinguishable from tokens actually claimed by the current caller. The non-compoundable-reward sweep loop has the identical issue: it sends `IERC20(_rewards[i][j]).balanceOf(address(this))` to `msg.sender` for any token not in `compoundableRewards` [4](#0-3) , again the whole contract balance rather than a per-call delta.

No modifier restricts who can call `compound`, and there is no reconciliation check between what `multiclaimOnBehalf` returned to this caller and what is subsequently transferred out. This directly violates the stated invariant: residual value belonging to a previous caller becomes claimable by whoever calls `compound` next.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged attacker can call `compound` with `_lps` containing pools they hold no stake in (so `multiclaimOnBehalf` contributes nothing for them) and still receive/consume the entire dust balance of every registered reward token accumulated from other users' prior interactions. This is a direct theft of value that rightfully belongs to previous compounders (Critical - Direct theft of user funds), since the stranded dust — which should either be attributed to its original depositor or accumulate for legitimate protocol use — is instead handed to an arbitrary, non-contributing caller.

### Likelihood Explanation
The precondition (contract holding nonzero reward-token balance beyond what the current caller claimed) arises naturally from routine operation: helpers/convertors/lockers rarely consume exactly 100% of an approved amount due to rounding, and `_convertRatio`/`_minRec` driven partial swaps are an explicit feature of `IConverter.convertFor`. No special capital or timing is required — the attacker simply needs to front-run or simply be the next caller after any compound leaves dust, which is highly likely over the contract's lifetime given `compound` is permissionless and called repeatedly by many unrelated users/pools.

### Recommendation
Compute per-token deltas instead of relying on `balanceOf(address(this))`: record `balanceBefore` immediately prior to `multiclaimOnBehalf` and immediately before each conversion/lock/deposit/transfer step, then use `balanceAfter - balanceBefore` (or the value directly returned/reported by `multiclaimOnBehalf`) as the amount attributable to `msg.sender`. Any pre-existing dust must be swept by a privileged, accounted-for admin function (e.g., `sweepDust(address token, address to)`) rather than silently folded into the next unrelated caller's compound.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat test plan:
1. Deploy `MasterMagpie` mock, `ManualCompound`, a mock reward token, and a convertor mock that intentionally leaves 1% of `receivedBalance` unconsumed (simulating rounding/under-consumption) when `convertFor` is called.
2. User A stakes in pool P and accrues claimable reward R of token T. User A calls `compound([P], [[T]], ratio, minRec, false)`. Assert that the convertor is called with `receivedBalance = R`, but only consumes `0.99 * R`, leaving `0.01 * R` of T stranded on `ManualCompound`.
3. User B (attacker), who has zero stake in any pool, calls `compound([unrelatedPool], [[]], ratio, minRec, false)` (or even `compound([], [], ...)`), where `multiclaimOnBehalf` returns/transfers 0 tokens of T to the contract for B.
4. Assert that despite B claiming 0 T via `multiclaimOnBehalf`, the second loop in `compound` reads `IERC20(T).balanceOf(address(this)) == 0.01 * R` (User A's leftover dust) and forwards it into the convertor/locker/helper or directly to B, i.e. B receives value derived entirely from A's stranded dust.
5. Assert final state: `IERC20(T).balanceOf(address(ManualCompound)) == 0` while B's on-chain claimed amount via `multiclaimOnBehalf` was `0`, proving the residual `0.01 * R` was misattributed to B instead of A.
