### Title
Unprivileged fee-median manipulation causes Serai to sign Bitcoin transactions paying arbitrarily inflated fees (no max-fee bound) - (File: processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs)

### Summary
The Carapace bug class is: a caller commits to an action (withdrawal), and before it executes a permissionless `assessState` call changes the exchange terms so the caller receives far less than expected — with no min-amount-out bound. The Serai analog lives in `Bitcoin::median_fee` / `make_signable_transaction`: the fee rate committed inside every signed Bitcoin transaction is derived from the *median fee of a single attacker-influenced block*, and `SignableTransaction::new` applies that rate with no upper bound or sanity cap. An unprivileged party who lands high-fee transactions in the sampled block raises the median, causing the threshold multisig to sign transactions whose change output is drained into excess miner fees.

### Finding Description
`make_signable_transaction` selects the fee entirely from `median_fee(block_for_fee)` for the `block_number` the plan is evaluated against (`processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs:429-431`). `median_fee` computes `(in_value - out) / vsize` for every non-coinbase transaction in that one block, sorts them, and takes `fees.get(fees.len() / 2)` — the lower median — clamped only below at `1` sat/vbyte (`processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs:388-414`). There is no upper clamp.

`needed_fee` and `signable_transaction` both funnel through this (`processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs:803-834`), and `SignableTransaction::new` then enforces `needed_fee = fee_per_vbyte * vbytes`, reducing the change output by `payment_sat + fee_with_change`, or turning the whole remainder into fee when no change output exists (`networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs:206-235`). The resulting transaction is signed via `TransactionSignMachine::sign` with `Prevouts::All`, committing signatures to that attacker-influenced fee (`networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs:373-390`). Like the Carapace `withdraw`, the signing request is queued at one point in time and executed against state (the sampled block's fee median) that an outsider can shift in between.

### Impact Explanation
Every sat of inflated fee is taken from Serai's change output (or becomes raw fee when there is no change), i.e., funds the multisig intended to retain are burned to miners. The signed transaction is valid and irreversible — the protocol signs a message (a Bitcoin tx paying an unintended fee) it never would have consented to with accurate fee information. Repeated across plans/blocks this is an unbounded drain on change reserves proportional to how far the attacker pushes the median.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged party can send Bitcoin transactions. To move the *median* of a block, the attacker needs ≥ half of that block's transactions to carry a high fee rate — trivially achievable when mining a block (self-paid fees are free) or by flooding a block during low-activity periods with high-fee transactions at the cost of the fees themselves. No validator key, RPC access, or collusion is required — only public Bitcoin transactions, matching the reachability rules. The cost/benefit favors persistence attacks during quiet mempool periods; on a busy chain it requires mining, so Medium rather than High.

### Recommendation
Bound `median_fee` above — e.g., clamp to a protocol-level `MAX_FEE_RATE` (or a multiple of the relay minimum / a fee-estimate RPC cross-check), and/or average the median over several recent blocks instead of a single `block_number`. Additionally, have `prepare_send`/`signable_transaction` reject (rather than sign) when the derived fee exceeds the declared fee context of the plan — the equivalent of Carapace's recommended `minAmountOut`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a low-activity period and broadcasts enough high-fee transactions (e.g., 1000 sat/vbyte) that they constitute the majority of transactions in Bitcoin block `B` — or mines `B` with self-paying high-fee transactions at zero net cost.
2. Serai's processor evaluates a withdrawal plan at `block_number = B`. `median_fee` (`processor/src/networks/bitcoin.rs:388-414`) returns ~1000 sat/vbyte instead of the ~1–5 sat/vbyte market rate.
3. `make_signable_transaction` → `BSignableTransaction::new` sets `needed_fee = 1000 * vbytes` and shrinks the change output by that amount (`networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs:227-234`).
4. The FROST `TransactionSignMachine` signs the transaction committing to this fee via `taproot_key_spend_signature_hash(i, &Prevouts::All(...))` (`networks/bitcoin/src/wallet/send.rs:383-390`).
5. On publication, `sum(inputs) - sum(outputs)` pays the inflated fee to miners; Serai's change output — user/protocol funds — is reduced by the difference, with no error or bound tripped anywhere in the pipeline.
