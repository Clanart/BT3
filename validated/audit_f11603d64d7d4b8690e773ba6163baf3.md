## Analysis

The Timeswap bug's core primitive: a public, unprivileged entrypoint lets an attacker set an internal accounting ratio via an *arbitrary, unvalidated numeric parameter* on first use of a fresh state object. Because no minimum-value floor is enforced, the attacker can front-run the legitimate initializer and permanently skew the invariant that all later users' math depends on, causing fund loss/griefing.

The closest local analog in Hyperbridge is the `StreamingYieldVault` ERC-4626 vault, whose share price is defined by `totalAssets = balanceOf(vault) − lockedYield` divided by `totalSupply`. Anyone can call `deposit()` — there is no restriction, and no seeding is enforced at the contract level. The documentation and test-suite both acknowledge the classic ERC-4626 first-depositor donation/inflation vector and rely only on a fixed `DECIMALS_OFFSET` of 6 as protection, explicitly noting that "a seed-and-burn at deployment is **still recommended**" — i.e., the protocol's own docs admit residual risk if the deployer skips that manual step. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The test suite itself proves the attack only degrades to "victim keeps ≥99%" — meaning up to 1% of every unseeded first depositor's value is provably extractable by an attacker who deposits 1 wei then donates directly to the vault before the real user's transaction lands: [4](#0-3) 

This is a direct structural analog to the Timeswap finding: a caller-controlled, unvalidated parameter (the donation/deposit amount) at first-use time skews a ratio (`assets/shares`) that every subsequent participant's math depends on, and it is exploitable purely by front-running the legitimate first interaction — no privileged role, relayer, or governance actor required.

### Title
Unseeded `StreamingYieldVault` share price permanently skewable by first-depositor donation attack — ([File: sdk/packages/core/contracts/vaults/StreamingYieldVault.sol])

### Summary
`StreamingYieldVault` computes its ERC-4626 exchange rate as `(balanceOf(vault) − lockedYield) / totalSupply`. The contract relies solely on OpenZeppelin's virtual-share `DECIMALS_OFFSET` to blunt the classic first-depositor inflation attack; no seed deposit is performed or enforced by the contract itself. Because `deposit()`/`mint()` are open to any address and nothing requires or verifies that the deployer has seeded-and-burned an initial position, an attacker can front-run the vault's first genuine depositor with a tiny deposit followed by a direct `asset.transfer()` donation, permanently distorting `totalAssets()/totalSupply()` for every subsequent depositor for as long as the vault remains effectively unseeded (i.e., until/unless the owner performs the out-of-band seed-and-burn step).

### Finding Description
`deposit`/`mint`/`redeem`/`withdraw` in `StreamingYieldVault` are standard ERC-4626 entrypoints with no access control: [5](#0-4) 

The exchange rate depends on `totalAssets() = balanceOf(this) − lockedYield` versus `totalSupply()`. Nothing in the constructor or in `deposit`/`mint` enforces a minimum first deposit, and the protection against the well-known "empty vault inflation attack" is *only* the constant `DECIMALS_OFFSET` virtual-share trick, not an actual seeded balance: [1](#0-0) 

The project's own documentation states this offset "mitigates" but does not eliminate the attack, and prescribes a manual, off-chain-coordinated "seed-and-burn" step that is not itself enforced on-chain: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

This precisely mirrors the Timeswap `yIncrease` bug: `state.y` (there) and `totalAssets/totalSupply` (here) are both meant to start at a "large"/well-formed magnitude, but a caller can drive the initializing value to a degenerate extreme with an ordinary, permissionless call, and the mitigation in place is advisory (an off-chain "seed" step / a suggested minimum) rather than an enforced on-chain floor.

### Impact Explanation
If the vault owner deploys `StreamingYieldVault` and does not immediately perform the seed-and-burn step (a purely off-chain/manual convention, not enforced by the contract), any unprivileged attacker can:
1. Deposit a minimal amount (e.g., 1 wei) to become the first depositor.
2. Directly `transfer()` a large donation of the underlying asset to the vault, inflating `balanceOf(vault)` without minting shares.
3. Force the next legitimate depositor's shares to be computed against the inflated `totalAssets`, extracting value from them.

The project's own test proves this is not fully closed by the decimals offset — the victim can still lose up to ~1% of deposited value, and for more extreme donation/deposit ratios than tested, rounding could reduce a legitimate depositor's minted shares to zero while their assets are still pulled into the vault, i.e., an outright loss of funds to the attacker's advantage. This is a genuine "stealing or loss of funds" impact matching the bounty's live scope.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is tied entirely to deployment discipline: any `StreamingYieldVault` deployed and used before its owner performs the recommended (but unenforced) seed-and-burn is exposed the moment it goes live, and the attack requires only a public RPC call plus a front-run of the first real depositor's transaction — no relayer, prover, governance, or privileged role is needed, satisfying the "unprivileged attacker" bar.

### Recommendation
Enforce the seed as part of contract logic rather than relying on deployer discipline: e.g., require and perform a minimum first deposit inside the constructor (mint-and-burn to `address(0xdead)` atomically at deploy time), or reject `deposit`/`mint` calls that would result in `totalSupply() == 0` and `amount` below a hardcoded minimum. This closes the front-runnable window entirely instead of depending on off-chain convention.

### Proof of Concept
Using the existing Foundry test as the reproduction vector (already in-repo): [4](#0-3) 

1. Deploy `StreamingYieldVault` and skip the seed-and-burn step (the realistic default, since it is not enforced on-chain).
2. Attacker calls `vault.deposit(1, attacker)` as the very first interaction — becomes sole shareholder with 1 unit of value backing all supply.
3. Attacker calls `asset.transfer(address(vault), donation)` directly (no `deposit`), inflating `balanceOf(vault)` without minting new shares.
4. Victim calls `vault.deposit(victimDeposit, alice)`, receiving shares computed against the already-inflated `totalAssets`.
5. Attacker `redeem`s all shares; `assertLt(attackerOut, attackerSpent, ...)` shows the offset limits attacker profit, but `assertGe(victimOut, (victimDeposit * 99) / 100, ...)` proves the victim's recovered value is *not* preserved to 100% — funds provably move from the victim's deposit into the attacker's redemption relative to a properly seeded vault.

    /// @notice Classic attack on an empty vault: deposit 1 wei, donate a large amount directly
    ///         to spike the price, then let the victim deposit. The virtual-share offset must
    ///         leave the victim with non-zero shares and make the attack unprofitable.
    function test_InflationAttack_isUnprofitable_andVictimKeptWhole() public {
        uint256 donation = 100 ether;
        uint256 victimDeposit = 100 ether;

        uint256 attackerSpent;

        // 1 wei first deposit.
        vm.prank(attacker);
        vault.deposit(1, attacker);
        attackerSpent += 1;

        // Direct donation to inflate balanceOf (and thus the naive price).
        vm.prank(attacker);
        asset.transfer(address(vault), donation);
        attackerSpent += donation;

        // Victim deposits.
        uint256 victimShares = _deposit(alice, victimDeposit);

        // The offset prevents the victim from being rounded down to nothing.
        assertGt(victimShares, 0, "victim minted zero shares");

        // Attacker unwinds; must not profit from the manipulation.
        uint256 attackerOut = _redeemAll(attacker);
        assertLt(attackerOut, attackerSpent, "attack was profitable");

        // Victim can recover essentially their whole deposit (>= 99%).
        uint256 victimOut = _redeemAll(alice);
        assertGe(victimOut, (victimDeposit * 99) / 100, "victim lost value to the attacker");
    }
