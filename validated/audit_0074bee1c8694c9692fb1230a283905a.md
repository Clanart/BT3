### Title
Unprotected `initializer` on upgradeable `mWOM` contract allows front-running takeover and theft of the initial mint - ([File: wombat/mWOM.sol])

### Summary
`mWOM` is an OpenZeppelin upgradeable contract (`Initializable`, `ERC20Upgradeable`, `OwnableUpgradeable`) deployed behind a `TransparentUpgradeableProxy`. Its initializer, `__mWom_init`, is a `public` function guarded only by the standard `initializer` modifier, with no access control restricting who may call it and no `_disableInitializers()` call in a constructor to lock the logic/implementation contract. Anyone monitoring the mempool can front-run the legitimate deployer's initialization call.

### Finding Description
`__mWom_init` sets the core protocol wiring for `mWOM` — `wombatStaking` and `wom` addresses — and calls `__Ownable_init()`, which makes `msg.sender` the owner, followed immediately by an unconditional mint of `_initMintAmt` tokens to that owner: [1](#0-0) 

This function is `public` and callable by any address as long as `initializer` has not already fired (i.e., the contract has been deployed but not yet initialized). There is no restriction such as `onlyOwner`, no deployer allow-list, and no constructor calling `_disableInitializers()` to lock the logic contract per OpenZeppelin's upgradeable-contract guidance. Because deployment (proxy creation) and initialization are two separate transactions in this codebase's `TransparentUpgradeableProxy`/`ProxyAdmin` deployment flow, an attacker watching the mempool for the deployer's `__mWom_init(...)` transaction can submit their own call with higher gas to the same uninitialized proxy (or to the uninitialized implementation contract directly) and win the race.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who successfully front-runs initialization becomes the `owner` of the `mWOM` token contract and receives the entire `_initMintAmt` supply of `mWOM` directly to their own address via the unconditional `_mint(owner(), _initMintAmt)` call. They can also set `wombatStaking` and `wom` to attacker-controlled addresses, meaning any subsequent `convert`/`deposit`/`incentiveDeposit` flows that route WOM through `wombatStaking` for locking would be redirected. Since `mWOM` sits at the center of the "mWOM minting" flow used by `WombatStaking` and `SmartWomConvert`, an attacker-controlled `mWOM` deployment results in outright theft of the initial minted supply and permanent compromise of protocol wiring for this token, requiring redeployment — a direct theft-of-funds and insolvency-class impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires only observing a public, unprivileged initialization transaction in the mempool and front-running it with a higher gas price — no special privileges, governance rights, or insider access are needed. The deployer's initialization transaction is a normal, unprotected transaction reachable by any wallet capable of watching mempool activity, making this practically exploitable on any public chain deployment.

### Recommendation
Add a constructor to `mWOM` (and any other upgradeable logic contracts following this pattern) that calls `_disableInitializers()` to lock the implementation contract at deployment time. For the proxy, ensure the initializer call is bundled atomically with proxy deployment (e.g., pass `initialize` calldata into the proxy constructor) rather than issued as a separate follow-up transaction, eliminating the front-runnable window.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deployer deploys the `mWOM` implementation and a `TransparentUpgradeableProxy` pointing to it, but has not yet called `__mWom_init`.
2. Deployer broadcasts a transaction calling `__mWom_init(wombatStaking, wom, initMintAmt)` on the proxy.
3. Attacker observes this pending transaction in the mempool and submits their own call to `__mWom_init(attackerWombatStaking, attackerWom, initMintAmt)` on the same proxy with a higher gas price.
4. Attacker's transaction is mined first; `initializer` modifier marks the contract as initialized, attacker becomes `owner()`, and `_initMintAmt` of `mWOM` is minted directly to the attacker.
5. The deployer's original transaction reverts (already initialized), and the protocol's `mWOM` token is now permanently controlled by the attacker, requiring redeployment.
