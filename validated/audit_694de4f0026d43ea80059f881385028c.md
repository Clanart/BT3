### Title
Native KAIA donation to `PublicDelegation` can distort share price for stakers before any depegation protection is confirmed - (File: contracts/bindings/publicdelegation/PublicDelegation.go)

### Summary
The `PublicDelegation` system contract implements an ERC4626-style share-accounting staking vault for native KAIA: it exposes `stake()`/`stakeFor()` (payable) to mint shares, `convertToShares`/`convertToAssets`/`previewDeposit`/`previewRedeem` to price shares against `totalAssets()`, and a `receive() payable` fallback. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1)  This is the same architectural pattern (assets/shares ratio computed from a live balance) that produced the StakedUSDe `MinShares` DoS in the referenced report.

### Finding Description
Because `PublicDelegation` declares a `receive() payable` entry point in its ABI, any unprivileged account can send native KAIA to the contract address directly, bypassing `stake()`/`stakeFor()` entirely. [3](#0-2)  If `totalAssets()` is computed from the contract's live KAIA balance (as is standard for this vault pattern, mirroring `StakedUSDe.totalAssets()` returning `_asset.balanceOf(address(this))` in the referenced report) then a direct transfer inflates `totalAssets()` without minting any corresponding shares via `totalSupply()`. Because `convertToShares`/`previewDeposit` divide by `totalAssets()+1`, an attacker can front-run the first staker (or any staker at a moment when supply is small) by sending a large, unmatched KAIA balance directly into the contract, which skews the price-per-share and can round subsequent legitimate `stake()` calls down to zero shares — an unauthorized value-movement / DoS vector identical in mechanism to the referenced `MIN_SHARES`/donation bug, but here reachable through Kaia's own staking/reward-distribution system contract rather than a third-party token vault.

### Impact Explanation
If exploited, a malicious staker can (a) cause legitimate stakers' `stake()` transactions to mint zero shares — silently donating their KAIA to existing/no shareholders — or (b) brick the contract for further staking until redeployed, both of which constitute denial of service and/or unauthorized value transfer within a staking and reward-distribution system contract that is reachable by any unprivileged sender who can submit a plain-value transfer transaction to the contract's address.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: the attack only requires knowledge of the contract address (public, since it is a governance-facing public delegation/staking contract) and the ability to send a plain native-KAIA transfer — no special privileges, and it is most potent immediately after deployment/at low `totalSupply()`, which an attacker can front-run.

### Recommendation
Verify and, if absent, add: (1) a virtual-offset / minimum-shares floor consistent with OpenZeppelin's ERC4626 `_decimalsOffset` mitigation, (2) reject or account for unsolicited native-KAIA transfers in `totalAssets()` (e.g., track internal accounting rather than raw `address(this).balance`), and (3) enforce a minimum first-deposit size so no depositor can mint to a near-zero share supply.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy/observe `PublicDelegation` at address `P` with `totalSupply() == 0`.
2. Attacker sends `X` KAIA directly to `P` via the `receive()` fallback (not via `stake()`), inflating `totalAssets()` to `X` while `totalSupply()` remains `0`.
3. A legitimate staker calls `stake()` with amount `Y` (even `Y` significantly less than `X`); `convertToShares(Y)` computed as `Y * (totalSupply()+offset) / (totalAssets()+1)` rounds down to `0` shares, so the staker's KAIA is absorbed into the pool with no share entitlement — reproducing the `MinSharesViolation`/donation-DoS pattern from the referenced report, applied to Kaia's `PublicDelegation` staking contract.
