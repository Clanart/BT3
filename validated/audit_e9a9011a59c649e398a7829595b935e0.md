### Title
Missing upper-bound check on oracle timestamps permanently poisons the monotonic `last-update` tracker, freezing price resolution for an asset — (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
The bug class in the Covalent report is: a permissionless actor can submit data timestamped ahead of the real chain time, and because the affected contract has no upper bound on how far in the future a submitted timestamp may be, that future value gets latched in as a reference point that improperly shrinks the effective validity window available to honest participants afterward. The same structural flaw exists in Zest's own oracle staleness/monotonicity logic in `oracle-timestamp-fresh` and `price-resolve`.

### Finding Description
`oracle-timestamp-fresh` computes staleness as:

```clarity
(define-private (oracle-timestamp-fresh (ts uint) (prev uint) (max-staleness uint))
  (let ((delta (if (> ts stacks-block-time)
                   u0
                   (- stacks-block-time ts))))
    (and
      (<= delta max-staleness)
      (>= ts prev))))
``` [1](#0-0) 

There is no check rejecting `ts` values that are ahead of `stacks-block-time`; instead, whenever `ts > stacks-block-time`, `delta` is forced to `u0`, so the `<= max-staleness` gate is trivially satisfied regardless of the configured per-asset staleness threshold. This is called from `price-resolve`, which also unconditionally advances the monotonic tracker whenever a "newer" timestamp is seen:

```clarity
(asserts! (and (oracle-price-legal final-price) (oracle-timestamp-fresh timestamp last-update-time max-staleness))
          ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT)
(if (> timestamp last-update-time)
    (map-set last-update key timestamp)
    false)
``` [2](#0-1) 

Because there is no upper bound on `timestamp` relative to `stacks-block-time`, a single accepted price update whose `publish-time` is ahead of the real chain clock writes that future value into `last-update` for the `{type, ident}` key. From that point on, every subsequent, correctly-timestamped, real-time price update fails the monotonicity clause `(>= ts prev)` in `oracle-timestamp-fresh` (since real timestamps will be smaller than the poisoned future value stored in `last-update`), causing `price-resolve` (and therefore `price-multi-resolve`) to revert with `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` for that feed until the real chain time finally advances past the poisoned future value.

This mirrors the Covalent root cause exactly: a submission with a timestamp set ahead of the real reference point is accepted without an upper-bound check, and that acceptance permanently (until real time "catches up") degrades the reliability/duration of the window relied upon by honest participants — here, the ability to resolve any price for the affected feed at all.

### Impact Explanation
Every market operation that requires price resolution for the affected feed — borrow, withdraw, health checks, and critically `liquidate` — calls into `price-resolve`/`price-multi-resolve`. Once the `last-update` map for a feed is poisoned with a future timestamp, all of those calls revert with `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` until real time passes the poisoned value. This directly enables a temporary freeze of the ability to liquidate under-collateralized positions on the affected asset, and blocks withdrawals/repayments gated on price resolution — a temporary freezing of funds/unclaimed yield for lenders and the protocol, matching the in-scope "High" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching this requires a price update whose `publish-time`/oracle timestamp is ahead of `stacks-block-time` to be accepted by `price-resolve` at least once. `resolve-dia`/`resolve-pyth` pass through whatever timestamp the upstream feed reports without any Zest-side check that it isn't in the future [3](#0-2) , so any clock skew, relayer misbehavior, or delayed/queued update batch that lands with a forward-dated timestamp is sufficient — Zest's own contract does not defend against it regardless of the upstream source.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit upper-bound check rejecting timestamps ahead of `stacks-block-time` (plus a small allowed clock-skew tolerance) before treating a price as fresh, instead of silently clamping `delta` to `u0`:

```clarity
(define-private (oracle-timestamp-fresh (ts uint) (prev uint) (max-staleness uint))
  (and
    (<= ts stacks-block-time)            ;; reject future timestamps outright
    (<= (- stacks-block-time ts) max-staleness)
    (>= ts prev)))
```

Additionally, consider bounding how far `last-update` can jump per accepted update, so a single anomalous/forward-dated timestamp cannot permanently lock out subsequent legitimate updates.

### Proof of Concept
1. A price update for feed `{type, ident}` is relayed with `publish-time = stacks-block-time + N` (N large, e.g. minutes/hours ahead), passing `oracle-timestamp-fresh` because `delta` is forced to `u0` per lines 366-368.
2. `price-resolve` accepts it and executes `(map-set last-update key timestamp)` since `timestamp > last-update-time` (lines 391-393), storing the future value.
3. All later legitimate updates for that feed carry real, smaller timestamps, so `(>= ts prev)` fails in `oracle-timestamp-fresh`, and `price-resolve`/`price-multi-resolve` revert with `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` for every caller (borrow, withdraw, liquidate) that needs a price for this feed.
4. This condition persists until `stacks-block-time` naturally advances past the poisoned future value, during which under-collateralized positions on the affected asset cannot be liquidated. [4](#0-3)
