Confirmed: `process_outbound_request_delivery_claim` at [1](#0-0)  and `process_outbound_consensus_delivery_claim` at [2](#0-1)  both look up the reward amount from live governance storage (`OutboundRequestDeliveryReward`/`OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward`) at claim-processing time rather than at the time the underlying delivery/rotation actually happened. This is the direct structural analog of the `updateFeeType()` bug: the amount owed for a completed, immutable piece of work is not fixed until an unbounded time later, so a rate change after the work occurred changes the payout for that already-completed work.

### Title
Outbound delivery rewards are priced at claim time, not at delivery time, letting a relayer extract inflated treasury payouts for old deliveries by delaying submission past a reward increase - (File: modules/pallets/relayer/src/outbound_request.rs, modules/pallets/relayer/src/outbound_consensus.rs)

### Summary
`claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` and `claim_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward` are permissionless, unsigned-origin extrinsics that pay out `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward[module_id]` / `OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward[destination]` [3](#0-2) [2](#0-1)  read from current governance storage. Neither the request commitment nor the consensus rotation carries the reward rate that was in effect when the work was actually delivered — there is no snapshot of the rate at dispatch/delivery time. Because the claim can be submitted at any point after delivery (no expiry, no deadline tied to the reward that was advertised), a relayer can withhold submission of an already-completed, already-provable delivery until governance raises the reward, then submit the old claim and collect the new, higher amount from the treasury.

### Finding Description
The reward-setting extrinsics `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward` and `set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward` overwrite the storage value in place with no history and no effect on in-flight/unclaimed work [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) .

The claim-processing functions verify delivery via a state proof of the destination's receipt/epoch slot, but pull the payout amount fresh from storage at verification time:
- `process_outbound_request_delivery_claim`: `let reward = OutboundRequestDeliveryReward::<T>::get(&module_id); ensure!(reward > 0, ...)` then transfers `reward` from the treasury [6](#0-5) .
- `process_outbound_consensus_delivery_claim`: `let reward = OutboundConsensusDeliveryReward::<T>::get(destination); ensure!(reward > 0, ...)` then transfers `reward` [7](#0-6) .

Idempotency is keyed only on "has this commitment/rotation ever been claimed", not on "what was the rate when it was delivered": `OutboundRequestsClaimed[commitment]` and `OutboundConsensusRotationsClaimed[destination, set_id]` [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) . There is no deadline or expiry tying the claim window to the rate that was live at delivery time — a proof of an old delivery (any past `RequestCommitments`/`_epochs[set_id]` entry) remains claimable indefinitely at whatever the *current* reward happens to be, exactly like the reported `FeeCollector` flaw where `distributeCollectedFees()` applies the *current* `feeTypes` rates to `collectedFees` that were accrued under a stale rate.

This is the same broken invariant as the seed report: work is performed/committed at time T0 under rate R0, but settlement at time T1 uses rate R1 with no binding between the two. In the reference bug this misattributes previously-collected protocol fees to the wrong distribution split; here it lets whoever controls (or merely observes) claim timing convert old, already-completed deliveries into payouts at a rate the protocol never intended for that specific delivery.

### Impact Explanation
This directly produces incorrect fund movement out of the Hyperbridge treasury (`TreasuryPalletId` account) [10](#0-9) : the amount paid for a given delivery is not fixed by the delivery, it floats with whatever the reward parameter is set to at the moment the claim happens to land. A relayer who delivers messages continuously accrues a backlog of unclaimed, already-provable claims (`RequestCommitments`/receipt proofs never expire) and can time submission to whichever period has the highest configured reward, extracting more BRIDGE from the treasury than governance intended to pay for those specific deliveries. Because the claim extrinsics are unsigned and permissionless (`ensure_none(origin)` plus `validate_unsigned`) [11](#0-10) [12](#0-11) , any relayer holding a valid delivery signature can execute this without needing elevated privileges, a compromised operator, or a malicious peer — only ordinary participation in relaying plus normal patience.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reward rates are expected to be tuned over time by governance (that's the entire purpose of `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward`/`set_outbound_consensus_delivery_reward`), so rate changes are a routine, not rare, event. Any relayer that has delivered messages simply needs to hold its claim (a purely off-chain decision — nothing on-chain forces prompt submission) until it observes a reward increase, then submit. No race with other relayers is required beyond the normal one-claim-per-commitment idempotency, and no cryptographic or consensus assumption is broken.

### Recommendation
Bind the payout rate to the delivery event rather than to claim-processing time:
- Snapshot the reward rate into the same storage entry that proves delivery eligibility (e.g., record `(module_id/destination, rate)` at the moment `RequestCommitments`/`_epochs[set_id]` is written, or store a rate history keyed by block/height and have the claim reference the rate that was current at delivery height), or
- Require the claim to be submitted within a bounded window after the delivery height, after which the claim can only pay out at the rate that was in effect during that window, or
- Snapshot the current reward into a per-commitment/per-rotation field at dispatch/first-receipt time instead of reading live governance storage at claim time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Governance sets `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward[module_id] = R0` via `set_outbound_request_delivery_reward` [13](#0-12) .
2. A hyperbridge-originated request from `module_id` is dispatched and successfully delivered to its destination by relayer `A`; the destination's `RequestReceipts[commitment]` now proves `A` delivered it, but `A` does not submit `claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` yet.
3. Governance later raises the rate: `OutboundRequestDeliveryReward[module_id] = R1` with `R1 > R0`.
4. `A` now submits `claim_outbound_request_delivery_reward` with a valid state proof of the (unchanged, already-existing) `RequestReceipts[commitment]` entry from step 2.
5. `process_outbound_request_delivery_claim` reads `reward = OutboundRequestDeliveryReward::<T>::get(&module_id)`, which is now `R1`, and transfers `R1` (not `R0`) from the treasury to `A` [14](#0-13) . `A` collected `R1 - R0` more than the protocol intended to pay for that specific, already-completed delivery, purely by delaying the claim.
