### Title
PedPoP encryption keys accepted without identity check let shares be stored effectively in plaintext - ([File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs](crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs))

### Summary
The PedPoP DKG encrypts each secret share with a ChaCha20 keystream derived solely from an ECDH between a fresh ephemeral key and the recipient's registered `enc_key`. `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` and `Decryption::register` accept `enc_key` with no validation — in particular, no rejection of the identity element and no proof of possession. A participant who registers `enc_key = identity` forces every sender's `ecdh()` result to be the identity point, whose encoding is a known public constant. The cipher key is therefore publicly derivable, and every `EncryptedMessage` addressed to that participant is plaintext-equivalent to any observer of the wire — the analog of storing a secret in an unencrypted config file readable by an unauthorized party.

### Finding Description
- `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` parses `enc_key` via `C::read_G(reader)?` with no identity/torsion rejection (encryption.rs:57-59).
- `Decryption::register` stores `msg.enc_key` directly into `enc_keys` with no validity check (encryption.rs:351-362).
- Senders call `Encryption::encrypt` → `encrypt()`, computing `ecdh(&key, to)` where `to` is the registered `enc_key`; with `to = identity`, `ecdh = identity` (encryption.rs:95-97, 154, 466).
- `cipher()` derives the ChaCha20 key from `transcript(context || ecdh.to_bytes())`; with `ecdh = identity`, `to_bytes()` is the public identity encoding, so the key is `H(context || identity)` — computable by anyone (encryption.rs:101-133).
- The per-message PoP (`pop_challenge`) binds the ciphertext to the *sender's* ephemeral key, not the recipient's `enc_key`, so it does not detect this (encryption.rs:302-324). The PoP verifies fine, the shares pass `share_verification_statements`, and the DKG completes normally.
- The honest-path decrypt (`Encryption::decrypt`, encryption.rs:487-488) uses `self.enc_key`, so the malicious recipient themselves decrypts normally; nothing faults.

An external observer who collects all `EncryptedMessage`s addressed to the malicious participant can compute the same keystream, XOR-decrypt each `SecretShare<C::F>`, and sum them per the PedPoP construction to recover that participant's complete secret share of the threshold group key — without any cooperation from the participant beyond their (public) commitment message.

### Impact Explanation
Recovery of a participant's secret share by an unprivileged eavesdropper. Each leaked share is a full private key share of the threshold signing key; combined with `t-1` other leaked/compromised shares it yields the group secret key and arbitrary FROST signatures (e.g., draining Bitcoin held by the validator set). Even a single share leak degrades the security threshold and is a direct secret-material disclosure — matching the CWE-256/CWE-522 class of the reference advisory (secret stored where unauthorized parties can read it; here, "stored/transmitted" under a publicly computable encryption key).

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only that one DKG participant submits `enc_key = identity` in their `EncryptionKeyMessage` — pure public input to `EncryptionKeyMessage::read`/`register`. The attack is non-interactive for the observer afterwards and undetectable at protocol runtime (PoPs and shares all verify). It does not require collusion: the recipient need not even cooperate with the observer, since a passive listener learns everything.

### Recommendation
Reject non-prime-order-subgroup/identity encryption keys at registration: after `C::read_G` in `EncryptionKeyMessage::read` (or in `Decryption::register`), check `!bool::from(enc_key.is_identity())` (and for curves with cofactor, verify subgroup membership). Additionally or alternatively, require a proof of possession over `enc_key` in the round-1 message so participants demonstrate knowledge of the discrete log.

### Proof of Concept
1. In a `ThresholdParams::new(t, n, i)` PedPoP session (e.g., `t=2, n=3`, Ristretto), participant `j` generates coefficients normally but publishes `EncryptionKeyMessage { msg: commitments, enc_key: <Ristretto as Ciphersuite>::G::identity() }`.
2. Every other participant `l` calls `generate_secret_shares`, producing `EncryptedMessage`s to `j` where `ecdh(key_l, identity) = identity`.
3. Observer recomputes `cipher::<Ristretto>(context, &Zeroizing::new(identity))` — `key = H("DKG Encryption v0.2" || context || "encryption_key" || identity.to_bytes() || "key")`, `iv = b"DKG IV v0.2\0"` — and XORs the keystream against `msg.msg` for each sender `l`, recovering each `SecretShare` scalar `f_l(j)`.
4. Observer sums `Σ_l f_l(j)` = participant `j`'s secret share of the group key, without `j` ever decrypting or cooperating. Verify: `share * G == Σ_k commitments_l[k] * j^k` holds for the recovered value.
