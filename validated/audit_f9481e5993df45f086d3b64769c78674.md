### Title
Front-runnable reward dilution in `DelegateVoteRewardPool` via same-block `stakeFor` before `WombatBribeManager.castVotes()` harvest - (File: rewards/DelegateVoteRewardPool.sol, wombat/WombatBribeManager.sol)

### Summary
`WombatBribeManager.castVotes()` is a fully permissionless function that, after casting votes and forwarding bribe rewards, unconditionally triggers `IDelegateVoteRewardPool(delegatedPool).harvestAll()`, which immediately mints/queues the freshly harvested bribes into `rewardPerTokenStored` based on the **current** `totalSupply` of the delegate pool [1](#0-0) . Because `DelegateVoteRewardPool.stakeFor` performs no time-weighting, vesting, or cooldown before a staker becomes eligible for the very next reward distribution, an attacker can call `vote()` to route vlMGP into the delegate pool immediately before (front-running) a pending `castVotes()` transaction, and thereby capture a full pro-rata share of bribes that were actually earned by long-standing delegate-pool participants over the preceding voting epoch.

### Finding Description
`vote()` on `WombatBribeManager` lets any vlMGP holder increase their vote on any active pool, including the pseudo-pool whose `rewarder` is set to `delegatedPool`. This calls `IBribeRewardPool(pool.rewarder).stakeFor(msg.sender, uint256(delta))` [2](#0-1) , which resolves to `DelegateVoteRewardPool.stakeFor`:
- `updateRewards(_for, rewardTokens)` checkpoints the attacker's `userRewardPerTokenPaid` to the *current* `rewardPerTokenStored` (before any new rewards are queued).
- `totalSupply` and `_balances[_for]` are incremented, and `_updateVote()` redistributes the pool's underlying votes across `votePools` in proportion to `votingWeights` and the new `totalSupply` [3](#0-2) .

Anyone can then call (or the attacker can simply wait for someone else to call) the fully public `castVotes()`. After casting the underlying Wombat votes and forwarding bribe rewards, it calls `delegatedPool.harvestAll()` [4](#0-3) , which pulls the pool's bribes via `claimAllBribes(address(this))` and passes them to `_manageRewards` → `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` [5](#0-4) . That function increases `rewardInfo.rewardPerTokenStored` using the **current** `totalSupply` [6](#0-5) , i.e., the supply that already includes the attacker's just-staked balance.

Since the attacker's `userRewardPerTokenPaid` was checkpointed at the *old* `rewardPerTokenStored` right before the harvest, the very next `earned()`/`getReward()` call gives the attacker a reward proportional to their balance against the entire newly distributed reward — despite having held that stake for effectively zero time relative to the bribe-accrual period. This dilutes the share that should accrue to genuine long-term delegate-pool stakers, who collectively earned those bribes through their sustained vote allocation. There is no minimum holding period, cooldown, streaming/vesting of rewards, or checkpoint against flash-staking in `stakeFor`/`_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer`, and `castVotes()` has no access control preventing this ordering from being triggered atomically after the attacker's `vote()`.

### Impact Explanation
This is a "theft of unclaimed yield" issue (Immunefi impact class: theft of unclaimed yield): an unprivileged attacker can capture bribe rewards that economically belong to long-term delegate-pool voters by staking into `delegatedPool` immediately before a `castVotes()` harvest and withdrawing shortly after, repeatable every time bribes are cast. The magnitude scales with the size of the attacker's stake relative to the pool's `totalSupply` at harvest time and the size of the harvested bribe batch.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker needs: (1) vlMGP voting power (requires having locked MGP, but no waiting period is evident for the vote/unvote mechanics themselves — only the ability to move already-locked vlMGP between pools is needed), (2) the ability to front-run/observe a pending `castVotes()` transaction in the public mempool, which is realistic since `castVotes()` is a public, gas-incentivized function anyone can call. No special privileges, reentrancy, or protocol misconfiguration are required, and the attack is repeatable each casting cycle, making it moderately feasible for a capital-holding MEV-aware actor.

### Recommendation
Introduce time-weighted or checkpoint-based reward accrual (e.g., stream rewards over time via a `rewardRate`/`periodFinish` model instead of instantly adding to `rewardPerTokenStored` at harvest time), or require a minimum staking duration in `DelegateVoteRewardPool` before new stake is eligible to earn from the next harvest. Alternatively, snapshot `totalSupply` and eligible balances prior to accepting new `stakeFor` calls within the same block/harvest window, or decouple vote submission from stake accounting so that newly added stake cannot participate in bribes already earned by prior voting.

### Proof of Concept
Foundry test plan:
1. Deploy `WombatBribeManager`, `DelegateVoteRewardPool` (as `delegatedPool`), and register a pool with `rewarder = delegatedPool`; set up `votePools`/`votingWeights`.
2. Have two long-term stakers, Alice and Bob, each `vote()` a fixed amount into the delegated pool and hold for several epochs while bribes accrue on Wombat's underlying bribe contracts.
3. Simulate a pending `castVotes()` transaction (do not call it yet).
4. As the attacker, call `vote()` to stake a large amount into the delegated pool in the same block, immediately before calling `castVotes()`.
5. Call `castVotes()`, triggering `harvestAll()` → `_queueNewRewardsWithoutTransfer` with the new (diluted) `totalSupply`.
6. Immediately call `getReward()` for the attacker and assert `earned(attacker, rewardToken) > 0` despite zero holding duration, and that the sum of Alice's and Bob's `earned()` amounts is measurably reduced compared to a scenario without the attacker's front-run stake, quantifying the diverted yield.
