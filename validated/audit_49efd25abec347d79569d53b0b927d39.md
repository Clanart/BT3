### Title
Hardcoded 1 sat/vB Fee-Rate Fallback When Both Fee Sources Fail Causes Time-Critical Bridge Transactions to Miss Timelocks During Network Congestion — (`crates/clementine-extended-rpc/src/client.rs`)

---

### Summary

`get_fee_rate_sat_per_kvb()` in `crates/clementine-extended-rpc/src/client.rs` silently falls back to a hardcoded **1 sat/vB (1000 sat/kvB)** when both mempool.space and Bitcoin Core's `estimatesmartfee` fail simultaneously. The tx-sender task loop uses this fee rate to broadcast every pending bridge transaction — including time-critical ones such as `ChallengeTimeout`, `AssertTimeout`, `DisproveTimeout`, and `WatchtowerChallengeTimeout`. During Bitcoin network congestion (when the market fee rate is well above 1 sat/vB), these transactions will be stuck in the mempool. The incremental fee-bump mechanism advances the fee by only ~1 sat/vB every 10 blocks, making it impossible to reach the market rate before the timelocks expire. A malicious operator can exploit this window to escape disproval and drain the bridge vault.

---

### Finding Description

**Root cause — hardcoded fallback value:** [1](#0-0) 

When both fee sources fail, the function returns `Amount::from_sat(1000)` — exactly 1 sat/vB — without any way for the operator to override this value through configuration.

**Fee sources that can fail:**

1. `get_fee_rate_from_mempool_space()` — an external HTTP call to `mempool.space/api/v1/fees/recommended`. It fails on timeout, HTTP 5xx, or missing `fastestFee` field. [2](#0-1) 

2. `estimate_smart_fee(1, None)` — a Bitcoin Core RPC call. It fails when the node has insufficient data (new node, pruned node, syncing), or when the RPC call times out after 30 seconds. [3](#0-2) 

Both can fail simultaneously during a Bitcoin network congestion event that also causes mempool.space to be overloaded or unreachable.

**The tx-sender task loop uses this fee rate for every pending transaction:** [4](#0-3) 

**The incremental fee-bump mechanism is too slow to recover:**

When a transaction is "stuck" for `fee_bump_after_blocks` (default 10) blocks, `calculate_target_fee_rate()` bumps the fee by `get_network_info().incremental_fee` — typically 1 sat/vB. Starting from 1 sat/vB, reaching 500 sat/vB (a realistic congestion peak) would require 499 × 10 = **4,990 blocks (~35 days)**, far beyond any protocol timelock. [5](#0-4) 

**Time-critical timelocks that would be missed:**

| Transaction | Timelock (blocks) | ~Real time |
|---|---|---|
| `ChallengeTimeout` | 144 | ~1 day |
| `WatchtowerChallengeTimeout` | 288 | ~2 days |
| `AssertTimeout` | 576 | ~4 days |
| `DisproveTimeout` | 720 | ~5 days | [6](#0-5) 

---

### Impact Explanation

If the verifier's tx-sender is using the 1 sat/vB fallback during congestion, its `AssertTimeout` or disprove transaction will not confirm before the `disprove_timeout_timelock` (720 blocks) expires. The operator's `DisproveTimeout` transaction then confirms, and the operator is reimbursed from the bridge vault even though they acted maliciously. This constitutes a **loss of bridged BTC from the vault** — a critical/high impact under the allowed impact gate.

Similarly, if the operator's tx-sender is affected, `ChallengeTimeout` transactions miss their 144-block window, causing the operator to lose their challenge timeout and potentially their collateral.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

**Low-to-medium.** The scenario requires:
1. Bitcoin network congestion (fee rate > 1 sat/vB, which is the normal state on mainnet).
2. mempool.space being unreachable (outages occur; the API has rate limits).
3. Bitcoin Core's `estimatesmartfee` returning no fee rate (happens on nodes with insufficient mempool history, during IBD, or after a restart).

All three conditions have occurred historically and can coincide during high-activity periods (e.g., Ordinals/BRC-20 inscription waves in 2023 caused both extreme congestion and mempool.space degradation simultaneously).

---

### Recommendation

1. **Replace the hardcoded 1 sat/vB fallback with a configurable minimum fee rate** (e.g., `tx_sender_fee_rate_fallback_sat_kvb`) that operators can set to a safe floor for their network.
2. **Add a configurable maximum staleness** — if the fee rate has not been successfully fetched for N seconds, halt sending time-critical transactions rather than using a stale/fallback value.
3. **Decouple the hard cap from the fallback** — the hard cap (`fee_rate_hard_cap = 100 sat/vB`) already prevents the system from paying market rates during extreme congestion even when sources are healthy; consider raising the default or making it per-transaction-type.
4. **Integrate a third fee source** (e.g., a local fee estimator or a secondary API) so that both sources must fail before the fallback is used.

---

### Proof of Concept

**Setup:** Mainnet node with insufficient mempool history (so `estimatesmartfee` returns `null` feerate). mempool.space is unreachable (firewall rule or outage). Bitcoin network fee rate is 200 sat/vB.

**Trace:**

1. `TxSenderTaskInternal::run_once()` calls `self.inner.get_fee_rate()`. [7](#0-6) 

2. `get_fee_rate()` calls `get_fee_rate_kvb()` → `get_fee_rate_sat_per_kvb()`. [8](#0-7) 

3. `get_fee_rate_from_mempool_space()` times out after 5 s × 4 retries → `Err(FeeErr::Timeout)`. [9](#0-8) 

4. `estimate_smart_fee(1, None)` returns `{ feerate: null }` → `Err("Failed to extract fee rate")`. [10](#0-9) 

5. Both-fail branch executes: `selected_fee_amount = Amount::from_sat(1000)` → **1 sat/vB**. [11](#0-10) 

6. `try_to_send_unconfirmed_txs(fee_rate=1_sat_vb, ...)` broadcasts the pending `ChallengeTimeout` transaction at 1 sat/vB. At 200 sat/vB market rate, the transaction sits at the bottom of the mempool. [12](#0-11) 

7. After 10 blocks (`fee_bump_after_blocks`), `calculate_target_fee_rate()` bumps to 2 sat/vB. After 144 blocks (the `operator_challenge_timeout_timelock`), the fee is only ~14 sat/vB — still far below the 200 sat/vB market rate. The `ChallengeTimeout` transaction never confirms within the timelock window. [13](#0-12) 

8. The operator's `DisproveTimeout` transaction (sent at market rate from a separate wallet) confirms first. The operator is reimbursed from the bridge vault despite acting maliciously.
