### Title
PedPoG share encryption degenerates to a public key when a participant registers an identity/torsion encryption key — ([File: crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs](crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs))

### Summary
Mirroring CVE-2024-55912's class (weaker-than-expected cryptography enabling decryption of sensitive data), the PedPoP DKG's ECDH-based share encryption accepts an identity group element as a participant's registered encryption key. The ECDH then always yields the identity point, making the ChaCha20 key publicly derivable and every secret share encrypted to that participant decryptable by any observer.

### Finding Description
`EncryptionKeyMessage::read` parses `enc_key` with `C::read_G` (crypto/dkg/pedpop/src/encryption.rs:57-58). `Ciphersuite::read_G` enforces only canonical encoding — it does not reject the identity element (crypto/ciphersuite/src/lib.rs:91-100); the identity-rejecting variant exists only on `frost::Curve::read_G` (crypto/frost/src/curve/mod.rs:125-131), which PedPoP does not use. `Decryption::register` inserts `msg.enc_key` without any validation (encryption.rs:351-361), and no proof of possession is ever required for `enc_key` — the PoP covers only the per-message key.

`encrypt` derives the cipher from `ecdh(key, to)` where `to` is the victim's registered `enc_key` (encryption.rs:154). If `to` is the identity, `public * private = identity` for every message (encryption.rs:95-97), so `cipher` produces a ChaCha20 key computed solely from the public `context` and the publicly-known identity encoding, with the fixed IV `"DKG IV v0.2\0"` (encryption.rs:101-130). Any party who sees the ciphertext bytes can reconstruct the keystream and recover the `SecretShare` plaintext. The same holds for low-order points on the Ed25519 ciphersuite (`EdwardsPoint`, which has a cofactor of 8), reducing the key space to 8 values.

### Impact Explanation
Each `EncryptedMessage<C, SecretShare<C::F>>` carries a threshold secret share — explicitly "sensitive information" whose confidentiality is the entire purpose of this encryption layer (spec/cryptography/Distributed Key Generation.md, "Encryption"). A passive observer of the authenticated channel, or any participant relaying ciphertexts, recovers the targeted participant's share. While one share is below threshold, the attacker can combine this with other attacks (e.g., adversarial dealer selection or multiple compromised DKG sessions) and it unconditionally breaks the protocol's confidentiality guarantee.

### Likelihood Explanation
An unprivileged DKG participant fully controls the `EncryptionKeyMessage` bytes they broadcast; setting `enc_key` to the identity requires no computation and passes all validation. Any observer of the wire traffic can then decrypt with only public data (the DKG `context`). The only prerequisite is participating in a DKG round.

### Recommendation
Reject identity (and, for cofactored curves, non-prime-order) points when reading/registering encryption keys — e.g., use the identity-checking `Curve::read_G` or explicitly check `enc_key.is_identity()` in `EncryptionKeyMessage::read`/`Decryption::register`, and additionally require a proof of possession for `enc_key` in the commitments message to bind it to its owner.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker (Participant `j`) runs `KeyGenMachine::new(params, context)` → `generate_coefficients`, but before broadcasting, overwrites the `enc_key` field of their `EncryptionKeyMessage<Commitments>` with the canonical encoding of `C::G::identity()`. No PoP covers `enc_key`, so no other change is needed.
2. Every dealer `l` calls `SecretShareMachine::generate_secret_shares`, which for `l == j` computes `ecdh(key_lj, identity) = identity` and encrypts `polynomial(coeffs_l, j)` under `cipher(context, identity)` — a keystream derivable by anyone knowing `context`.
3. An observer reads `msg.key`/`msg.msg` from the wire, recomputes `transcript("DKG Encryption v0.2").append(context).domain_separate("encryption_key").append(shared_key = identity.to_bytes()).challenge("key")[..32]` as the ChaCha20 key with IV `"DKG IV v0.2\0"`, applies the keystream, and recovers participant `j`'s secret-share bytes from every dealer.
