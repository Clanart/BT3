The reported bug is specific to Solidity's `msg.sender` semantics in an EVM contract-to-contract call context (a batch function internally invoking `matchOrder`, causing the calling contract's own address to be substituted for the original caller's address). Chia's architecture has no equivalent caller-identity primitive: coin ownership and spend authorization are enforced through puzzle hashes and aggregated BLS signatures over the specific coin/puzzle/solution, not through a mutable "sender" context that a batching wrapper could silently overwrite [1](#0-0) . I reviewed the closest analogs in the codebase — wallet-side batch/aggregation logic in `combine_coins` [2](#0-1) , `spend_clawback_coins` batching [3](#0-2) , the mempool's block-batch builder [4](#0-3) , and offer/trade aggregation in `TradeManager` and `Offer` [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)  — and none of these substitute a "batch operator" identity for the original coin owner in a way that would let an unprivileged caller move someone else's coins or drain collateral. Each coin spend is independently validated against its own puzzle hash and signature regardless of batching, so this bug class does not map onto a reachable, unprivileged Chia analog.
